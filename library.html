Papers on the desk

Local PDFs, copied verbatim — not rewritten. Genre labels matter: the first block is the 1.3 GHz / Faraday-cage hardware literature. Adjacent LENR, anecdote, NASA survey, laser-plasma, and company-newsletter items follow so they are not silently mixed into the lab protocol.

Inclusion is not endorsement. NASA contractor reports are not NASA certification of over-unity. Company newsletters are marketing. AHE/phonon papers are condensed-matter / LENR-adjacent, not bifilar manuals.

Primary sources for the 1.3 GHz lab

Primary · McMaster 1.3 GHz

Nikolova, N.K. & Zimmerman, R.K. (Nov 2007). Detection of the Time-dependent Electromagnetic Potential at 1.3 GHz. CEM-R-46.

Computational Electromagnetics Laboratory, McMaster University. Wireless link designed so field vectors are ideally zero while the 4-vector potential is not; longitudinally polarized magnetic vector potential; detection with biased low-temperature plasma. Authors conclude RF 4-potential is measurable and receiver RF power is converted from local bias, not radiated TX power.

Download PDF

Primary · amateur RF

Zimmerman, R.K. (2011). Transmission and Reception of Longitudinally-Polarized Momentum Waves.

QEX July/August 2011, pp. 31+. Author NP4B / VE3RKZ. Five-page Epson scan. TEM vs vector-potential comparison and a 1296 MHz circular-waveguide transmitter: 7-inch galvanized stovepipe, TM01, λcut ≈ 23.23 cm, 69 cm guide + 69 cm longitudinal probe, 5.8 cm choke collar, Type N + 3 dB pad, U-shaped fluorescent plasma detector.

Primary · L-band range

Zimmerman, R.K., Jr. (2011). Macroscopic Aharonov–Bohm Effect at L-band Microwave Frequencies.

Modern Physics Letters B 25(9), 649–662. TM01 circular waveguide (7-inch, 3 m) 1280–1380 MHz; folded mercury-vapor plasma detector at 200 mA; free-space range claimed 1.5 m out to 25 m+. Argues Lorenz gauge is the physically correct gauge for propagating A waves.

Primary · plasma antenna

Zimmerman, R.K., Jr. (2013). Reception of longitudinal vector potential radiation with a plasma antenna.

Journal of Applied Physics 114, 044907. NASA Goddard / Sigma Space. Time-harmonic vector-potential reception at 1.3 GHz using a plasma antenna; interaction energy e[φ − v·A] with plasma drift velocity collinear with A.

Seawater / SLW-adjacent · cited by Hively · not lab hardware

Tomilin, A.K., Lukin, A.F. & Gulkov, A.N. (2021). An Experiment to Form a Radio Communication Channel in a Marine Environment.

Technical Physics Letters 47(8):577–579. DOI 10.1134/S1063785021060146. Russian serial: Письма в ЖТФ 2021 т.47 №11 с.48–50. Field report of a radio channel through ~470 m of seawater; authors report ~195 dB/m loss for transverse EM at 27.4 MHz and hypothesize longitudinal / electro-scalar waves via spherical antennas — contested against ordinary Maxwell seawater attenuation. Cited on p. 31 of Hively’s close-hold manuscript. Maps to this hub’s Experiment A/B submersible / later-seawater narrative on kit.html. Educational inclusion only; Springer 2021 is not open access; PDF mirrored from the Hively email set.

Toroidal / anapole multipoles · foundational · Greenyer-adjacent cite

Dubovik, V.I. & Cheshkov, A.A. (1967). Form Factors and Multipoles in Electromagnetic Interactions.

Soviet Physics JETP 24(5), May 1967 (Russian JETP 51, 1369–1373, Nov 1966). Defines electric and magnetic multipoles of the first and second kind; the second-kind family is the toroidal / anapole line (Zel’dovich anapole as the dipole). Open JETP English translation — educational multipole background often cited in Greenyer / LENR “toroidal moment” discussions. Not a Hively 1.3 GHz Faraday-cage hardware paper.

Textbook · longitudinal E / skin & internal impedance · SNR cite

Ramo, S., Whinnery, J.R. & van Duzer, T. Fields and Waves in Communication Electronics.

John Wiley & Sons, New York. First edition commonly dated 1965 (often cited as 1967); later eds 1984 (2nd) and 1994 (3rd). Hively’s note points at pp. 332ff for material used when discussing longitudinal electric field / conductor loss in SNR work. Page numbers drift by edition: in the 3rd ed the classic skin-effect / round-wire internal-impedance treatment is earlier (§§3.16, 4.4–4.5); mid-300s pages are typically guided-wave / transmission-line territory — confirm against the edition you hold before wiring formulas into the hub SNR pages.

Access (legal): Internet Archive borrow of the 1965 1st ed — archive.org/details/fieldswavesincom00ramo (restricted; not free redistribute). Purchase / library: Wiley 3rd ed ISBN 978-0-471-58551-0. Open engineering notes that cite the same skin / Zint formulas (e.g. G3YNH Zint.pdf) are useful for SNR prototyping without hosting the book.

Not on this hub: no full PDF. If you own a print/scan of the cited pages, attach them here and we can add a fair-use excerpt card for the SNR calculators.
