0. What goes in the cage, and the one hard rule

  • The Dipole Detector is an instrument, not an experiment. It gets its own sealed cage, separate from the bifilar-coil cage, the Experiment B (433.59 MHz M&W) polarizer array and the oven.
  • Inside the cage:
    • one Dipole Detector variant on its HDPE plate: DD-433 (433.59 MHz, Experiment B), DD-1296 (1296 MHz, Experiment A) or DD-2450 (2.45 GHz oven test)
    • the SMA jack and about 300 mm (12 in) of coax with the sleeve balun
    • TinySA Ultra on its internal battery (about 100×60×30 mm, 4×2.4×1.2 in)
    • the Tekbox E5/H10 near-field probe
  • Dan's standing rule: no cable crosses the cage wall. That means no feed-through, no bulkhead connector and no penetration of any kind. Data leaves in one of two ways:
    • saved on the TinySA (e.g. to its microSD card, if your unit has one; ASSUMPTION, check) and read after opening, or
    • an optical link through a waveguide-below-cutoff (WBC) tube (§6).
  • ASSUMPTION: the dipole sits in the middle of the cage. Clearances below are measured from the cut-long length of 0.47λ, which is the longest rod you would put in.

1. Why the walls must stay back from the dipole

  • RULE OF THUMB (labelled): a conducting wall closer than about λ/2 to 1λ noticeably detunes and loads a dipole. It shifts the resonant frequency and changes the feed resistance.
  • CLASSICAL-TEM background: Balanis, Antenna Theory (linear wire antennas, "ground effects" section), treats a horizontal dipole a height h above a conducting plane with image theory. Its input impedance swings around the free-space value and settles toward it as h grows past roughly λ/2. The specific λ/2–1λ threshold above is the rule of thumb, not a quoted figure. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • CLASSICAL-TEM: a closed box is also a cavity. Its resonances are at f = (c/2)·√((m/a)² + (n/b)² + (p/d)²) (Harrington 1961 §6-9 / Balanis AEE §10.4, as cited in the hub). transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
    • When the cage is many wavelengths across it is "overmoded": the field inside depends on position.
    • So every dipole reading is calibrated in its own cage, at a fixed position. A cage-loaded resonance is acceptable as long as it is measured and recorded.

    • 2450 MHz: 3.85λ at the tips, 2.45λ at the sides.
    • One cage could therefore cover all three variants.

4. Materials (pros and cons)

Opening-size rule (RULE OF THUMB, common EMC guideline): every hole or gap should be much smaller than λ, typically ≤ λ/20:

  • 2450 MHz: 6.1 mm
  • 1296 MHz: 11.6 mm
  • 433.59 MHz: 34.6 mm

All the meshes below have openings of about 1.3 mm or less, so the mesh itself is fine. In practice seams and the door leak first, not the mesh.

  • LBA/Faraday Cyber NC-Berry: 75 dB average from 100 MHz to 18 GHz.

Frame: wood (1×2 or 2×2 in) or PVC pipe. A non-metal frame is fine near the dipole. Put the frame outside the mesh, or at least keep metal hardware at the corners, away from the rod.

Recommendation: 16-mesh copper with soldered seams for the best result. Ni-Cu fabric is the fastest build. Aluminium screen is the cheapest but has the weakest seams.

5. Seams, door and hardware

  • Wall seams:
    • Overlap adjacent panels by 20–50 mm (0.8–2 in).
    • On copper mesh, solder along the whole seam, or run copper foil tape over it and solder the tape edges.
    • Where you can't solder (aluminium, fabric), use conductive-adhesive copper foil tape on both sides of the seam and press it hard.
    • Vetco VUPN2321 copper tape, 2 in × 33 ft, conductive acrylic adhesive, $20.95 (verified).
    • Do not rely on Less EMF aluminium tape for seams: its adhesive is synthetic rubber, so it is not a conductive joint.
  • Door (lid):
    • Make one face a separate lid with a 50 mm (2 in) lip that folds down over the walls.
    • Contact must be continuous, not spot clamps: gaps between contact points act as slots. At 2450 MHz, λ/20 is only 6 mm.
    • Choices:
      • (a) EMI finger stock (beryllium-copper) around the opening, or
      • (b) a conductive fabric-over-foam gasket, or
      • (c) the lip pressed flat along its full length by a wooden batten on each side, clamped at both ends.
    • Part numbers and prices for finger stock and gaskets were not verified.
  • Fabric version: make it a bag with a roll-down closure (fold at least 3 times) clamped flat, like a dry bag. That is the simplest door.
  • Other hardware:
    • plastic spring clamps
    • 60–100 W soldering iron and rosin-core solder (for the mesh)
    • tin snips
    • staple gun, or nylon zip ties to hold mesh to the frame (keep staples at the frame corners, away from the dipole)
    • an HDPE shelf or Lazy Susan for the dipole plate

6. Grounding

  • CLASSICAL-TEM: a closed conducting shell with no penetrations shields the inside whether or not it is connected to earth. An earth wire adds nothing to the shielding here. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Earthing is optional, for static or safety only.
  • An earth wire must not enter the cage. If you add one, bond it to the outside skin only.

7. IR-thermometer hole (waveguide-below-cutoff tube)

  • Source flag: the hub repo (/workspace/hub-efield-repo) has no WBC-tube guidance. The only related hub item is the TM01 stovepipe, λ_cut ≈ 23.23 cm.
  • The formula below is the one already used in sim-lab (/workspace/sim-lab/expt-d/rxchain/REPORT.md §2), which cites H. W. Ott, Electromagnetic Compatibility Engineering, Wiley 2009:
    • FACT (Ott): a circular tube of inner diameter d has TE11 cutoff f_c = 1.841·c/(π·d), about 176/d GHz with d in mm.
    • Well below cutoff it attenuates about 32 dB per tube diameter of length.
Tube Inner diameter Length f_c f/f_c at 2450 Attenuation (32 dB × L/d)
3/4 in Type L copper water tube (OD 0.875 in, ID 0.785 in, copper.org Table 14.2b) 19.94 mm 80 mm (3.15 in) 8.81 GHz 0.28 ≈128 dB
same tube 19.94 mm 60 mm (2.36 in) 8.81 GHz 0.28 ≈96 dB
1/2 in Type L copper (ID 0.545 in) 13.84 mm 56 mm (2.2 in) 12.7 GHz 0.19 ≈129 dB
  • Recommendation: a 3/4 in Type L copper tube 80 mm long, soldered all the way round (360°) into a copper plate (about 100×100 mm), which is soldered or taped into the wall. Add a 3/4 in copper end cap to close it.
    • At 1296 MHz f/f_c = 0.15, and 433 MHz is further still below cutoff; the same length applies.
  • IR note:
    • Infrared passes straight through an open tube. It does not pass reliably through mesh or fabric, so don't aim the thermometer through the wall.
    • The tube narrows the gun's view. Use a high distance-to-spot gun, or put the gun right against the tube mouth.
    • Or use a battery temperature logger inside and read it after opening (no cable crosses the wall).
  • HYP: "SLW passes the cage wall" is the SLW hypothesis, not established. The hole is also a test: take readings with the tube open and with it capped.
    • CLASSICAL-TEM: at about 128 dB the tube adds essentially no ordinary-RF leakage, so a classical expectation is no difference between open and capped. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
    • A difference would need explaining: first a leak or seam check, then an anomaly log.

Leak check (CLASSICAL-TEM, simple): transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

  1. Put the battery TinySA inside with the dipole.
  2. Record ambient Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz) and cell signals with the lid open.
  3. Close the lid, record again, then open and compare.
  4. The drop is a rough shielding figure. It is limited by the TinySA noise floor.

10. Cautions

  • TinySA self-signal: in ULTRA mode (above 800 MHz) the TinySA leaks its local oscillator at up to −10 dBm out of the RF input (FACT, TinySA docs, noted earlier). In a sealed, reflective cage the dipole re-radiates this. Check with a 50 Ω load in place of the dipole, and enable spur suppression.
  • Keep metal bodies back: the TinySA and the E5/H10 probe are metal objects too. Use the 300 mm coax to keep the TinySA in a corner, behind the dipole along the coax direction, not beside the rod.
  • Safety: the cage is for receive only. Detectors, rods and the TinySA never go inside a running microwave oven. The hub's lab note ("Do not cook a sealed cage") applies if anything is ever transmitted inside.
