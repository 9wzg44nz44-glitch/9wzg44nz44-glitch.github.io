SAFETY (read first)

Rods, probes, TinySAs and cables stay OUTSIDE the oven cavity. Never put loose metal in a running microwave oven: it can arc and damage the magnetron (and the oven). The "near-door" scenario means rods on a non-metallic stand outside the closed door. Do not defeat interlocks or run the oven empty/modified. Oven leakage limits are a federal product standard (21 CFR 1030.10: 5 mW/cm^2 at 5 cm, FACT); do not try to increase leakage.

0. Framing

  • Classical skin-depth / R_s (TEM) heating is not used as the governing model (Dan's correction). One confound line appears in Sec. 3.4 only.
  • openEMS: N/A for the SLW mechanism (it solves Maxwell/TEM). Not run. Logged in SYNC_LEDGER. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Model toggle: Erratum (Phys. Essays 35, 320, 2022) "SLW is not subject to resistive loss in conductive media" -> S0: P = 0 (FACT toggle).

1. Sources: charge accumulation, and whether sigma*E_par^2 is a stated heating term

US 9,306,527 B1 (Hively), FACT quotes:

  • Col. 1 ll. 19-31: a classical linear monopole "driven by a sinusoidal current to put charge onto and remove charge from the linear conductor (antenna)" (charge-accumulator picture, transmitter).
  • Col. 8 ll. ~44-55: "essentially all of the electrical current goes into charging and discharging the antenna."
  • Col. 7 ll. ~21-23: reciprocity, transmitter geometry can act as receiver.
  • Col. 5 l. ~53 and col. 6 l. ~14: "J=sigma E"; EQN. 14 (col. 6 l. ~36) contains the term J.E in the energy balance; col. 6 ll. ~39-44 says with B=0 it "may yield an identity, 0=0 ... verifying the no-skin-effect prediction". Table 1 item 6: "C, E, and J exchange energy in conductive media."
  • Result: the patent never writes sigma*E^2 and never calls J.E heating/dissipation. No rod-absorption formula exists in the patent.

Hively & Loebl, Phys. Essays 32, 112 (2019), FACT: Eq. (44), p. 117: d/dt(B^2/2mu + C^2/2mu + epsE^2) + div(ExB/mu + CE/mu) + J.E = rhoC/(eps mu). p. 116: "E^L = J^L/sigma ... no eddy currents occur". The paper does not call J.E a loss or give an attenuation constant.

Keyport US 12,525,711 B1, FACT: silent on receiving rods, absorption, sigma*E^2, hysteresis, rod material (text search of Google full text: no "heat", "dissipat", "absorb", "hysteres", "ohmic", "joule", "resistiv", "attenuat"; only "conductive material" for antenna parts, col. 4 ll. 5-7).

Monstein & Wesley, EPL 59, 514 (2002), FACT: p. 516: "only the E field component E0 cos(phi) in the direction of a wire produces Ohmic losses"; Eq. (7) dP = -[sigma A E0^2 cos^2 phi] dL. This is the only source-derived rod Joule formula (it assumes internal E = external E0 cos phi). Eq. (9) T = exp[-kappa cos^2 phi], kappa = 4.4 fitted.

Verdict on sigma*E_par^2 as a heating term: from HL2019 Eq. 44 (J.E) + J = sigma E (paper and patent) and M&W Eq. 7 (paper). Not from the patent. Applying it to Dan's 1/8 in rods with internal E_par = external E_par is HYP (Dan's extrapolation). Geometry (L vs lambda/2) setting absorbed energy: no source formula; M&W kappa ~ L (FACT, Eq. 9 context) is the only length dependence stated.

Conductivities (FACT):

(The ~1e7 placeholder is not used.)

Same E_par (Model S1, HYP internal = external): heating ratio brass/W1 = sigma ratio = 3.02 (range 2.71-3.32 annealed; 4.5 vs hardened W1). Brass wins, about 3x more Joule heating per unit volume at the same E_par. This contradicts the "high-hysteresis rods" advice under the sigma*E^2 picture. Reason: hysteresis loss needs a cycling H/B; SLW has B = 0 (Erratum 2022; patent col. 2 ll. 54-61 "no magnetic component", FACT), so mu_r and coercivity drop out of sigma*E^2 (HYP inference on FACT premises).

Examples (tables.md): E_par = 0.1 V/m inside a 433.59 MHz rod -> brass 0.10 W, 3.0 K; W1 0.034 W, 1.1 K (painted). But see Sec. 3: those fields are not reachable from the scenario fluxes without violating energy conservation.

Model S2, charge relaxation (HYP, first-principles continuity + Gauss): if the external E_par terminates on surface charge, E_in ~ (omega eps0/sigma) E_out, so p = (omega eps0 E_out)^2/sigma: ranking reverses, W1 > brass by 3x (via sigma, not hysteresis). Magnitude is ~1e-8 of S1-capped: negligible (<1e-8 K in every scenario).

Model S1-capped (HYP, energy-conserving, M&W <P/A> = <S>): P = S * pi a^2 * (1 - exp(-kappa cos^2 phi)). Both metals absorb essentially everything through the cross-section -> material-independent (ratio 1.00).

So: brass/W1 = 3.0 (S1 literal), 1.0 (S1 capped), 0.33 (S2). No model makes hysteresis matter.

2.1 Question for Dan: Dr. "Heidley/Hadley" (likely Dr. Hively, unconfirmed). We did NOT contact anyone.

He specified HIGH-HYSTERESIS rods for the M&W 433.59 MHz replication and a full 360 deg Lazy Susan rotation. Possible justifications, all HYP:

  • (i) Transverse-EM thinking: under ordinary TEM, magnetic loss in a ferromagnetic rod matters (TEM has B). Would test for TEM leakage, not SLW. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • (ii) A transverse mode excited at the rod ends by the longitudinal field (end discontinuity), whose B could then cause hysteresis loss. No source equation found for this.
  • (iii) Charge-relaxation difference. Quantitative, with assumption eps = eps0 for metals (stated):

(Caveat: in real metals the response is limited by electron collision time ~1e-14 s, still << period, standard Drude picture; HYP-level remark.) This ties to the patent's own transient E = E0 exp(-...t) with exponent printed eps/sigma (col. 5 l. ~55, a typo for sigma/eps).

A full 360 deg sweep gives two repeats: a free consistency check; any 360 deg-periodic component points to asymmetry, a fixture/cable artifact, or a source off-axis (HYP on his intent). Also FACT: Hively himself rotated the receiving antenna 360 deg in 1 deg increments (HL2019 p. 121, Fig. 5), so a full 360 sweep matches his own practice.

Flux-to-field map (HYP algebra on FACT equations): energy flux CE/mu with |C/E| = 1/c (patent EQN. 13, eps' = 1) -> S = E^2/Z0, E_rms = sqrt(Z0 S). Spherical spreading S = P/(4 pi r^2) is FACT within Hively's framework (HL2019 p. 117: 4 pi r^2 (C^2/2mu) constant through a spherical boundary; patent col. 5 ll. ~62-65). 3.4 One confound line (not the governing model)

For ordinary TEM leakage at the door (not SLW), a lambda/2 2.45 GHz rod has a classical maximum available power of S * 1.64 lambda^2/(4 pi) = 50 W/m^2 x 1.95e-3 m^2 ~ 98 mW into a matched load (standard antenna effective aperture, FACT); an unloaded metal rod dissipates only a small fraction of that. This does not govern SLW, but it means any near-door rod heating is confounded by TEM leakage. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

4. Measurement protocol

Emissivity (FACT, Optris table sourced to Bramson / Wolfe & Zissis):

  • Brass, dull/tarnished: 0.22; oxidized: 0.61; polished: 0.03.
  • Iron & steel, freshly worked with emery (dull bare bar): 0.24; rolled sheet 0.56; oxidized 0.74; rusty red 0.69.
  • Black dull lacquer: 0.96-0.98 (Optris); black electrical tape 0.97 (ThermoWorks table); black plastic paint 0.95.
  • Recommendation: put a flat black paint or black electrical-tape dot (eps ~0.96) on each rod at the same spot and set the IR instrument to 0.96. This removes the brass-vs-steel emissivity difference (0.22 vs 0.24 bare is too low and uncertain for IR). The paint/tape adds a thin lossy dielectric and a little mass; use identical dots on every rod, including the reference. (Aluminized-back Kapton is NOT high-eps: 0.24-0.86 depending on thickness, design1st table.)

Irradiation time (computed): tau_th = m cp / (h A_s): painted 207-259 s (3.5-4.3 min); bare 313-405 s (5.2-6.8 min). Expose >= 5 tau_th: ~20 min painted, ~35 min bare before reading steady state, then an equal off period.

Drift control: HVAC off, no people moving nearby, rods and HDPE plate equilibrated >= 1 h, room thermometer logged, identical dummy HDPE plate, repeated on/off cycles (oven on/off is the modulation; keep oven duty and water load identical each cycle).

Differential measurement: worth doing (yes). Exposed rod vs an identical reference rod (same material, dot, mount) rotated to phi = 90 deg (M&W T = 1, predicted zero absorption) or placed far out of the beam. Caution: a metal "shield" is not a valid reference in Hively's framework because the SLW is claimed to pass Faraday enclosures (patent col. 2 ll. 65-67; col. 10 ll. 35-38); use rotation/distance. The difference cancels common room drift. Expected noise floor (typical-instrument values, not verified against Dan's instruments, HYP-level):

  • IR spot thermometer: ~0.1 C resolution. Sees nothing predicted here.
  • Thermal camera, both rods in one frame: NETD typically 30-70 mK per frame; averaging many frames and pixels along the rod: ~5-10 mK.
  • Differential type-T thermocouple (junction on each rod): Seebeck ~40 uV/K (standard type-T tables); a 0.1 uV meter gives 2.5 mK resolution. Realistic air-current noise several mK, reduced by sqrt(N) over N on/off cycles -> ~1-5 mK floor after ~10 cycles (HYP). Caution: TC leads are metal conductors in the field; route them perpendicular to the propagation axis (phi = 90) and keep them identical on both rods; prefer fiber-optic (GaAs) probes, which have no metal leads, if available.
  • Against predictions: only the near-door S1-capped case (~55-85 mK) is above this floor; everything else is below it.

5. NEW: Dan's Faraday-cage bifilar coil + E5 observation

Observation (Dan's data, UNVERIFIED by us): inside a sealed Faraday cage with the oven on, the Hively bifilar coil + TinySA shows 2.45 GHz activity; an E5 E-probe on its own TinySA near the coil showed oven-correlated activity; with the E5 moved as far from the coil as possible it went quiet while the coil's TinySA kept showing activity. Dan's HYP: the coil, driven by the longitudinal field, carries oscillating charge/current and makes a local near field that the E5 picks up.

5.1 What the sources say (FACT quotes) about SLW producing transverse fields at conductors/receivers

  • No source statement found that an SLW converts to, or produces, transverse/TEM fields at a receiving conductor. Checked US 9,306,527, HL2019, Keyport US 12,525,711. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • US 9,306,527 col. 10 ll. 1-7 (bifilar coil): "There may be zero or approximately zero inductance associated with the coil as a result of magnetic-field cancellation by counter-going electrical currents in adjacent turns of the coil. There may be zero or approximately zero capacitance associated with the coil as a result of adjacent turns of the coil having the same or approximately the same electrical charge density."
  • US 9,306,527 col. 10 ll. 35-38: a solid copper Faraday box enclosing the coil "may be configured to block most or all transverse electromagnetic waves impinging on the Faraday box." Col. 3 ll. 1-4: the result "may not be explainable by radiation of classical transverse waves because such waves are eliminated via Faraday cage(s)".
  • US 9,306,527 col. 7 ll. ~21-23 and HL2019 p. 116: "a SLW transmitter can act as a receiver, and vice versa" (reciprocity).
  • HL2019 p. 116: "The SLW has not been previously observed, because transverse electromagnetic (TEM) antennas detect only waves that produce a circulating current. A TEM transmitter produces only circulating currents, yielding C = 0 and E_L = 0 with no SLW power output or reception"; Appendix C, pp. 123-124: "This result explains the nondetection of the SLW by TEM antennas, which can detect waves that generate only a circulating current (no gradient-driven current)." transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • HL2019 p. 121 (Hively's own confounds): "One issue may be TEM signals being picked up by the coaxial cables, either inside or outside the Faraday cage... we did not use a TEM receiving antenna inside the Faraday cage to confirm the absence of such a signal." Also: "a longitudinal E-field component occurs in the near-field under classical (Maxwell) electrodynamics" within a few wavelengths (symbol garbled in extraction; ~3 lambda). transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Keyport US 12,525,711 col. 7 l. ~62 to col. 8 l. 5: "Symmetric coil windings, such as a bifilar coil, may have SLW transmission advantages through cancelation of the E-Field [sic]. This may work similarly to a twisted pair ... and may cause the magnetic field of each wire to cancel with its opposing neighboring fields." Col. 7 ll. ~40-45: spark-fed antenna "capable of generating SLW and scalar waves, while reducing the transverse electromagnetic (TEM) wave 4." Col. 7 ll. ~46-52: one-wire capacitive coupling, "By sloshing electrons between two capacitive bodies, the sloshed current may be rectified on the receiving side." (Line numbers read from the rendered image, approximate.) transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

Consistency assessment:

  • FACT (classical Maxwell): any time-varying charge/current distribution produces a reactive near field that falls off steeply. Reactive zone ~ lambda/(2 pi) (Wheeler radiansphere; kr = 1 in Jackson 3e Sec. 9.1): 110 mm @ 433.59 MHz, 36.8 mm @ 1296 MHz, 19.5 mm @ 2.45 GHz (computed). For a small neutral source the quasi-static E falls as 1/r^3 (electric-dipole near zone, Jackson 3e Sec. 9.2), vs 1/r far field. So IF the coil carries 2.45 GHz current, a local E near it is expected, and its steep fall-off fits "quiet when far."
  • HYP: that the coil current is driven by an inbound SLW. Hively's framework says TEM-type receivers (a small E-probe is one) do not detect SLW directly (p. 116), so an E5 reading near the coil is, in that framework, most naturally a classical local field from the coil's current: consistent with Dan's HYP. But no source claims the coil re-emits classical fields, and Hively's own col. 10 says the bifilar coil cancels B and has ~zero capacitance, so by design its local fields should be small (HYP tension worth noting). transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Important instrument FACT: tinySA Ultra covers 100 kHz-800 MHz in normal mode and up to 6 GHz only with ULTRA mode enabled (tinysa.org TinySA4 specification). It has a "spur suppression option for assessing if certain signals are internally generated" (same page). Use it.

5.2 Alternatives Dan should rule out (all HYP)

  • (a) The coil's own TinySA/coax/USB/power path is the antenna, or a cage leak (seam, vent, cable) couples in and the coil (a conductor that concentrates whatever gets in) re-radiates it. Hively flagged exactly this cable pickup (HL2019 p. 121).
  • (b) The two TinySAs couple to each other, or through a shared ground/battery/USB, when the E5 is near the coil (the instruments are closest then).
  • (c) TinySA self-generated spurs/harmonics (LO, ULTRA-mode mixing products, firmware) near 2.45 GHz, possibly modulated by the oven's mains-synchronous magnetron switching via power lines or vibration.
  • Cage rule check: no cable crosses the wall; battery power; data out only optically or after the run (stored traces). Were both TinySAs fully inside on battery, with USB unplugged? If not, that is a leak path and the result is not a Faraday test.

5.3 Discriminating controls (IDEA)

  1. Oven off vs on (same setup) and oven on with an empty-cage baseline (TinySA with 50 ohm terminator, no coil).
  2. E5 near a dummy: a non-resonant coil or a straight wire of the same wire length, in place of the Hively coil.
  3. Coil disconnected from its TinySA and terminated in 50 ohm, E5 kept near it. If E5 still sees the oven, the coil is not being driven via its TinySA path.
  4. Swap the TinySAs; move both instruments; enable spur suppression; repeat.
  5. Seal each TinySA in its own small shielded box (probe coax through a bulkhead), both inside the big cage.
  6. Distance sweep: E5 amplitude vs distance from the coil at fixed E5 orientation, log-log. Expected classical drops (computed): 5->50 mm: 20 dB for 1/r, 40 dB for 1/r^2, 60 dB for 1/r^3; 5->20 mm: 12 / 24 / 36 dB. A steep (~1/r^3 beyond the coil radius) fall-off centered on the coil supports a local source; a flat or 1/r curve, or one centered on a cable/instrument, points to (a)-(c). Keep within the TinySA's ~70 dB spur-free range (spec page).

5.4 Implication for the rods (HYP)

If rods absorb by charge accumulation (sigma*E_par^2, HYP), they carry oscillating charge (max near the ends for a half-wave rod) and current (max at the center; classical half-wave distribution, FACT), so they should also show a local near field. IDEA: use a near-field probe sweep near a rod as an absorption proxy, with rotation phi as the key discriminator: a real rod-coupled signal should follow the M&W cos^2 phi dependence, 180 deg periodic, max at phi = 0/180 and minimum at 90/270, over a full 360 deg. Scale illustration (HYP arithmetic, nearfield.json): if the near-door S1-capped 0.39 mW were Joule loss with DC-uniform current (no skin effect per Hively), a 2.45 GHz rod would carry ~0.87 A rms (brass) / ~0.50 A (W1), i.e. classical H ~21 / 12 A/m at 5 mm: very easy to see with an H-probe. In Hively's framework the gradient-driven current has B = 0, so whether an H-probe sees an azimuthal field at the rod center is itself a discriminator between a classical current and Hively's irrotational current (HYP). Note this proxy is material-dependent (I ~ sqrt(sigma)) even when heating is not.

6. Instrumentation per frequency (rods floating, unperturbed; non-contact primary)

  • Series ammeter / current probe: an ordinary ammeter cannot read RF current, and cutting the rod to insert one detunes it (FACT classical). Real example, verified: Tektronix CT6 (Tek datasheet): 250 kHz-2 GHz typical (1.5 GHz guaranteed), closed core that accepts bare wire up to 20 gauge (0.8 mm) only: it cannot fit a 1/8 in (3.175 mm) rod and does not reach 2.45 GHz. So clamp probes are a poor fit; treat as a "class of device." A thermocouple-type RF ammeter inside the rod is impractical.
  • Non-contact current equivalent: a small shielded loop (H-probe, like H10) a few mm from the rod center (current maximum for a half-wave rod, FACT classical).
  • Voltage: half-wave rod voltage maximum is at the ends (FACT classical). Any contact probe loads and detunes the rod; prefer a capacitive E-probe (like E5) a few mm from a rod end.
  • TinySA + probes: H-probe near the center, E-probe near an end, spaced a few mm, either (1) fixed to the HDPE plate so they rotate with the rod (measures the rod's own response vs phi), or (2) fixed in space while the rod rotates (maps the field seen at one point; includes the probe-rod coupling). Both give different information; do both. The probe and its coax perturb the field and can themselves be the pickup (same caveat as the coil): repeat with the rod removed. Inside the cage: battery TinySA, no cable through the wall. 1296 and 2450 MHz need tinySA ULTRA mode (FACT, spec).
  • Oscilloscope: Tek "5 Times Rule" (FACT, Tektronix XYZs of Oscilloscopes primer): bandwidth >= 5x signal for <2% amplitude error -> 2.2 GHz (433.59 MHz), 6.5 GHz (1296 MHz), 12.3 GHz (2.45 GHz). Prices not verified; say "multi-GHz real-time scope class, expensive." Not needed: the TinySA zero-span mode (min burst 50 us at 850 kHz RBW, spec page) or an SDR gives the envelope, which is enough for on/off and vs-phi work. SDR coverage (vendor specs, not re-fetched this session): HackRF One ~1 MHz-6 GHz; RTL-SDR (R820T2) up to ~1.7 GHz, so 433.59 and 1296 MHz only.
  • Real-time brass vs W1: paired rods (one of each) at identical positions, differential IR camera or fiber-optic probes on painted dots, plus near-field probe amplitude vs phi for each material. Expect equal heating if S1-capped, brass 3x if S1 literal, W1 3x if S2; none predicts a hysteresis effect.
