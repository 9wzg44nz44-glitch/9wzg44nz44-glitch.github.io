    • In Hively's framework these have no eddy-current or skin-effect loss (HYP; Hively & Loebl 2019 p. 117; Erratum 2022; US 9,306,527 cols. 5–7).
    • So TEM figures are irrelevant here and are no longer used to rank or dismiss anything.
  2. The only SLW loss left is Hively's Joule term J·E (Hively & Loebl 2019 Eq. 44), or no loss at all (Erratum, α = 0).

1. Propagation model (SLW/SW only)

1.1 What the sources actually say (quotes)

  • Hively & Loebl, Phys. Essays 32, 112 (2019), Eq. 44 (p. 117, as printed; HYP). The revised energy balance: ∂/∂t(B²/2μ + C²/2μ + εE²) + ∇·(E×B/μ + CE/μ) + J·E = ρC/(εμ).
    • Their gloss: "Equation (44) has new energy density terms: scalar field energy (C²/2μ), SLW energy (CE/μ), and a power source (ρC/εμ)."
    • Note (ASSUMPTION): the printed E-energy term lacks the /2 that the text on p. 116 uses ("4πr²(εE²/2)"). This is probably typographic. Update 2026-09-29 (FACT): the erratum (Phys. Essays 32(3) 417) prints Eq. (44) with εE²/2, confirming the /2.
  • Eq. 37 (p. 116; HYP): Z = |E_r|/|C/μ₀μ′| = √(μ₀/ε₀)·√(μ′/ε′)·[1 − 1/(jkr)]/[1 − j tan(δ_ε)].
    • Checked against the rendered PDF: the loss factor 1 − j tanδ sits outside the square root.
    • The text says Eq. 37 uses "|E|/(C/μ) to obtain the SLW impedance, like the CED form, Z = |E|/|H|".
    • Hively's σ: "σ = ε₀ε″ω" (text after Eq. 36).
  • Same page, straight after Eq. 37 (HYP): "The C− and E− field energies from Eq. (44), 4πr²(εE²/2) and 4πr²(C²/2μ), are constant through a spherical boundary." Also p. 115: "a constant energy, 4πr²(C²/2μ), through a spherical boundary around a source in arbitrary media, as required."
    • Constant energy through every sphere means no loss beyond spreading. The paper's own text therefore describes no per-metre loss.
  • p. 117 (HYP): "Thus, no eddy currents occur, so the SLW is unimpeded by the skin effect for propagation through linearly conductive media."
  • p. 120 (HYP): "SLW attenuation in conductive media is the subject of future work." The 2019 paper explicitly does not give an SLW attenuation constant.
  • p. 120–121, Fig. 4 (HYP; single-lab, not replicated). 2 × 1.02 mm copper ("2914 skin depths") around the antennas at ~8 GHz. Extrapolating to 2 mm separation "gives −79 dB and −86 dB". "These results show that the SLW is unconstrained by the skin effect with 1/r² attenuation in free-space."
  • Erratum, Phys. Essays 35, 320 (2022) (HYP): "Physically, Eq. (A6) means that the SLW is not subject to resistive loss in conductive media. The same no-loss result occurs from the energy balance equation, Eq. (44), by substituting for (ρ/ε) from Eq. (22) and by substituting for J^L from Eq. (21) with B = 0; then cancelling terms. Consequently, the SLW is unconstrained by the skin effect and power loss in resistive media."
  • Hively & Land, IARD 2020 slides 59–60 (/workspace/ohmic-slw-search/Hively-Land-IARD-2020.txt; HYP, conference slides).
    • Slide titles: "SLW Attenuation in Conductive Media" and "No SLW Attenuation in Conductive Media".
    • σ appears only as a temporal relaxation, "E = Eo e^(−σt/ε)".
    • For harmonic fields: "lossless propagation in linear conductive media".
    • FACT (arithmetic): ε₀ε′/σ = 1.5×10⁻¹⁰ s in seawater. Hively does not turn this into a spatial loss.
  • US 9,306,527 B1 (HYP): "the electric wave's energy, 4πr²(εE²/2), may be constant through a spherical boundary of arbitrary radius"; Table 1: "SLW propagates in conductive media without the skin effect"; "C, E, and J exchange energy in conductive media. EQNS. 11-17".
  • Hively & Reed, Symmetry 12, 2110 (2020) (HYP): SLW and SW "are unconstrained by the skin effect, because neither wave has a magnetic field that generates" eddy currents (p. 1, abstract; p. 9 text; Table item 7, p. 10).
  • Hub, experiment-c.html #ohmic (hub rule, not a Hively quote): "SLW still has linear Ohmic loss ... p_Joule = J·E = σE∥²" and "exp(−α_ohm L) ... with α tied to σ / loss-tangent story". The hub gives no formula for α_ohm.
    • Open item (RESOLVED 2026-09-30): the hub quoted the patent transient as "E ∝ exp(−ε t/σ)" TYPO as printed in the patent. Hively's IARD slide has e^(−σt/ε). The inverted form was dimensionally inconsistent; hub experiment-c.html was fixed in PR #58, and the author confirmed e^(−σt/ε) on 2026-09-30 (note at the top of this page).

1.2 Audit of α_J = σ_H|Z|/2 (the Joule-as-previously-assumed conversion)

(a) What Eq. 44 literally gives. Eq. 44 is a local energy balance with four kinds of term:

  • storage (B², C², E²);
  • flux (E×B/μ, plus the SLW flux CE/μ);
  • Joule work J·E;
  • a source ρC/(εμ).

It contains no attenuation constant and no length scale. Hively's own text says field energy is constant through any sphere (p. 115–116). It says attenuation in conductive media is "future work" (p. 120). The Erratum says the J·E term cancels once the other terms are substituted. Verdict: Eq. 44 alone cannot give a per-metre attenuation without added steps that are not in the source. HYP (source text) + ASSUMPTION (our reading).

(b) The added steps our α_J needed (all ASSUMPTION unless marked):

  1. The flux. Treat CE/μ as the only energy flux of the SLW. B = 0 removes E×B/μ (HYP, Eq. 27).
  2. Impedance, magnitude only. Replace C/μ by E/|Z| using Eq. 37, keeping only the magnitude of Z. This fixes both the E-to-flux relation and the phase convention (see d).
  3. E∥ definition. Take J·E = σE∥² with E∥ = E_L, the radial field of the Eq. 37 spherical wave, and J_L = σE_L. The E_L = J_L/σ relation is Hively's (p. 117, HYP). Using σ = σ_H = ε₀ε″ω, which includes dipolar loss, is Hively's definition (HYP).
  4. Drop the source term. Discard ρC/(εμ) and the time-averaged storage terms. This is the decisive invented step: the Erratum says ρC/(εμ) cancels J·E exactly.
  5. Far field and local plane wave. Set kr → ∞ in Eq. 37 and treat the loss as a local exp(−2αr) on the flux. Spreading is handled separately.
  6. Then flux loss per metre = σ_H|Z| (in power), so α_J = σ_H|Z|/2 (in field). This is the existing Expt G code line (water()), and it is our construction, not Hively's.

(c) Does |Z| smuggle in a TEM/transverse impedance? transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

  • Not the impedance itself. Eq. 37 is Hively's own SLW impedance (HYP). It differs from the TEM intrinsic impedance because 1 − j tanδ is outside the root. Overall audit verdict.

  • α_J = σ_H|Z|/2 is our ASSUMPTION, not something Eq. 44 gives. It needs a magnitude-only impedance substitution, a choice of E∥ and σ, and deletion of the ρC/(εμ) source term. That source term is the one Hively's Erratum uses to cancel J·E.
  • |Z| is Hively's (not TEM), but the attenuation recipe is the classical one. So at 2.45 GHz it reproduces TEM-scale loss: 953 dB/m against the TEM reference 1063 dB/m. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • The more literal Hively-grounded reading (b) is no per-metre loss. It rests on the 2019 text itself, the patent, the 2020 slides and the Erratum.

2. Re-ranked candidate sources (enemy submarine, detected from another submerged platform)

How to read the SNR labels.

  • No source has a known SLW source-term magnitude. HYP: Hively's source coupling has never been measured, and the fraction of a device's electrical power that becomes SLW is unknown.
  • So each label is qualitative and relative. It combines four things:
    1. propagation under the reading;
    2. how literally the device matches Hively's charging-current criterion (§4);
    3. duty cycle;
    4. how distinct its fingerprint is against the SLW background that the same hypothesis implies (every grid, drive and oven on Earth; lightning per Hively & Land 2021 p. 2).
  • Powers quoted are electrical powers from open sources, not SLW powers.
  • The column "(c) Erratum" also stands for reading (b).

GHz content gets the same (a1) seawater penalty as the magnetron. Tactical context: only when shallow. Moderate–weak. No loss, but only near the surface. Antenna types mostly TEM-closed.
6 8 Electrolytic oxygen generator DC, rectifier ripple (ASSUMPTION). Electrode–membrane interface: electronic → ionic current, which does not stop. Closed DC circuit. Sea Wolf prototype OGP: 83-cell stack, "maximum current rating of 250 A ... 249 V" (NASA GRC report on the Hamilton Standard OGP). Weak. Weak.
7 9 Degaussing coils DC to slow; supply ripple. Closed solenoidal loops: C = 0 (HYP; Hively & Loebl App. C). Weak. Weak.

Not ranked:

  • VLF/ELF comms antennas (floating wire, towed buoy): receive-only.
  • Electro-chlorination: not verified aboard submarines.
  • Crew Wi-Fi/Bluetooth: would share the magnetron's propagation but has ~mW-class power (ASSUMPTION).

Cross-cutting counterpoint.

  • HYP-internal: Hively's own patent EQN. 8→9 gives □²C = 0 under charge conservation.
  • Rentzber (arXiv:2608.19405, 2026; HYP preprint) argues "For a conserved source with no scalar initial field, C=0". If that holds, every row above is zero, because all of these devices conserve charge.
  • FACT (mainstream): longitudinal E is near-field only (Bray & Britton, EPL 66, 153, 2004).

3. Per-source notes

3.1 Magnetron (Dan's top interest).

  • FACT (datasheet, LG 2M246, local home-motors/src/2M246.pdf):
    • 1000 W average output in a typical oven, 1150 W into a matched load;
    • 4.40 kV peak anode; 330 mA average anode current; 1500 mA peak maximum;
    • supply: "Half-wave doubler with leakage" transformer.
  • FACT (Sharp service manuals): power levels by relay cycling within a "32 second time base", with ~2 s filament warm-up. The commercial Sharp R-2397 has two magnetrons, "oscillated alternatively according to the frequency of the power supply".
  • FACT: a galley microwave oven is present on at least one US SSN (QSR Magazine, USS Jefferson City; batch 1). Shipboard units are 120 V 60 Hz (IMPA 175090; batch 1).
  • Fingerprint to search for (ASSUMPTION, derived from the FACT items):
    • 2.45 GHz energy present only during one mains half-cycle (16.7 ms repeat at 60 Hz; 20 ms at 50 Hz for a 50 Hz navy);
    • or 8.3 ms alternation for dual-magnetron ovens;
    • a ~50 MHz intra-cycle frequency sweep;
    • an on/off envelope at a 32 s class period at reduced power;
    • use clustered at meal times.
  • This modulation (60/120 Hz gating plus a 32 s envelope) is the discriminant. Under (c) it would ride on a lossless carrier.
  • HYP coupling: the probe/antenna in the waveguide launcher is an open-ended conductor carrying ~kW RF. It is the closest thing aboard to Hively's monopole, but with no skirt balun and inside a cavity.
  • Dan's cage observation of a 2.45 GHz longitudinal component is unverified (HYP at best) until §5 passes. If it passes, the obvious nearby source under (c) is a domestic oven, which the fingerprint test addresses.

3.2 Drives and converters.

  • FACT: PWM common-mode dv/dt up to 10 kV/µs and bearing charging current i_b = C_b dv_b/dt (Zhu et al. 2022).
  • FACT: US 440 V 60 Hz ship-service power with 400 Hz Type II/III (MIL-STD-1399-300B).
  • FACT: electric-drive propulsion on Columbia (CRS R41129; GAO-18-158); diesel-electric submarines are electric-drive by design.
  • HYP: the capacitive charging currents are Hively-type sources.
  • Under (a1) only the carrier and low harmonics travel. Under (c) the MHz edges become the favoured part.

3.3 Sonar. The most literal match to the criterion: charge accumulates on a capacitive dielectric termination, outboard (HYP). It ranks top under (a1) because ≤10 kHz propagates far and there is no hull. Its intermittency, and the fact that an active sonar is already acoustically loud, limit its tactical value.

3.4 ICCP / UEP. The strongest continuous in-water current. Its classical ELF field is measured, so any SLW claim must pass an SLW discriminant: E∥ with B = 0, or a range law inconsistent with classical quasi-static fields.

3.5 Arcing. The only category Hively himself names (faults, lightning, tape peeling; Hively & Reed 2020 p. 11 and p. 13; Hively & Land 2021 p. 2). It is HYP, with no measurement.

4. Hively transition mechanism (kept from batch 1)

  • Not found verbatim. No Hively paper, patent, erratum or hub page says "where a conductor ends and an insulator begins, energy converts to an SLW". Searched: /workspace/ohmic-slw-search, the hub papers and pages, US 9,306,527, US 12,525,711, web.
  • Closest analogues (HYP):
    • US 9,306,527 B1: "the skirt balun may dramatically reduce the return current to the source, so that essentially all of the electrical current goes into charging and discharging the antenna."
    • The same patent: "gradient-driven current density (J) that is maximal at the feed point (z=0) and zero at the end (z=L)."
    • Hively & Loebl 2019 App. B, p. 123: "The irrotational current density (JL) is maximal at the feed point, z = 0 (where the center conductor exits the coaxial cable), and zero at the end of the antenna (z = L)."
    • Hively & Reed 2020 pp. 10–11: "Essentially all of the current then goes into charging and discharging the center conductor ... The resultant current is irrotational, which is a necessary and sufficient condition for producing the SLW."
    • Hively & Reed 2020 p. 11: "charge accumulation causes arcing across the fault; this gradient-driven current creates the SLW."
    • Monstein & Wesley, EPL 59, 514 (2002): "an oscillating uniform spherical charge density that is the source of the radiating longitudinal electric E field."
  • Reading (HYP): the source is an irrotational charging current where current must stop: open ends, gaps, capacitive terminations. The insulator as such plays no role; the patent says the jacket "may make no difference". Strength would scale with the charging current, i = C dv/dt (FACT circuit law).
  • Negative case (HYP): closed return currents give C = 0 (Hively & Loebl App. C; Hively & Reed p. 9).
  • Mainstream counterpoint (FACT): Bray & Britton 2004: the radial current density must vanish at the conductor surface. Retarded potentials satisfy the Lorenz condition under charge conservation.

5. Verification checklist for Dan's 2.45 GHz cage reading (no TEM-based dismissals) transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

Standing rules:

  • No cable crosses the cage wall. The battery is inside. Data leaves by fiber.
  • Nothing modifies or opens an oven.
  • We contact nobody. The Steve Morris comparison is Dan's own.

Classical alternative to rule out experimentally, not by a propagation argument: ordinary TEM leakage through seams, apertures or penetrations of the cage. Steps 4–7 test for it directly. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

  1. 50 Ω swap (TinySA spurs). Replace the sphere with a 50 Ω load at the amp input, then at the TinySA input: same settings, same place, closed cage. Above ~800 MHz the TinySA Ultra generates "various mirrors and spurs" and LO leakage "up to -10dBm" (tinySA wiki; FACT). If the feature stays with the load, it is internal. Also run the amp alone, terminated, to catch ZK06-UM oscillation.
  2. Internal emitters. Power the inside instruments one at a time (fiber converter, battery booster, TinySA CPU). Change RBW and span: spurs move with settings, real signals do not.
  3. Magnetron fingerprint. Use HackRF or TinySA zero-span/waterfall, time-stamped. Look for:
    • (i) 2.45 GHz present only in one half of each 60 Hz cycle (16.7 ms repeat, ~50 % duty; IEEE 802.11-98/240), or 8.3 ms alternation for dual-magnetron ovens;
    • (ii) a ~50 MHz intra-cycle sweep and warm-up drift;
    • (iii) at reduced power, a 32 s-class on/off envelope (Sharp manuals);
    • (iv) no 60 Hz gating if the local oven is an inverter type (20–50 kHz; Panasonic).
    • Compare with Wi-Fi (20 MHz OFDM, 102.4 ms beacons) and Bluetooth (1 MHz hops). A feature locked to the local mains half-cycle is the oven signature, whatever the path.
  4. Router and oven off. Router off, phones in airplane mode, household oven unplugged; log times. Optionally run the unmodified oven normally (a cup of water), with times logged, to see if the feature follows it.
  5. Cage SE check at 2.45 GHz. Battery TinySA plus sphere or E5 inside, logging to SD. Known 2.45 GHz source outside (HackRF) at 1–3 m. Measure door open vs closed. Seam-coupon S21 with the NanoRFE VNA outside the cage. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  6. Seam bonding. Add continuous conductive-adhesive copper tape or more clamps along the rolled overlap, then repeat steps 5 and 3. If the feature falls as SE rises, it is leakage. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  7. Nothing crosses; nothing noisy inside. No USB, chargers or metal-sheathed fiber at the wall. No USB 3 devices running inside.
  8. Move and rotate the cage. Room standing waves change with position.
  9. Sphere vs dummy vs 50 Ω. Only the connected sphere should show the feature.
  10. E5 vs H20 (first HYP-specific test). Same spot, then rotate each through 90°. HYP: an SLW has B = 0, so E5 responds and H20 does not. A TEM leak or cavity mode shows both, orientation-dependent. Near a small internal source E/H ≠ 377 Ω, so a lone E response is not proof by itself. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  11. Material test (distinguishes the three readings). A water layer or tank between the source and the cage, varying thickness and salinity:
    • (a1) predicts 953 dB/m at 2.45 GHz;
    • (b)/(c) predict 1/r only;
    • the (a3) sensitivity predicts ~7.9 mm 1/e.
  12. Timestamped comparison with Steve Morris (Dan arranges it). Both stations log GPS/NTP-stamped spectra with the same span, RBW and windows. Compare event times, spectral shape and gating period, and whether each station tracks its own local router or oven.
    • No propagation argument is used to pre-judge a common source. Under (b)/(c) distance only adds 1/r.
    • Correlation at matching times with matching instrument models means a shared-artefact check first (same firmware spur, mains-synchronous gating at both sites).
    • Tracking of local sources means local leakage or local SLW.
    • Only a feature that passes steps 1–11 at both sites and is not explained by local sources or instruments is interesting.

6. Open questions

  1. Which propagation reading is right?
    • (a1), (a3), (b) and (c) differ by up to ~7 orders of magnitude in 1/e length at ELF. That is a spread in modelling conventions; there are no data to decide between them.
    • Only a controlled material test (step 11; Expt G pool mockup) can separate them. No Hively text supplies a per-metre SLW attenuation.
  2. Should the hub's Joule default stay? It is Dan's standing rule. §1.2 shows it is our construction, contradicted by the Erratum's cancellation and convention-sensitive. Dan may want to relabel it "pessimistic bound (ours)" rather than "Hively's Joule term". Decision for Dan; nothing changed here.
  3. SLW source-term magnitude: unknown for every device. Hively's EQN. 15 (P ∝ I²) applied to ordinary charging currents would make every power system a kW-class emitter (batch-1 MECHANISM §3). No independent report exists.
  4. Charge conservation: Hively's own □²C = 0 (patent EQN. 9) and Rentzber 2026 imply zero scalar sourcing by charge-conserving devices. Unresolved within EED.
  5. Interfaces (Eq. 26): no transmission coefficient is given for water→steel→air. Hull and cage transmission under (b)/(c) is assumed ≈ 1 (HYP), not derived.
  6. Background: under (c), every oven, drive and grid on Earth, plus lightning, is SLW background. No estimate exists.
  7. Hub typo (RESOLVED): experiment-c.html wrote "E ∝ exp(−ε t/σ)" where Hively's slide has e^(−σt/ε). Fixed in PR #58 (2026-09-26); author-confirmed 2026-09-30.
