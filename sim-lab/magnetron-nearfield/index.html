Test Plan: Longitudinal fields near a 2.45 GHz microwave-oven magnetron, and where to put the SLW/SW detectors

0. The question, and where it came from

  • Source (verbal only): Hively, personal communication to D. Britton, verbal, reported by Dan, 2026-09. There is no written record.
    • What was reported: the near field of any TEM source inherently contains longitudinal components, so SLW/SW detectors must sit well outside the near field. Otherwise a near-field longitudinal component could be mistaken for a distant SLW/SW. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • HYP (Dan): anything that generates a transverse wave also generates an SLW, and possibly an SW.
  • The two questions this plan answers:
    1. Does the near field of a 2.45 GHz oven magnetron (and of the oven's leakage points) contain a measurable longitudinal E alongside the transverse E?
    2. How far away must the SLW/SW detectors be so they can't confuse that ordinary near-field longitudinal E with a distant SLW/SW?

Short answers

  1. FACT (classical): yes, very close to any source. Inside the oven it is everywhere. Outside it, the longitudinal share dies off quickly with distance.
  2. Recommendation: put the receiver at least 3 × 2D²/λ ≈ 10–12 m (≈ 100 λ) from the oven door, sealed inside Dan's Faraday cage. The absolute minimum is ≈ 4 m (1 × 2D²/λ for a 0.5 m door, ≈ 32 λ).

1. (a) Near-field boundaries at 2.45 GHz

1.1 Basic numbers

  • FACT: λ = c/f = 0.1224 m (12.24 cm) at 2.45 GHz.
    • The LG 2M246 magnetron datasheet gives 2460 MHz into a matched load. That makes a < 0.5 % difference, so it's ignored here.
  • FACT (Balanis, Antenna Theory, 3rd ed., §4.2; Wheeler's "radian sphere"): for an electrically small source, the reactive near field is dominant inside r ≈ λ/2π = 1.95 cm (kr = 1).
  • FACT (Balanis 3e §2.2.4; IEEE Std 145-2013, definitions of field regions): for a radiator of largest dimension D, where D is comparable to or larger than λ:
    • the reactive near field extends to R1 = 0.62 √(D³/λ);
    • the radiating near field (Fresnel region) runs from R1 to R2 = 2D²/λ;
    • beyond R2 is the far field (Fraunhofer region).
  • FACT (Balanis §2.2.4): for D ≪ λ, these D-based formulas give distances shorter than λ, and so they don't apply. The far field then requires r ≫ λ (in practice several λ).

1.3 Classical longitudinal fields that exist for sure (FACT, and distinct from SLW)

These are ordinary, textbook longitudinal (along-the-propagation-direction) field components. They are not Hively's SLW. The SLW is a HYP. The point of this plan is to keep the two from being confused.

  1. TE10 launcher waveguide (FACT, Pozar 4e §3.3): the TE10 mode has transverse E_y only. It has no longitudinal E, but it does have a longitudinal magnetic field H_z, alongside H_x.
    • Computed for WR-340 (ASSUMPTION: oven launchers are close to, but not always exactly, WR-340, a = 86.36 mm):
      • TE10 cutoff = 1.736 GHz;
      • guide wavelength λg = 17.3 cm;
      • peak |H_z| / |H_x| = 1.004 (≈ 0 dB). The longitudinal H is as large as the transverse H.
    • FACT (Pozar 4e §4.7, probe excitation): longitudinal E appears only in the evanescent higher-order modes right around the magnetron's antenna probe. These decay within a fraction of a guide width.
  2. Cavity (FACT, Pozar 4e §6.3; Harrington 1961 §6-9): TM modes have a nonzero E_z, and TE modes have a nonzero H_z. A multimode oven cavity has field components along every axis. "Longitudinal" has no single meaning inside it.
  3. Small radiators, i.e. leaks near seams, slots and vents (FACT, Balanis 3e Eqs. 4-10a, 4-10b; Jackson 3e §9.2): the Hertzian (infinitesimal) dipole has a radial (longitudinal) E_r, which contains only the 1/r² and 1/r³ terms. The transverse E_θ also has a 1/r term, and that term is the one that radiates.
    • So far from the source, E_r / E_θ → 0 (it falls as 2/kr, from the ratio of Eqs. 4-10a/4-10b). Close in, E_r dominates.

Answer to question 1:

  • FACT (classical): yes. A measurable longitudinal E exists within a few centimetres of any leak point (E_r ≥ E_θ for r ≲ 4 cm), and inside the launcher and cavity.
  • Whether it is measurable outside the oven depends on the leak level. A legal oven leaks ≤ 5 mW/cm² at 5 cm (21 CFR 1030.10(c)(1)), and usually far less. That is easily detectable with a small probe at 5–20 cm (see Phase 1).

2.1 WL-1: analytic Hertzian-dipole decomposition (textbook closed form)

  • Inputs: f = 2.45 GHz and kr from 0.1 to 1000 (log-spaced).
  • Fields: use Balanis 3e Eq. 4-10a (E_r) and Eq. 4-10b (E_θ) exactly as printed. Drop the common factor ηI₀l/(4π), which cancels in the ratio.
  • Plot 1: 20·log10(|E_r(θ=0)| / |E_θ(θ=90°)|) vs r (log axis, in cm and in λ). Mark vertical lines at λ/2π, λ, R1 and R2 for D = 0.40/0.45/0.50 m, 3 × R2, and the −20/−30/−40 dB crossings.
  • Plot 2: the same ratio at θ = 45° (it is identical, as a check).
  • Pass criterion for the WL vs Python compare: every row of the table in §1.4 agrees to ≤ 0.01 dB, and the boundary table in §1.2 agrees to ≤ 1 mm.

2.2 WL-2: NDSolve / NDEigensystem FEM options (classical checks)

  1. Cavity eigenmodes:
    • Use a rectangular box. ASSUMPTION: typical interior ≈ 0.33 × 0.33 × 0.22 m; Dan should measure his.
    • Solve the scalar Helmholtz problem for TM modes (E_z with Dirichlet walls) with NDEigensystem on a 2D cross-section.
    • Compare the eigenfrequencies with the closed-form rectangular-cavity resonances (Pozar 4e §6.3). Plot |E_z| for the modes nearest 2.45 GHz.
    • This shows (FACT) that the cavity field is a standing wave with a longitudinal E.
  2. TE10 launcher (WR-340 cross-section):
    • Plot the analytic TE10 field components from Pozar §3.3: E_y, H_x, H_z.
    • Optionally, use NDEigensystem on the 2D cross-section to confirm the cut-off of 1.736 GHz.
    • Readout: peak |H_z|/|H_x| = 1.004 (0.03 dB) at 2.45 GHz.
  3. Optional (only if a full-wave solver is available in WL, heavy):
    • Model a slot in a conducting plane at 2.45 GHz and extract E along a line normal to the slot.
    • Otherwise, use openEMS (§4). It already covers this job on the shared computer.

3. (c) Test procedure and detector placement

3.0 Safety (all phases)

  • FACT: 21 CFR 1030.10(c)(1) limits oven leakage to ≤ 1 mW/cm² at ≥ 5 cm before purchase and ≤ 5 mW/cm² thereafter. §1030.10(c)(2) requires at least two operational interlocks.
  • FACT: 47 CFR 1.1310 Table 1 sets MPE limits for 1.5–100 GHz of 1.0 mW/cm² (general population) and 5 mW/cm² (occupational).
  • Rules:
    • The magnetron stays inside the intact, interlocked, unmodified oven. Never remove it, never run it outside the oven, never defeat an interlock, and never open the case.
      • FACT (LG service manual LSMC3089BD): the high-voltage circuit is "nearly 4000 volts". The LG 2M246 anode runs at 4.40 kVp.
    • Always run with a water load (e.g. 1 L of water in a microwave-safe container). Running empty damages the magnetron. Keep each run short (≤ 2 min) and change the water when it gets hot.
    • Check the oven with a leakage meter before Phase 1. If it reads above 1 mW/cm² at 5 cm, stop and don't use that oven.
    • People stay ≥ 1 m from the oven during runs, except when placing the Phase 1 probe with the oven OFF.

3.1 Phase 1 (classical, outside the cage): show the near-field longitudinal E exists, and map how fast it dies

  • Purpose: to measure the ordinary, classical longitudinal component that Hively warned about. This gives the "confusion zone" a real, measured size.
  • Probe: a short electric probe or small dipole (e.g. Tekbox E-field probe, or a DD-2450 cut short) feeding a TinySA on battery. This phase is outside the cage, so a coax is allowed.
  • Pointing (at each distance):
    • (A) radial: probe axis pointing at the leak point, which senses the longitudinal E;
    • (B) transverse, horizontal;
    • (C) transverse, vertical.
  • Distances from the hottest seam point (found with the leakage meter): 5, 7, 10, 14, 20, 28, 40, 56, 80 cm and 1.1 m (steps of √2). Stop wherever the signal hits the TinySA floor.
  • Expected (FACT, classical, Balanis Eqs. 4-10a/b, for a small leak): A/(B or C) ≈ 0 dB near 5 cm, falling to about −14 dB by 20 cm and −20 dB by 40 cm.
    • The total level should drop about 12–18 dB per doubling inside ~λ, i.e. 1/r²–1/r³ behaviour, and about 6 dB per doubling beyond a few λ (1/r).
    • ASSUMPTION: a real seam isn't a point dipole, so expect scatter. The trend is what matters.

3.2 Phase 2 (SLW/SW search): the receiver inside the sealed cage, far away

Where the receiver goes

  • Main position: ≥ 3 × 2D²/λ from the door face.

    Door diagonal D 3 × 2D²/λ In wavelengths
    0.40 m 7.9 m 64 λ
    0.45 m 9.9 m 81 λ
    0.50 m (use as the default) 12.3 m 100 λ
    • Recommendation: 10–12 m (≈ 100 λ). Here the classical longitudinal ratio of a small leak is ≈ −50 dB (FACT, from Eqs. 4-10a/b), and the door is 3 × deep into its far field (FACT, Balanis §2.2.4).

  • Minimum (if space doesn't allow the main position): ≥ 4.1 m (1 × 2D²/λ for D = 0.5 m; ≈ 33 λ; classical ratio ≈ −40 dB). Report any result taken there as "minimum-distance".

  • Oven orientation: the door faces the cage, because the door is the dominant external leak source. The cavity walls are solid metal, so leakage escapes mainly at the door seam and vents (ASSUMPTION: typical construction; Phase 1 leakage-meter survey confirms it for Dan's oven). Do a second set with the oven rotated 90° (see the controls).

Detector and cage

  • The receiver is a DD-2450 Dipole Detector plus a battery-powered receiver/logger (e.g. TinySA on battery), sealed inside Dan's Faraday cage.
    • FACT (classical): the cage strips TEM. HYP: whether SLW passes the cage wall. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
    • Do not use cage shielding numbers to predict or rank SLW/SW. Under the SLW hypothesis, the only material interaction is the Hively Joule term, J = σE (Hively & Loebl 2019, Eq. 44). transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • No cable crosses the cage wall. Power is a battery inside. Data comes out optically through a waveguide-below-cutoff tube, or is read after opening the cage.
  • Detector orientation: measure three orientations:
    • L: dipole axis along the line to the oven, i.e. longitudinal. HYP: this is where an SLW would couple, per Hively's longitudinal-E picture.
    • T1 and T2: transverse.

Measurement

  • Frequency: 2.40–2.50 GHz; watch 2.45–2.46 GHz.
  • ASSUMPTION: a transformer-type oven with a half-wave voltage doubler fires the magnetron in bursts at the mains rate (60 Hz). That on/off pattern is a signature the logger can look for. Inverter ovens differ; Dan should check which type his is.

Important logic

  • FACT (classical, Balanis Eqs. 4-10a/b): a radiating field falls as 1/r in amplitude.
  • HYP (Hively & Loebl 2019, Table I item 11): the SLW is also claimed to fall as 1/r² in power, i.e. 1/r in field amplitude.
  • So the distance sweep (C4) separates near-field from radiating. It does NOT separate SLW from TEM. That separation rests on the sealed cage (C5, C7), the controls (C1, C6) and orientation (C3). Even then, any "SLW" interpretation stays HYP. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Why the cage is expected to block TEM: that expectation is transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis. It is used only to remove TEM, never to predict SLW.
