Three readings of SLW loss: Joule-literal, Erratum α = 0, and charge relaxation

Dan asked whether the Joule term is being used properly. Some Hively texts say a scalar-longitudinal wave (SLW) has no loss in conducting media. Monstein & Wesley (2002) report an ohmic-like loss with distance. This page puts three clearly labelled readings side by side, using the hub's existing model code, and says what Monstein & Wesley's numbers can and cannot tell apart.

MIXED FACT = from a paper, patent or erratum (equation and page given). HYP = Hively's SLW hypothesis or our inference. ASSUMPTION = a modelling choice made on this page. ← Simulation Lab catalogue · Equation source and meaning map · Joule-loss positions · Corrections log

Framing rules on this page (Dan). Under the SLW hypothesis there is no skin-depth, permeability or ferromagnetic argument anywhere below. Allowed loss terms: Hively's Joule term σE∥² (Hively & Loebl 2019 Eq. 44 as corrected by the 2019 erratum), or none (the 2022 Erratum's "not subject to resistive loss"). Any classical transverse-EM reasoning is tagged transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis. Nobody has been contacted about any of this. The 2019 erratum PDF is private: its equations are cited, the PDF is not embedded or linked.

3. What follows directly: τ = ε/σ for each medium

FACT (author-confirmed exponent) E = E₀ exp(−σt/ε), τ = ε/σ, with ε = ε₀εr. τ/period = τ·f tells you how many relaxation times fit in one wave cycle: if it is much less than 1, free charge relaxes many times per cycle; if it is much greater than 1, charge hardly moves during a cycle. "Distance in τ" is how far the assumed plane-wave speed v = c/√ε′ would carry a signal in one τ (ASSUMPTION). εr here is the static value (MW2004 εs for the water rows), evaluated at 433.59 MHz for seawater and fresh water.

4. How Mode C enters a received-signal prediction: it cannot be made without inventing physics

Mode C, stated minimally

  • FACT Hively & Loebl 2019 (A4)–(A7), p. 122: the transient solution Ė + σE/ε = 0 decays as exp(−σt/ε) (correct form per the author). The same appendix then sets the transient aside and takes the non-transient solution E = E₀(r) e^(−jωt), □²E = 0, for which "the SLW propagates without loss" (A7). IARD 2020 slide 60 says the same in the same order. The 2022 Erratum drops the transient altogether.
  • HYP Mode C keeps that split: the propagating wave is not attenuated (curve identical to Mode B), and the transient describes what free charge does in time.
  • ASSUMPTION Nothing in any source we have turns the transient into a factor on the received signal. The exponent is in time t after a disturbance of the free charge. A received signal at distance d involves a travel time, not a waiting time after a step, and no source says that t = d/v, or any other t, belongs in that exponent.
  • FACT (arithmetic) If one did put t = d/v into exp(−σt/ε), the exponent would be the "t ÷ τ" column of the table in section 2. It would be huge in seawater at any distance beyond a few millimetres (v·τ ≈ 5.3 mm in the table) and would equal an attenuation constant σ/(ε₀ c √ε′). That would be a propagation loss, which is what Mode C says is absent, so we do not do it.

What is undetermined

  • Which time t, if any, enters the received signal (source-side, travel time, detector integration time).
  • Whether the relaxation acts on the amplitude of E, on C, or only on stored free charge.
  • Whether the receiver sees the relaxing charge at all (M&W and Hively both use a ball or monopole receiver that responds to induced charge, but no source gives the transfer function).
  • Whether "linear" σ (Hively slide 60) still holds in a real medium at these frequencies (slide 61 gives a loss mechanism only for nonlinear σ(|E|)).

So in this page Mode C's predicted signal is the Mode B curve, and what differs is only the τ table above. This is deliberate. A number that came from a guessed mapping would look like a prediction and would not be one.

5. The Monstein & Wesley Rhine geometry

Everything in this section is read from M&W, Europhys. Lett. 59(4) 514 (2002), pp. 517–520, and agrees with the hub /ranging/ page.

  • FACT 433.59 MHz (λ = 0.6914 m). Source ball at 4.0 m, receiver ball at 4.4 m physical height. Effective heights y = 4.346 m and y′ = 4.746 m (Eq. 12; the effective reflecting plane is λ/2 = 34.6 cm below the ground). The path is through air along a street on the bank of the Rhine near Sennwald, Switzerland (p. 518). They give the Earth's conductivity as "only about 10⁻³ S/m" and use it only to place that reflecting plane (p. 517).
  • FACT Signal model Eq. (14) with direct and image amplitudes A and B, R² = (y′−y)² + x², R′² = (y′+y)² + x² (Eq. 11). Nulls at R′ − R = (2n+1)λ/2 (Eq. 17). Table I: predicted minima at 119, 39, 23 and 16 m; observed at 39 m and 23 m; the other two "probably exist" but are not definite (noise and ±5 m GPS uncertainty), p. 519.
  • FACT Fitted: √(ωk/2)·A = 0.825 and √(ωk/2)·B = 0.573 (Eq. 22); ℜ = B²/A² = 0.482 (Eq. 24). Agreement with Eq. (14) is "quite satisfactory for x < 100 m".
  • FACT p. 520: beyond about 100 m the signal falls faster than 1/x². "This loss corresponds to an additional exponential loss with distance corresponding to 1/eth decrease in the signal for a distance of 145 m. This loss is probably due to the Ohmic losses produced by the currents induced in the Earth by the longitudinal E field, which is parallel to the Earth's surface." No equation and no σ is given for this loss, and the "signal" is the energy flux (p. 517).
  • FACT Fig. 5 (received power in dB versus distance, 10–1000 m) is a graph: one of six runs (V1) as dots, plus the Eq. (14) line. No numeric data points from Fig. 5 are recorded in the hub or in our notes, so none are plotted here.

Can M&W's data tell these readings apart?

Short answer: no. The numbers say why.

  1. The Rhine path is through air, where σ ≈ 0. With σ ≈ 0 the Joule term is zero (Mode A), Mode B has no loss, and Mode C has no relaxation. All three give the same curve at every Rhine distance (0.00 dB apart in the table above). No quality of data could separate them on that path. FACT (path and medium from M&W p. 518) plus arithmetic.
  2. Do the path length and 1/x² spreading alone reproduce the observed distance dependence? Not beyond about 100 m, by M&W's own account. Their model (Eq. 14, the image-source term plus spreading) matches "quite satisfactorily" for x < 100 m, including nulls at 39 m and 23 m. Past 100 m they needed an extra factor of 1/e in power per 145 m. With our arithmetic that extra factor is only −0.57 dB at 119 m, but −5.99 dB at 300 m and −26.96 dB at 1000 m. FACT (their statements) HYP (our dB arithmetic). Butterworth et al. 2013 (J. Sci. Explor. 27(1) 13) describe M&W's data as "a long tail-off that roughly approximates an inverse-square relation until about 200 m which then falls off more rapidly", so even the onset distance is reported as 100 m by one source and 200 m by another.
  3. Is that extra loss a Mode A Joule loss? Not determinable. M&W give no σ and no equation for it (they say it is "probably" Ohmic loss from currents induced in the Earth). Taking the hub's Joule-literal construction and our representative ground (σ = 10⁻³ S/m, ε′ = 15, an ASSUMPTION, using M&W's own σ ≈ 10⁻³ S/m) gives a power e-fold length of about 10.28 m, about 14.1 times stronger than the reported 145 m. To get 145 m the construction needs an effective σ of about 7.09×10⁻⁵ S/m. That is a free parameter: one adjustable σ can be made to fit one number, so a fit would not be a test. ASSUMPTION (ground values, and applying αJ to a ground path at all) and the construction αJ is ours, not Hively's (section 1).
  4. Mode C has no received-signal prediction to compare (section 4).
  5. Other explanations are open. Hively & Loebl 2019 App. D, p. 124, say M&W's outer coaxial conductor was grounded ("inducing unquantified electrical ground currents"), that the tests were "poorly controlled, and not reproducible", and that M&W omitted the image current. Whether the excess loss is SLW Joule loss, classical ground loss, or set-up effects cannot be separated from what is recorded.

What is missing for any real discrimination: (a) the raw Fig. 5 data points (the paper shows a graph only); (b) an effective ground σ and ε and a path geometry for the current induced in the ground, from any source; (c) a stated mapping from Mode C's relaxation to a received signal; (d) runs in a medium with large σ at a known distance, where Modes A, B and C actually differ (for seawater Mode A predicts about 313 dB/m of power loss at 433.59 MHz; B and C predict none). The wire-polarizer result (M&W Eqs. 7–9, κ = 4.4 fitted) is a separate Joule-type loss in brass and is not used here.

What this page does NOT show

  • It does not show that the SLW exists, that any of the three modes is true, or that Hively's or M&W's claims are right or wrong.
  • It does not show what Dr. Hively thinks about Mode A or Mode C. Nobody has been contacted. The only author statement used is the single sentence quoted for the relaxation exponent.
  • Mode A's αJ is the hub's construction, not a formula from any Hively text, and it depends on conventions (magnitude-only |Z|, choice of E∥, the dropped ρC/(εμ) source). The hub's own notes show another reasonable convention changes it by orders of magnitude at low frequency (Joule-loss positions; REDO_SLW §1.2).
  • Mode C's curve is Mode B's. The page does not claim charge relaxation has no effect on a received signal, only that no source gives how it would enter.
  • No measured Rhine data points are plotted, because none are recorded. The gold and salmon curves are M&W's own model, not their data.
  • Ground and fresh-water σ and ε are representative values (section 6), not measurements of the Rhine bank.
  • It does not use any skin-depth, permeability or ferromagnetic argument. The reasoning stays inside Hively's own Eq. 37, Eq. 44 and the Erratum.
  • Classical transverse-EM numbers are not computed here. If you compare with them elsewhere, label them transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis.

6. Sources, values and provenance

  • Mode A code. alphaJ(ep, epp, f) is copied unchanged from /slw-rcs/ (and from Experiment G, sleeve-balun-snr v0.3): αJ = ε₀ε″ω·|Z|/2 with |Z| = η₀/√ε′/√(1+tan²δ). Constants: c = 299 792 458 m/s, ε₀ = 8.8541878128×10⁻¹² F/m, μ₀ = 1.25663706212×10⁻⁶ H/m, η₀ = μ₀c. For the ground and custom media ε″ = σ/(ωε₀) (so σH = σ, dc conduction only); for seawater and fresh water the MW2004 dipolar part is added, as on /slw-rcs/.
  • Seawater. Meissner & Wentz, IEEE TGRS 42(9) 1836 (2004), Eqs. 6–8, 11–17, 15 °C and 35 ppt, the same code the hub already uses. The user's rule of thumb "εr ≈ 80, σ ≈ 4 S/m" is close; the model gives σ = 4.291353 S/m and ε′ = 73.27 at 433.59 MHz.
  • Fresh water, wet ground, dry ground. No hub values existed. Representative values (ASSUMPTION, editable): fresh water σ = 0.003 S/m with the MW2004 pure-water permittivity; wet ground ε′ = 30, σ = 0.01 S/m; medium-dry ground ε′ = 15, σ = 0.001 S/m. These follow the typical-values figure of ITU-R P.527-6 (Fig. 24) and P.527-3, which are given for ground-wave work below 30 MHz and vary at UHF (P.527-3 §2: "approximate range of values"). They are not from an SLW source.
  • Brass and copper. Brass σ = 1.5080260×10⁷ S/m (hub rod-thermal numbers.json, from copper.org), copper 5.8×10⁷ S/m.
