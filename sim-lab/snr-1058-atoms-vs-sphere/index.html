H 2S tickled-relaxation detector vs Experiment-A sphere at 1057.8 MHz: SNR comparison

Desk comparison at 1057.8 MHz: the hydrogen 2S tickled-relaxation detector vs the Experiment A ¾″ sphere with LNA chains. Verdict: the atoms are worse at every field from 1 nV/m to 1 kV/m for every sphere chain with an LNA (55–122 dB gap).

Verdict

The atoms are worse, by a very large margin, at every field from 1 nV/m to 1 kV/m, for every sphere chain that has an LNA.

Field needed for SNR = 1 in 1 s:

realistic: atom numbers demonstrated in the lab (N_2S 5×10⁷, ε 2×10⁻⁶), 10 nm filter, demonstrated stray-field level vs the modelled ¾″ sphere into 50 Ω with a ZX60-P33ULN+ LNA 49.6 mV/m 37.2 nV/m 122 dB
same atoms vs the same sphere on a TinySA Ultra alone (internal LNA on) 49.6 mV/m 69.2 nV/m 117 dB

the brief's optimistic case: N 10¹², ε 0.3, 10 nm filter vs the best sphere 111 µV/m 16.5 nV/m 77 dB
ideal bound: N 10¹², ε 0.3, a filter that passes only the Lyα line, 290 K vs the best sphere 29.2 µV/m 16.5 nV/m 65 dB
the same ideal bound in a 4 K radiation shield with no stray field vs the best sphere 9.77 µV/m 16.5 nV/m 55 dB
  • Integration time. When background dominates, the atoms improve as E_th ∝ t^−¼, while the sphere improves as t^−½. So every factor of 10 in averaging time widens the gap by another 5 dB (table in results_tables.md).
  • Crossover. There is none against any sphere chain that has an LNA, with either SNR convention, anywhere up to 1 kV/m. The only crossovers are against the sphere on a TinySA with its LNA off (NF 27.2 dB). They appear only in the σ-convention (defined under "SNR ratio" below), and only for the idealized atom cases A4–A6. Those cases need count rates of 10⁸–10¹² counts/s, which one detector cannot count (15 Mcps ceiling).

SNR ratio at fixed field, t = 1 s

Two conventions are shown for the sphere:

  • "brief": the brief's definition, P/(kT₀F·B) with B = 1/t.
  • "σ": the matched-filter significance √(2Pt/kT₀F), which is like-for-like with the atoms' S/√(S+B).

Ratio = 10·log₁₀(SNR_atom/SNR_sphere).

A5 ideal filter, 290 K vs S1 −65 / −49 −65 / −19 −94 / −18 290 K blackbody (saturated*)

* The no-signal count rate is above 15 Mcps.

The atom signal scales as E², so in the background-limited regime the "brief" ratio stays constant. It gets worse at high field because:

  • the signal becomes shot-noise limited (∝ E);
  • then it saturates, once the quench rate exceeds the 8.2 s⁻¹ refill rate (from about 0.3 V/m up).

In the σ-convention the sphere scales ∝ E, so the atoms narrow the gap as the field rises. The smallest gap is −13 to −18 dB for the idealized cases, near 10–140 mV/m, and −58 dB for the realistic case, near 0.27 V/m.

Dominant noise terms

Atoms. Per-atom background rates, with the resonant 1057.8 MHz field that would give the same rate:

  • Stray-field Stark quench. ≤ 1.77 s⁻¹ with the best compensation demonstrated (equivalent to 169 mV/m); 1000 s⁻¹ uncompensated.
    • FACT: Landhuis et al. saw 2S lifetimes ≤ 1 ms in a non-metallic cell. With copper-film electrodes and optimal compensation they reached up to 100 ms.
    • Ours: 1/0.1 s − 8.23 s⁻¹ = 1.77 s⁻¹. This is an upper bound on non-natural one-body loss.
    • This term dominates the realistic case, and it produces real Lyα photons, which no filter can separate from signal.
  • 2E1 continuum leaking through the Lyα filter.
    • FACT: commercial filters are 122 ± 2.5 nm, FWHM 10 nm max, T ≥ 15 % (Teledyne Acton FN122-XN). Pelham 122-NBX is about 10 nm with T ≥ 5 %; the 20 nm types are FN122-N and 122-NB.
    • Ours, from the Nussbaumer & Schmutz spectral shape: 0.044 (Lorentzian filter shape) or 0.021 (Gaussian) photons per decay get through a 10 nm filter. That is 0.37 or 0.17 s⁻¹ (equivalent to 77 or 53 mV/m).
    • A 20 nm filter passes 0.110 / 0.064 photons per decay. A hypothetical 1 nm filter would pass 3.9×10⁻⁴ (7 mV/m).
    • This term dominates once stray fields are below about 50 mV/m.
  • 290 K blackbody inside the cage: 8.1×10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ (equivalent to 3.6 mV/m).
    • It is 99.4 % the 9.9 GHz 2S→2P3/2 path. The 1.06 GHz path alone is 4.6×10⁻⁶ s⁻¹.
    • Ours: we used Lamb's matrix elements with the Planck spectrum. We cross-checked the 2P1/2 part as n̄·A with A(2S→2P1/2) = 8.0×10⁻¹⁰ s⁻¹; the two methods agree exactly.
    • At 4 K it drops to 1.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹.
    • It sets the floor only if the filter were ideal. It is the atoms' counterpart of kT₀.
  • ADS-B leaking through the cage: 2.8×10⁻¹¹ s⁻¹ at SE 55 dB (equivalent to 0.67 µV/m), which is negligible. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
    • With SE 0 dB it would be 8.7×10⁻⁶ s⁻¹, still below the blackbody term. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
    • It scales as 10^(−SE/10): 40 dB → 3.8 µV/m equivalent; 80 dB → 0.04 µV/m; 120 dB → 0.4 nV/m. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Detector dark counts: 0.02–1 cps. Negligible whenever N·ε ≳ 10.
  • Depletion at high field. When Γ_q > 8.2 s⁻¹ (above about 0.37 V/m), the signal saturates at the pump rate.
  • Counter saturation. Any case with N·ε ≳ 10⁸ sits above one detector's 15 Mcps ceiling.

Sphere:

  • Thermal noise. kT₀ from the 290 K cage walls that the antenna sees, times the chain noise factor. F_sys is:
    • 1.15 (0.60 dB) for ZX60-P33ULN+ → TinySA with its LNA on;
    • 4.0 (6.0 dB) for the TinySA alone, LNA on;
    • 525 (27.2 dB) with the TinySA LNA off.
  • Mismatch loss. The small sphere wired straight into 50 Ω loses −8.9 dB (openEMS, the sphere-alone model).
  • ADS-B at 1090 MHz is 32 MHz outside a 1 Hz matched filter, so it adds no in-band noise.
    • Even unshielded, one aircraft at 10 km (125 W) gives only −62 dBm peak at the input, far below compression.
    • At 1 V/m the wanted signal is −17.7 dBm (λ²/4π). That drives the TinySA near compression after the LNA, so an attenuator would be needed. The SNR is still enormous.

Everything the models rest on

Each item is tagged.

  • HYP: an SLW couples to ⟨2S|z|2P⟩ and to the sphere exactly as a TEM wave with the same E would. The coupling η is set to 1 for both, which is untested. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
    • The field-threshold gap closes only if η_atom/η_sphere ≥ 10^(gap_dB/10). That is 3×10⁶ for 65 dB and 1.6×10¹² for 122 dB.
  • ASSUMPTION: E is the RMS of a linearly polarized plane wave, with S = E²/η₀. The atom formula uses the peak value, √2·E.
  • FACT, quench rate: Lamb & Retherford, Phys. Rev. 79, 549 (1950), Eqs. 25–27 and 42. The rate was re-derived exactly as in LIT_CHECK, and the checks reproduce:
    • 3.10×10⁻¹¹ s⁻¹ for 1 µV/m peak;
    • 6.19×10⁻¹¹ s⁻¹ for 1 µV/m RMS (the full Eq. 25 plus the 2P3/2 path gives 6.194×10⁻¹¹ at 1057.8 MHz);
    • τ = 1.26 µs for Lamb & Retherford's worked example (they quote 1.25 µs);
    • 3.6 µs at 10 V/cm;
    • 2806 s⁻¹ at 1 V/cm, against Landhuis's quoted 2800·E² (cm²/V²) s⁻¹.
  • FACT/ours, hyperfine (ignored in LIT_CHECK, and still ignored in the headline numbers):
    • 1057.8 MHz is the fine-structure centroid. The real components sit at 909.87, 1087.43 and 1146.65 MHz (ours, from the 2S hyperfine interval 177.5568 MHz and the 2P1/2 hyperfine interval 59.22 MHz). Our 909.87 agrees with the published 909.894(20) MHz.
    • The trapped atoms of Landhuis et al. are 2S F = 1, m_F = 1 (FACT). For them the rate at 1057.8 MHz is 0.573× the centroid formula, which makes the atoms another 2.4 dB worse in field.
    • For the same atoms, ADS-B at 1090 MHz sits only 2.6 MHz from the F=1→F′=1 line (factor 0.81, against 0.706 for the centroid).
  • ASSUMPTION, pumped population: N_2S is held steady by a pump running at N·(loss rate). Landhuis made single-shot clouds, so this is optimistic.
  • ASSUMPTION, background subtraction: B is known exactly, so SNR = S/√(S+B). If B has to be measured in an equal off-time, the noise term becomes √(S+2B).
  • N_2S range: 10⁶–10¹². Landhuis demonstrated ≥ 5×10⁷ (8×10⁷ at 87 µK) as single-shot trapped clouds. Larger values have not been demonstrated.
  • ε_det range:
    • 2×10⁻⁶ is the Landhuis FACT value.
    • 0.039 is our physical ceiling: 4π collection × 15 % filter transmission × 26 % CsI quantum efficiency at 121.6 nm (FACT, Hamamatsu R13194 datasheet).
    • 0.3 is the brief's optimistic value, which cannot be reached with the cited filter.
  • ASSUMPTION, filter shape and blocking:
    • Gaussian or Lorentzian passband, centred at 121.6 nm.
    • Out-of-band blocking of 10⁻⁴ between 115 and 200 nm. This comes from Pelham's "visible rejection ~10⁻⁴"; the VUV blocking itself was not verified.
    • Above 200 nm, blocking of 10⁻⁴/8500, using the R13194 121.6/300 nm sensitivity ratio.
    • "Lyα-only" is a hypothetical ideal, not a product we found.
  • FACT, dark counts and maximum count rate:
    • Channeltron handbook: typically ≤ 0.05 cps.
    • Dr. Sjuts extended-dynamic-range channel electron multipliers (CEMs): < 0.02 cps, with a maximum of 15 Mcps.
    • Photonis Miniature Channeltron: 1 cps maximum.
  • ADS-B field:
    • FACT: Panken et al., MIT Lincoln Laboratory report ATC-390 (2012), measured at the JFK tower with a 0 dBi antenna: short Mode S replies above −74 dBm arrive at up to 1200/s × 64 µs, about 8 % time occupancy.
    • ASSUMPTION: the time-averaged power is 0.08 × (−74 dBm), which gives E_avg = 0.45 mV/m outside the cage. This is a lower bound at JFK; we bracket it ×0.1 to ×10.
    • FACT: 1090ES transmitters put at least 125 W at the antenna (FAA AC 20-165B; DO-260B classes A0 70 W, A1/A2 125 W, A3 200 W).
    • Cage SE was swept 0–120 dB, default 55 dB. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Stray-field bracket:
    • ≤ 1.77 s⁻¹ (demonstrated compensation);
    • 2.8×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ at 10 mV/m (ASSUMPTION, optimistic);
    • 7.6×10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ for the 52 mV/m motional field in LIT_CHECK;
    • 1000 s⁻¹ uncompensated.
  • Sphere geometry (FACT, hub): KB-1820, 19.05 mm outer diameter, 3003 aluminium, SMA bulkhead.
  • How the aperture was obtained (ours, openEMS FDTD, PEC): from each model's directivity and 50 Ω mismatch, using the reciprocity relation A = D(1−|Γ|²)λ²/4π (Balanis ch. 2). The two finest meshes agree to within 0.35 dB.

What the atoms offer that the sphere does not (fair statement)

  • An absolute, calibration-free response. The rate follows from atomic constants (FACT: Lamb & Retherford; NIST), so no antenna factor or S11 calibration is needed. The sphere's reading depends on the aperture, which the bracket above shows is uncertain by about 9 dB.
  • A line signature. The response is a Lorentzian of 99.7 MHz width at 909.9, 1087.4 and 1146.7 MHz. Its output is a single 121.6 nm Lyα photon, not a voltage. Sweeping the transmitter across these lines, including the hyperfine pattern, gives a spectroscopic fingerprint that pickup on a cable, connector or LNA cannot reproduce.
  • No metal at the sensing point. Readout is optical, which fits Dan's no-cable-through-the-wall rule better than any metal antenna does.
  • A different physical coupling channel. If the SLW HYP η differs between a bound electron and a metal sphere, only the atoms test the atomic E1 coupling.

As a field-sensitivity instrument, though, the atoms are 55–122 dB worse at 1058 MHz. They are also about 100 MHz wide, so without the cage they see the whole aeronautical radionavigation band around 1030/1090 MHz.

Not verified / open

  • ε ceiling components: the 4π collection assumption and filter transmission at exactly 121.567 nm (the spec is centred at 122 ± 2.5 nm).
  • Real filter shape: actual passband wings and VUV out-of-band blocking (we assumed 10⁻⁴).
  • Refill model: steady-state N_2S ≥ 5×10⁷ under continuous 243 nm pumping has not been demonstrated.
  • Laser-scatter background: under continuous pumping this could be large. Landhuis note laser-induced fluorescence that decays over a few ms after each pulse. Not quantified.
  • Radiation trapping: Landhuis report about 30 % loss at their densities. It is treated as part of ε.
  • Shielding of a real apparatus: a real trap sits in a cryostat and a copper-film cell. Those would shield TEM further (which helps against ADS-B) but make the same HYP η demand. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • Magnetic trap effects: Zeeman shifts of the hyperfine components in the trap are not modelled.
  • ADS-B at Dan's site: the average level there is unknown. JFK is only an anchor, bracketed ×0.1 to ×10. 1030 MHz interrogations and DME/TACAN (960–1215 MHz) also fall inside the atoms' Lorentzian; they were not quantified and the band edges were not verified in this pass.
  • Receiver noise with a reactive source: LNA noise parameters for a strongly reactive source are not modelled.
  • TinySA noise figure at 1.06 GHz: not measured.
  • Antenna model limits: the openEMS models omit the cable, tent and table. The PEC result is not validated against a VNA; Dan's VNA6000-A S11 of the real ball would pin case (a) down.
  • Citation checks: some citations were taken from abstracts, search snippets or datasheets, not full papers: Nussbaumer & Schmutz (constants as quoted in secondary sources), the 2023 PRL on the 2S hyperfine interval, and the 1975 PRL on the 2P1/2 hyperfine interval.
