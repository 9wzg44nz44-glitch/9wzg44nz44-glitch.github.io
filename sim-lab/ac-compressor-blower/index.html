NOTE.md — Home AC compressor and blower motors as SLW sources? (inrush vs running current)

Are home AC compressor and blower motors plausible SLW sources? Inrush vs running current, Hively's own charging-current criterion, ranked sub-events (PWM edges, contactor arcing, capacitor switching), a measurement plan, and the literature survey.

0. SUMMARY (verdict)

Is inrush magnitude itself a plausible HYP SLW source? No, by Hively's own written criterion.

Hively ties the SLW to an irrotational charging current where current must stop:

  • US 9,306,527: "essentially all of the electrical current goes into charging and discharging the antenna";
  • Hively & Reed 2020 p. 11: "charge accumulation causes arcing across the fault; this gradient-driven current creates the SLW."

A motor winding is a closed inductive loop, so its inrush and running currents are solenoidal. Hively's own writing gives circulating currents zero SLW (Hively & Reed p. 9; Hively & Loebl App. C).

Consistency check (ASSUMPTION, using HYP EQN. 15). If the whole motor current counted, a 5-ton compressor's 31.6 A running current would emit ≈30 kW of SLW. Its electrical input is only ≈5.5 kW. At 178 A LRA the figure would be ≈0.95 MW. Only a small charging fraction can be Hively's I.

The inrush (FACT).

  • About 6× running current for 100–300 ms (Copeland AE4-1331 p. 16).
  • The first half-cycle peak is 1.8–2.8× the rms symmetrical value (NEMA MG 1 §12.36).
  • Peak slope only ~10⁵ A/s, all at 60 Hz. That is the least HYP-relevant part, and the part Dan's TinySA can't see.

Sub-events ranked by HYP relevance (charging into open ends, gaps or capacitance, i = C dv/dt):

  1. Inverter PWM edges in ECM blowers (e.g. 20 kHz, TI TIDA-01353) and variable-speed compressors. dv/dt up to 10 kV/µs (Zhu 2022). Common-mode charging of winding-to-frame capacitance, continuous while running.
  2. Contactor or relay arcing. Showering-arc bursts with <10 ns rise, repeating at 10 kHz–1 MHz. This is Hively & Reed's arcing case.
  3. Start and run capacitor switching. Lumped ±Q, so weak (ASSUMPTION).
  4. 60 Hz line and winding-to-ground charging.
  5. The 60 Hz inrush envelope. Last.

Microwave oven. SLW emission from an oven is HYP. Its most HYP-like element is the magnetron's open-ended output probe at 2.45 GHz, plus a 4 kV doubler. Its "switching" is 60 Hz gating, or 20–50 kHz in inverter ovens. So it shares the fast-dv/dt ingredient with ECM blowers, not with compressor inrush.

Detection. Motor energy sits at 60 Hz–kHz, below the TinySA (100 kHz and up) and the Zeenko amps (~10 MHz and up). A copper-mesh cage gives only ~5–23 dB of magnetic shielding even at 10–150 kHz (Kühn 2013), and less at 60 Hz. False-positive risk is therefore high.

The discriminating test (§4):

  • inside the closed cage, battery-powered LF E (sphere plus preamp) and LF H (search coil) channels;
  • event times from a current logger or microphone outside;
  • pass only if E rises without H, beyond the calibrated cage transfer ratios.

The house-wiring and cage simulation is owed (§5).

Literature verdict (details in LIT.md, searched 2026-09-26):

  • (a) No measurement ties SLW or SW to motors, compressor inrush or inductive switching. Not peer-reviewed, not even self-published.
  • (b) Nothing compares magnetron switching with motor inrush. The scalar-wave literature uses microwave ovens as unpowered Faraday cages (Meyl kit, HYP), not as sources. The only "oven as SLW source" document is Dan's own July 2026 planning page (SPECULATION).
  • (c) Hively and the Keyport patent name arcs and sparks as SLW drivers: fault arcing (Hively & Reed 2020 p. 11), lightning and tape-peeling arcs (Hively & Land 2021 p. 2), a "resonant spark system" (US 12,525,711 claim 30). All HYP, none measured. Motors appear only as superconductor applications (Hively) or as receiver loads (Meyl, Handwerker).
  • An EED-internal preprint (Rentzber, arXiv:2608.19405, HYP) finds that charge-conserving sources give C = 0. If that holds, even arcs and PWM do not source the SLW unless local charge conservation fails.
  • Net: the ranking above rests on reading Hively's criterion, not on any observation.

1. AC compressor starting

1.1 Facts (cited)

  • Inrush ratio and duration (FACT). Copeland Application Engineering Bulletin AE4-1331 R9 (Sep 2024), p. 16, "Single Phase Starting Characteristics": "The starting Locked Rotor Amperage (LRA), also referred to as inrush current, is normally six or more times higher than the rated running amperage of the compressor and lasts from 100 to 300 milliseconds until the rotor starts turning."
    • p. 23: "'inrush current' and locked rotor amps (LRA) are one and the same." The nameplate LRA is measured on a locked compressor at the highest nameplate voltage, read after four seconds.
  • 5-ton example (FACT, datasheets). Copeland ZP57K5E-PFV (R-410A, 208–230 V, 1-phase, 57,000 Btu/h):
    • LRA 178 A and RLA 31.6 / 28.3 A (Baker Distributing and WalkairDirect listings);
    • AHRI-rated input 5460 W at 25.5 A (Copeland ZP-series sheet, dkkarani.com PDF);
    • so LRA/RLA ≈ 5.6 and LRA/rated current ≈ 7.0 (home_motor_numbers.json);
    • three-phase variants: ZP57K5E-TF5 LRA 136 A; -TFD (460 V) LRA 66.1 A, RLA 9.7 A (Sigler listing; Copeland ZPK5 service-kit table).
    • Note (FACT vs premise): the single-phase 5-ton LRA (178 A) sits above Dan's "~100–150 A" range. Three-phase or 460 V units fall inside or below it. The "3–8× RLA" range is consistent with Copeland's "six or more".
  • Asymmetric first half-cycle (FACT). NEMA MG 1-2016 Part 12, §12.36 "Instantaneous Peak Value of Inrush Current": "The values in the previous tables are rms symmetrical values ... There will be a one-half cycle instantaneous peak value which may range from 1.8 to 2.8 times the above values as a function of the motor design and switching angle." The DC offset depends on the point-on-wave at contact closure.
  • Start components (FACT).
    • AE4-1331 p. 16: "Start assist devices are usually not required"; PTC start devices "rated from 10 to 25 ohms may be used".
    • p. 18: "A start kit (specified start capacitor and relay)".
    • Single-phase scrolls use a run capacitor (e.g. ZR57K3-PFV: run cap 80 µF / 370 V, optional start cap 270–324 µF / 330 V; Copeland technical spec sheet).
    • ASSUMPTION (general HVAC practice, uncited): a potential relay drops the start capacitor out once the motor is up to speed. That is a contact opening in a capacitor circuit.
  • Contactor arcing (FACT). EMC Compliance training note "Transient susceptibility" (emccompliance.co.uk): "When a current is interrupted by an unsuppressed electromechanical switch (either a manual switch, contactor or relay) the resultant voltage rise across the gap causes a repetitive ignition and extinction of an arc discharge – the so-called 'showering arc' effect ... The burst repetition rate varies from 10kHz to 1MHz ... Typically the rise time of each strike is less than 10ns and the duration is tens to hundreds of ns; transit time is roughly 5ns/m." It also says these transients "couple along the supply circuit or are radiated from the conductors on either side of the switch" and "decay with distance".
    • ASSUMPTION: bounce on closure (with the 6× inrush) and arcing on opening both occur at the condenser contactor.
  • Inverter / variable-speed compressors (FACT).
    • Danfoss "Inverter scroll compressor VRJ, Application Guidelines" (residential, R-410A), p. 8: "With VRJ compressors, the LRA value is low because the variable speed drive reacts very rapidly (far below 1 second)."
    • The table lists VRJ028 at LRA 10.2 A versus MCC 18 A, so LRA is below the running maximum: no across-the-line inrush.
    • The PWM drive is the trade-off: carrier kHz to tens of kHz, edges to MHz (Zhu et al., IEEE Access 10, 125279–125280, 2022: PWM "several kHz up to tens of kHz", "dv/dt, typically up to 10kV/µs", EMI "several to tens of MHz").

1.2 Spectral content and slopes

  • Inrush: 60 Hz carrier with a 100–300 ms envelope step plus a decaying DC offset.
    • Peak slope of 178 A rms: ≈9.5×10⁴ A/s. At the NEMA half-cycle peak (320–498 A): ≈1.2–1.9×10⁵ A/s, treating that peak as a sinusoid amplitude.
    • Running (31.6 A): ≈1.7×10⁴ A/s.
    • Label: ASSUMPTION arithmetic, home_motor_numbers.json. This is "slow" (~10⁴–10⁵ A/s). The envelope change adds sidebands within a few Hz to tens of Hz of 60 Hz (ASSUMPTION).
  • Contactor arc: broadband, ns rise, 10 kHz–1 MHz burst rate (FACT, above).
    • ASSUMPTION: with hundreds of volts to kV across the gap in <10 ns, dv/dt ≳10¹⁰–10¹¹ V/s.
  • Inverter PWM: dv/dt up to 10 kV/µs = 10¹⁰ V/s (Zhu). Wide-bandgap example: 400 V in 25 ns ≈ 1.6×10¹⁰ V/s (Scelba et al., IEEE TIA 58, 4461, 2022, test conditions).

1.3 Coupling to Hively's HYP source term

  • Hively's criterion (HYP, quoted in /workspace/sim-lab/sub-sources/MECHANISM.md):

    • Hively & Loebl 2019 p. 123: "The irrotational current density (JL) is maximal at the feed point ... and zero at the end of the antenna (z = L)."
    • US 9,306,527: "essentially all of the electrical current goes into charging and discharging the antenna".
    • Hively & Reed 2020 p. 11: "charge accumulation causes arcing across the fault; this gradient-driven current creates the SLW."
    • Hively & Reed 2020 p. 9: "A TEM transmitter produces only circulating currents, yielding C = 0 and EL = 0 with no SLW power output or reception." transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

  • Motor winding (ASSUMPTION reasoning on HYP). The winding is a closed loop from line to line (or line to neutral). Current enters and leaves; no charge accumulates. Inrush magnitude therefore scales a solenoidal current, which gives no SLW under HYP.

  • Candidate HYP-relevant pieces (ranked in §0):

    • (a) contactor or relay arcing, the gap-charging and breakdown case;
    • (b) common-mode charging of winding and cable capacitance to the grounded frame, i = C dv/dt (Zhu Eq. 3). At 60 Hz this is small. With PWM edges it is large (Scelba et al. 2022: "the fluctuation of the common-mode voltage generates currents that flow to the ground through parasitic capacitances between the stator windings and the motor frame. The amplitude of such currents depends on the rate of variation of the common-mode voltage and increases with switching frequency");
    • (c) start and run capacitors. Charge does accumulate there, but as ±Q on closely spaced plates, which is dipole-like and closed by displacement current. ASSUMPTION: by analogy with Hively's App. C, whose balanced dipole gives no SLW output, a lumped capacitor is a weak HYP source compared with an unbalanced, monopole-like charging path to ground.

  • EQN. 15 internal-consistency flag (ASSUMPTION using HYP). Put P_OUT = (I²/4π)·√(μ/ε) with √(μ₀/ε₀) = 376.73 Ω:

    Current EQN. 15 result
    178 A (LRA) ≈950 kW
    31.6 A (RLA) ≈30 kW
    7 A (PSC start) ≈1.5 kW
    1.5 A (PSC run) ≈67 W

    The RLA figure exceeds the compressor's 5460 W input, which violates energy conservation. So I in EQN. 15 cannot be the motor's supply current. It can only be the irrotational charging component, consistent with Hively's own definition.

    • Same flag as the sub-sources MIL-STD-1399-300B case: a 20 A capacitive line-to-ground current gives ≈12 kW.
    • A testable prediction for HYP: if even 1 A of common-mode charging current were fully "gradient-driven", EQN. 15 predicts ≈30 W of SLW from every VFD-equipped appliance.

2. Indoor blower: PSC vs ECM

  • PSC (FACT). A PSC motor keeps the capacitor winding in circuit continuously. Beckett Technical Bulletin "An Introduction to PSC Motors" (Oct 1999), p. 1: "Both the capacitor (auxiliary) winding and the main winding remain in the circuit the entire time the motor is running, hence the name 'permanent'."
    • Table 1 (1/7 hp PSC burner motor, a close analogue of a small blower): starting (locked-rotor) current 7 A vs running 1.5 A, i.e. ≈4.7×. That is higher than the "~2–3×" in the prompt.
    • Blower-specific PSC LRA data were not found; see Blockers.
    • PSC motors start across the line through a relay in the furnace control, so inrush is at 60 Hz (ASSUMPTION: same physics as §1.2, at ~1/25 the current). Relay arcing also applies.
  • ECM (FACT). TI reference design TIDA-01353 (TIDUCE0A), "Electronically Commutated Motor Reference Design for HVAC Blowers" (½ hp, 115 V AC):
    • spec table p. 2: "Inverter switching frequency 20 kHz", DC link 163.5 V typical;
    • the intro contrasts it with "a permanent split capacitor motor, or PSC";
    • §2.2.3.5–2.2.3.6: open-loop start-up where "the START_UP_DUTY parameter is gradually increased to START_UP_DUTY_MAX", a soft start.
    • Power Integrations RDR-872 (70 W fan inverter) lists 10 kHz PWM. The carrier is manufacturer-specific.
    • The furnace's 100 Hz–1 kHz "PWM" airflow command is a low-voltage control signal, not the power switching (hvac-fanmotor.com guide; low-authority source, FACT only for the distinction).
    • ECM consequences: little inrush (soft start; plus the DC-link capacitor inrush at power-up, limited by an NTC or thermistor in typical designs, as in RDR-872 "inrush thermistor RT1"). But there are continuous fast PWM edges and common-mode currents to ground while running. This is the HYP-favoured ingredient (Zhu Eq. 3; Scelba et al. 2022).

4. Detection with Dan's gear, and a discriminating test

4.1 Why the current setup is blind or false-positive-prone for motors (FACT + Dan's gear)

  • Frequency coverage.
    • Motor inrush energy lies at 60 Hz–kHz. The TinySA Ultra's normal range starts at 100 kHz, and the Zeenko amps are not usable below ~10 MHz (per Dan's gear note).
    • Only contactor-arc bursts and the upper PWM harmonics fall in band. Those are classical broadband EMI that the TinySA will see if anything leaks.
  • Cage magnetic shielding is poor at low frequency (FACT). Kühn, John & Weigel, Adv. Radio Sci. 11, 189 (2013), measured a closed copper-mesh enclosure (30×30×10 cm, overlapped mesh) at 10–150 kHz. Magnetic SE was "very low (from 5 dB to 23 dB in the frequency range to be considered)" in one orientation, and ~20 dB higher in the other. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
    • They note measured values run ~22 dB below the Kaden analytical model, due partly to imperfect seam connections.
    • Magnetic (eddy-current) shielding falls with decreasing frequency (their Fig. 3/5 behaviour). At 60 Hz a thin non-ferrous mesh gives little H shielding.
    • The electric field, by contrast, is strongly shielded: the paper says "the incident electrical field could be neglected due to Faraday cage effect".
  • Resulting false-positive path (ASSUMPTION). 60 Hz or kHz H-field from the compressor supply loop leaks into the cage. It induces EMF in any loop formed by sphere, preamp, battery and leads. That EMF looks like a "sphere signal inside a Faraday cage". Vibration at compressor start (microphonics, triboelectric cable noise) is a second path.

4.2 Proposed discriminating test (literature-based design; not run)

Goal. Look for an E-channel response inside the closed cage, time-locked to motor start events, without a matching H-channel response. Its size must exceed what the measured cage transfer ratios predict classically.

  • The cage strips TEM (FACT for TEM). SLW passing the wall is HYP. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

Outside the cage (event timing and reference):

  1. Start-event timestamps.
    • Preferred and non-invasive: a clamp-on AC current logger on the condenser or air-handler supply. Use a split-core CT on a single conductor at an accessible point, installed by a qualified person if any panel or disconnect cover must be opened, with a battery logger sampling ≥1 kHz.
    • Alternatives: a microphone or accelerometer on the condenser, or a light sensor on the blower status LED. Either gives start times to ~ms without touching mains.
  2. Outside E and H reference sensors near the cage: LF E-probe (a short monopole or plate into a high-impedance preamp) and an LF search coil, logged on the same clock.

Inside the closed cage (battery-powered; no cable crosses the wall; data out by fiber or SD card): 3. E-channel. Existing KMAC sphere into a high-impedance, low-noise LF preamp (FET buffer, 10 Hz–100 kHz; specific part TBD) into a battery 24-bit recorder (96–192 kHz sampling covers 10 Hz–~90 kHz).

  • Also, or instead, an Airspy HF+ Discovery (Expt D shopping list: HF coverage 0.5 kHz–31 MHz per airspy.com, as quoted in expt-d/web/slw-detectability.html) behind an LF preamp, for the kHz–MHz PWM and arc bands, on a battery host with fiber out.
  1. H-channel. A multi-turn LF search coil or a battery fluxgate/ELF magnetometer on the same recorder. The Tekbox H20 loop is too small for 60 Hz sensitivity (ASSUMPTION).
  2. Control channels (same recorder):
    • a dummy sphere (same size, disconnected or terminated);
    • a shorted-input preamp;
    • an accelerometer (to flag vibration).
  3. Clock sync. Sync before closing the cage, plus a fiber-coupled LED flash as a shared marker. GPS will not work reliably inside the cage.

Procedure.

  • (i) Calibrate outside-to-inside transfer ratios for E and H using a known LF source (a Helmholtz or loop coil for H, a plate for E) at 60 Hz, 1 kHz, 20 kHz and 100 kHz, following the Kühn 2013 method.
  • (ii) Log ≥50 compressor and blower start/stop events. Include ECM run periods, and inverter-compressor periods if fitted.
  • (iii) Epoch-average around each event.
  • (iv) Classical prediction: inside E ≈ outside E × (E transfer, tiny); inside H ≈ outside H × (H transfer, near 1 at 60 Hz); sphere EMF explained by H pickup in its loop and by vibration.
  • (v) HYP-supporting outcome: a sphere E response not explained by the H-channel times the loop pickup, absent on the dummy sphere and shorted input, not correlated with the accelerometer, and repeatable across cage rotations.
  • (vi) Repeat with the cage moved farther from the supply run. Classical H falls with distance from the wiring loop.

Equipment to add (ASSUMPTION list; prices and parts not checked):

  • Airspy HF+ Discovery;
  • LF high-impedance preamp;
  • battery 24-bit audio recorder or DAQ;
  • LF search coil or fluxgate;
  • battery current logger with split-core CT (or a microphone logger);
  • fiber LED sync link.

4.3 What TinySA can still do now

  • Watch 100 kHz–30 MHz for contactor-arc bursts and ECM PWM harmonics, inside and outside the cage, time-locked to start events.
  • Anything seen inside is first a cage/seam/cable leak (VERIFY.md steps in /workspace/sim-lab/sub-sources/) before it is anything else.

Source heading: LIT.md: literature search on motors, compressor inrush, inductive switching and microwave ovens as scalar / longitudinal-wave sources

Labels

Evidence labels (as defined for this task):

  • FACT: published, peer-reviewed measurement.
  • HYP: published claim or hypothesis, not independently replicated. Peer-reviewed theory and self-published experiments both fall here.
  • SPECULATION: no experimental backing (books, forums, blogs, marketing, planning documents).

Direction column:

  • pro-SW: claims a scalar or longitudinal effect.
  • null/classical: finds an ordinary explanation.
  • theory: no measurement.
  • context: not about sources.

Local copies are in src/lit/. Earlier local texts are in /workspace/ohmic-slw-search, /workspace/hively-monstein and /workspace/hub/papers.

1. Category verdicts (short)

(a) Experimental detection of scalar/longitudinal waves from motor starting, compressor inrush or inductive-load switching: nothing found.

  • No peer-reviewed or self-published measurement ties a scalar or longitudinal wave to a motor start, compressor inrush or contactor/relay switching.
  • The closest items are:
    • one self-published spark-gap experiment on the propagation speed of a collapsing Coulomb field (Gasser 2016, HYP). It is not a motor or an inductive load.
    • Hively's and the Keyport patent's designation of arcs and sparks as SLW drivers (HYP, no measurement).
    • the Tesla-derived "radiant energy" thesis that abrupt DC interruption across a spark gap emits a longitudinal ray (Lindemann 2001, SPECULATION).

(b) Literature comparing magnetron switching with motor inrush as scalar-wave sources: nothing found.

  • No paper, patent or report compares the two.
  • In the scalar-wave literature the microwave oven appears as an unpowered Faraday cage (Meyl's kit manual; Meyl Docu §VII "The mobile phone in the microwave"), not as a source.
  • The only document proposing a microwave oven as an SLW source is Dan's own planning page, "Proposed Experiment: Hively and Microwave Oven" (July 2026). Its basis is extrapolation from Hively's 8 GHz reports; it contains no measurement (SPECULATION).

(c) Mentions by named researchers of motors, compressors, inductive switching or household appliances as candidate sources:

  • None names motors, compressors or household appliances as SLW/SW sources, with one exception: Meyl's generic claim that "technical devices" radiate longitudinal vortex waves (electrosmog book blurb, SPECULATION).
  • Hively mentions "electric motors and generators" only as applications of high-temperature superconductors (US 9,306,527, cols. 17–18).
  • Hively's closest source statements are about arcing: seismic arcing across a fault (Hively & Reed 2020, p. 11), lightning and tape-peeling arcs (Hively & Land 2021, p. 2).
  • The Keyport sleeve-balun patent claims a "resonant spark system" feeding the antenna (US 12,525,711 B1, claim 30).
  • Meyl and Handwerker use motors only as loads driven by Tesla-coil receivers.
  • A 2026 arXiv EED paper (Rentzber) states that charge-conserving sources, including all ordinary circuits, give C = 0 and no scalar-longitudinal radiation, even within extended electrodynamics. That excludes motors, arcs and PWM unless local charge conservation fails (theory, not peer-reviewed).

Hub pages. No hub page presents a microwave oven as an SLW source.

  • Every hub copy (hub-efield-repo, hub, slw-hub) states "No magnetron source in this hub's lab protocol". This appears in library.html, learn.html, compare.html, future-scalar-ab.html, sw-vs-slw.html and c-nuclei*.html.
  • lab.html says "no magnetron, no microwave oven, no 8 GHz TWT as the primary source" and "No modified microwave oven in this protocol".
  • monstein.html says "13 cm (2.45 GHz ISM / ham segments) only if you replicate. No magnetron protocol."
  • index.html links Dan's oven page as "Proposed oven experiment (historical; this hub does not use a magnetron)".
  • The only basis for "oven as source" in the hub ecosystem is that linked planning page (SPECULATION).
