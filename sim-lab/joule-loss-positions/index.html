1. Monstein & Wesley 2002: loss or attenuation of the longitudinal E field

1.1 Their field theory has no loss term

  • FACT, p. 514, Eq. (1): "∇²Φ − ∂²Φ²/∂t²c² = −4πρ, (1)" (printed exactly so, including the "Φ²").
  • FACT, p. 514, Eq. (2): "S⃗ = −∇Φ ∂Φ/∂t, D = (∇Φ)²/2 + (∂Φ/∂tc)²/2. (2)"
  • FACT, p. 515, Eq. (5): "E⃗ = ∇Φ = [k q cos(kr − ωt)/r − q sin(kr − ωt)/r²]e⃗_r, (5)"
  • FACT, p. 515, Eq. (6): "⟨S⃗⟩_t = k ω q/(2r²)e⃗_r, (6)"
  • FACT: Eqs. (1)–(6) contain no conductivity σ, no Ohm's law and no damping term. The only source term is −4πρ.
  • HYP: the −4πρ and "∂Φ/∂tc" forms suggest Gaussian units. Not stated in source.

1.2 Joule (Ohmic) loss that they DO derive: the wire polarizer (pp. 516–517)

  • FACT, p. 516: "…only the E⃗ field component E₀ cos(φ) in the direction of a wire produces Ohmic losses. The Ohmic loss δP per unit time in an element δL of the wire is then equal to δP = −(δV)²/δR, where δV = E₀ cos(φ)δL is the potential across the element δL and where δR = δL/σA is the resistance of the wire element of length δL, and A is the cross-sectional area and σ the conductivity of the brass wires; thus,"
    • Eq. (7), p. 516: "δP = −[σAE₀² cos²(φ)] δL. (7)"
  • FACT, pp. 516–517: "It may be readily shown from the first of eqs. (2) that the time average energy flux in the wire equals the time average electric field E₀ squared times c, ⟨P/A⟩_t = ⟨S⟩_t = cE₀². Thus, from eq. (7) for the rate of energy flow loss δ⟨S⟩_t in the wire per unit length δL is given by"
    • Eq. (8), p. 517: "δ⟨S⟩_t = −(σ/c) cos²(φ)⟨S⟩_t δL. (8)"
  • FACT, p. 517: "Integrating eq. (8), the fractional energy transmitted through the wire of length L is"
    • Eq. (9), p. 517: "T = exp[−κ cos²(φ)], (9)"
    • The text continues: "where κ is a constant proportional to the length L of the wire. … the theoretical result eq. (9) can be chosen to fit the observations when κ = 4.4, as shown in fig. 3."
    • Fig. 3 caption, p. 516: "The solid curve presents the theory, eq. (9), T = exp[−4.4 cos²(φ)] for the best-fit value of κ."
  • Derived or assumed?
    • FACT: they derive Eq. (7) from Ohm's law (δR = δL/σA) and Eqs. (8)–(9) from it.
    • FACT: κ is fitted (4.4), not computed from σ. No numerical σ for brass is given (not stated in source).
  • Type of attenuation. FACT: it is in distance: per unit wire length δL, exponential in L. It concerns energy flux (power), and it is a Joule term: δP ∝ σE∥²·(A δL), with E∥ = E₀cos φ.
  • Medium and frequency. FACT: a 3×3 array of "9 wires of length λ/2 (34.6 cm)" (p. 516), brass, at 433.59 MHz (Fig. 3 caption).
  • HYP: Eq. (8) taken literally, with a metallic σ, would give a far larger κ than 4.4 for 34.6 cm wires. The fitted κ is therefore not a quantitative check of Eq. (8). This depends on the unit system (see 1.1) and is not computed in the source.

1.3 The excess loss over distance above ground (p. 520): measured, attributed, no equation

  • FACT, p. 520: "Exponential loss of the signal with distance. – It is apparent from fig. 5 that the observed signal beyond 100 m decreases more rapidly with distance than the inverse square of the distance. This loss corresponds to an additional exponential loss with distance corresponding to 1/eth decrease in the signal for a distance of 145 m. This loss is probably due to the Ohmic losses produced by the currents induced in the Earth by the longitudinal E⃗ field, which is parallel to the Earth's surface. No such loss would be expected for transverse wave, where the electric E⃗ field is normal to the Earth's surface. This exponential decay of the signal with distance, thus, further indicates the fact that longitudinal electrodynamic waves are involved."
  • FACT: there is no equation number, no formula and no conductivity value attached to this loss. It is an empirical extra exponential in distance, read off Fig. 5. It is attributed ("probably") to Ohmic loss in the Earth, not derived.
  • FACT, p. 517: "The signal observed equals the time average energy flux S⃗, as given by the first of eqs. (2)." So "signal" means power flux.
    • HYP (our arithmetic): 1/e in power at 145 m gives α_P ≈ 1/145 ≈ 6.9×10⁻³ m⁻¹ ≈ 0.030 dB/m. The field coefficient would be ≈ 3.4×10⁻³ m⁻¹. Neither number is in the source.
  • FACT, p. 517 (the only Earth-conductivity number): "Since the conductivity of the Earth is only about 10⁻³ ℧/m [4], the effective reflecting surface of the Earth is a little below the actual surface. To agree with observations, the effective surface is chosen as half wavelength, λ/2 = 34.6 cm, below the actual surface."
    • This σ is used for the image/reflection geometry, not for the loss (not stated in source).
  • Medium, frequency, geometry and other numbers
    • FACT: propagation in air above ground on the bank of the river Rhein near Sennwald, Switzerland (p. 518), at 433.59 MHz (Abstract).
    • FACT: source 4.0 m and receiver 4.4 m above ground (Abstract); effective heights "y = 4.346 m and y′ = 4.746 m. (12)" (p. 517).
    • FACT: Fig. 5 spans 10–1000 m; acknowledgments mention "distance of 1 kilometer".
    • FACT: agreement with Eq. (14) is "quite satisfactory for x < 100 m" (p. 519).
    • FACT: minima observed at 39 m and 23 m (Table I, p. 518; p. 519). Earth reflection coefficient "ℜ = B²/A² = 0.482. (24)" (p. 519). Asymptotic fit "⟨S_x⟩_t = (1/x²)[1 + 0.945 cos k(R′ − R)], (23)" (p. 519).

1.4 Answer to Q1 in one line

  • FACT: M&W give one derived Joule loss law, Eqs. (7)–(9): Ohmic loss σE∥² in brass wires, exponential in wire length, fitted with κ = 4.4.
  • FACT: for the ground loss over distance they give no equation. They give a measured extra 1/e per 145 m (power) beyond 100 m at 433.59 MHz over ground, which they say is "probably" Earth Ohmic loss.
  • FACT: their scalar field equations (1)–(6) contain no conductivity. Both losses are added from Ohm's law or from the data, outside the field theory.

2. Hively's position on a Joule loss term for the SLW E field

2(a). Hively & Loebl, Phys. Essays 32, 112 (2019)

  • FACT, p. 114, Eqs. (21)–(22):
    • "∇ × B − (1/c²) ∂E/∂t − ∇C = μJ; (21)"
    • "∇•E + ∂C/∂t = ρ/ε. (22)"
  • FACT, p. 116 (the no-skin-effect argument): "The resultant electric field is Eᴸ = Jᴸ/σ = ∇κ/σ for media with a linear conductivity, σ. Eᴸ and Jᴸ for the SLW are curl-free. Faraday's law becomes ∇ × Eᴸ = −Ḃ = ∇ × ∇κ/σ = 0. … Thus, no eddy currents occur, so the SLW is unimpeded by the skin effect for propagation through linearly conductive media. See also Graham et al.⁵³ and Appendix A."
    • Then: "C, Eᴸ, and Jᴸ are all related by Eq. (32b) with⁵⁴ σ = ε_o ε″ ω and ε = ε_o(ε′ − jε″) for a SLW impedance, Z,"
  • FACT, p. 116, Eq. (37): "Z = |E_r|/|C/μ_oμ′| = √(μ_o/ε_o) √(μ′/ε′) (1 − 1/(jkr))/(1 − j tan(δ_ε)). (37)"
    • Text: "Equation (37) assumes spherical waves in linear conductive media: Eᴸ = E_r r̂ exp[j(kr − ωt)]/r, together with C = C_o exp[j(kr − ωt)]/r."
  • FACT, p. 117, Eq. (44) as printed:
    • "∂/∂t (B²/2μ + C²/2μ + εE²) + ∇•(E × B/μ + CE/μ) + J•E = ρC/(εμ). (44)"
    • How it is built (p. 117): "EED predicts a revised energy balance, Eq. (44), from the sum of: (C/μ) times Eq. (22); (B/μ)• applied to Faraday's law; and application of −(E/μ)• applied to Eq. (21)."
    • After it: "Equation (44) has new energy density terms: scalar field energy (C²/2μ), SLW energy (CE/μ), and a power source (ρC/εμ)."
    • Note: Eq. 44 prints "εE²" without /2, while the patent's EQN. 14 prints "εE²/2" (see 2(d)).
  • FACT, p. 117: "Revised energy balance, Eq. (44), shows that the scalar-wave energy is C²/2μ ∼ 1/r². Thus, the total scalar-wave energy, 4πr²(C²/2μ), is constant through a spherical boundary around a source and C(r → ∞) → 0, as expected."
  • FACT: HL2019 does not call J·E in Eq. 44 a loss or a gain for the SLW, and gives no attenuation constant. Not stated in source.
  • FACT, p. 122, Appendix A, Eqs. (A4)–(A7):
    • "(A4) □²(Ė + J/ε) = □²(Ė + σE/ε)."
    • "One solution to Eq. (A4) sets the terms inside the parentheses to zero, as a transient solution⁴ that typically decays in ∼10⁻¹⁹ s"
    • "E = E_o e^(−εt/σ). (A5)" TYPO as printed (ε and σ swapped; author-confirmed 2026-09-30, correct form E = E_o e^(−σt/ε))
    • (A6) "E = E_o(r)e^(−jωt)"; then (A7) □²E = 0.
    • "The lack of a source term on the RHS of Eq. (A7) means that the SLW propagates without loss in isotropic, homogeneous conductive media (σ = constant). That is, the SLW is unconstrained by the skin effect."
    • This is a time transient, not a distance attenuation. The units issue (τ = ε/σ favours e^(−σt/ε)) is in the internal Joule-term comparison note (not published). Resolved 2026-09-30: author-confirmed, see the note at the top of this page.
  • FACT, pp. 119–120: "…resonance-cavity effects that are not included in the present theory. SLW attenuation in conductive media is the subject of future work. The combined solid-copper thickness (2 × 1.02 mm) is 2914 skin depths in the result of Fig. 4, which should produce a classical attenuation of ∼10⁻¹²⁶⁵ in TEM waves. The test measurement yielded an attenuation between −115 dB at r = 2 cm separation and −137 dB at r = 30 cm. Extrapolating the straight-line fit to a separation of 2 mm (where the enclosed antennas are barely separated) gives −79 dB and −86 dB, for an attenuation of ∼10⁻⁴." transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
  • FACT, p. 121: "Wave penetration of the two 1-mm thick Faraday enclosures (2-mm total) demonstrates the low-loss nature of the SLW in conductive media. A more accurate and compelling result clearly requires a much better experimental design, which is the subject of future work."
  • Summary, 2019.
    • FACT: "without loss" in (A7) text, but "low-loss" on p. 121, and attenuation in conductive media is "future work" on p. 120.
    • HYP: the 2019 position is no skin effect plus no loss in linear media in theory, while the measured loss is not claimed to be zero.

2(e). What the Erratum's conclusion means for a Joule term σE∥²

  • FACT: J·E appears explicitly in Eq. (44) (HL2019 p. 117) and in EQN. 14 (patent). With J = σE it would be σE² (HYP: the substitution J = σE is the patent's col. 6 wording, not Eq. 44's).
  • FACT: Hively does not say J·E = 0, and does not say σE² disappears. He says the "no-loss result occurs from … Eq. (44), by substituting for (ρ/ε) from Eq. (22) and … Jᴸ from Eq. (21) with B = 0, then cancelling terms" (Erratum, p. 320). The patent says "may yield an identity, 0=0" (col. 6).
  • FACT: Eq. (44) itself was built from Eqs. (21), (22) and Faraday's law (p. 117 quote above).
  • So how far does the source show the cancellation? Only in words: "substituting … then cancelling terms". No algebra is shown in the Erratum or the patent.

HYP (our algebra, not in any source). This carries out exactly the substitution the Erratum names, using only printed equations.

  • Assumptions:
    • B = 0.
    • μ is constant.
    • 1/c² = εμ. This is implied by the Erratum calling "εμĖᴸ + ∇C + μJᴸ = 0" "Eq. (21) … with ∇ × B = 0".
  • Eq. (21) with B = 0 gives μJ = −εμĖ − ∇C, so J·E = −(ε/2)∂(E²)/∂t − E·∇C/μ.
  • Eq. (22) gives ρC/(εμ) = C(∇·E + ∂C/∂t)/μ.
  • Also, ∇·(CE/μ) = (E·∇C + C∇·E)/μ.
  • Inserting these into Eq. (44):
    • With the energy density written εE²/2 (as in patent EQN. 14 and HL2019 p. 116 text "4πr²(εE²/2)"), every term cancels and Eq. (44) reduces to 0 = 0. This matches the patent's "identity, 0=0".
    • With Eq. (44) exactly as printed (εE²), a residual (ε/2)∂(E²)/∂t = 0 is left over. This is one more sign the printed "εE²" is a typo (also flagged in REDO_SLW.md as ASSUMPTION).
  • Interpretation (HYP). The Joule term is not shown to vanish. It is re-expressed through Eq. (21) as field-energy change −∂(εE²/2)/∂t plus a C-coupling term −E·∇C/μ, which then cancel against the other terms. That is Hively's "C, E, and J exchange energy" picture: conduction work is traded with the E and C fields rather than lost.
  • Caveat (HYP). Eq. (44) was built from Eqs. (21)–(22). Substituting them back must give an identity for any fields that satisfy them. The same happens with the classical Poynting theorem, yet classical TEM waves in conductors do attenuate. So the cancellation alone does not show that σE² heating is absent or replenished. It shows the balance is self-consistent. Where the replenishing energy comes from (the ρC/(εμ) "power source", or the C field) is not quantified in any Hively source. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis
