Contact current measurement on a polarizing rod: is an ammeter / Fluke worth it?

Is an ammeter or Fluke worth it on a floating polarizing rod? Induced currents under S1 literal / S1 capped / S2 plus one CLASSICAL-TEM reference column, meter specs, contact loading, least-perturbing options and E5/H10 detectability.

SAFETY: rods, probes, meters and TinySAs stay outside the oven cavity. Loose metal in a running oven can arc and damage the magnetron.

Short answer: no. A Fluke in current mode cannot read RF current at 433.59 MHz to 2.45 GHz at any amplitude: its AC current band stops at 2 kHz (87V) or 500 Hz (117) (FACT, Fluke specs). Wiring it across both rod ends also closes a loop and destroys the thing being measured (the floating half-wave rod). Use non-contact probes. If contact is pursued anyway, the least-perturbing option is a clamp-type RF current probe at 433.59 MHz only (Fischer F-65 class, which is not really contact); for true contact, a split rod with a small sense resistor and a fiber/resistive-lead detector, accepting that it becomes a dipole with a feed gap.

1. Induced current in one floating half-wave rod

What the SLW sources give: US 9,306,527 has J = sigma E (col. 5 l. ~53, col. 6 l. ~14) and the no-skin-effect claim (col. 6 ll. ~6-12). HL2019 Eq. 44 (p. 117) has J.E. M&W Eq. 7 (EPL 59, 514, p. 516) gives Joule power per length, with the internal field taken equal to E0 cos phi. Keyport US 12,525,711 gives nothing for receiving rods. None of them gives a current formula or a current distribution for a floating rod (FACT: not found). So everything below is derived from the models' own quantities, plus labelled assumptions:

  • S1 literal: I = sigma E A (uniform J = sigma E over the cross-section, internal = external field: HYP). A floating rod must have I = 0 at its ends (charge continuity, classical), so a uniform current is self-inconsistent at the ends. It also gives amperes to tens of kA, far more than the incident flux can supply: unphysical.
  • S1 capped: P = S pi a^2 (1 - e^-4.4) (M&W <P/A> = <S>, Eq. 9 kappa, HYP), R = L/(sigma A) (no skin effect: Hively's claim, used as HYP). Current: I = sqrt(P/R) if uniform (ASSUMPTION), or peak I0 = sqrt(2P/R) for I0 cos(kz) (ASSUMPTION, classical half-wave shape). Because R is milliohms, even nanowatts give sub-mA to mA currents.
  • S2 surface charge: I = omega eps0 E A (report Model S2, HYP). Material-independent.
  • CLASSICAL-TEM reference (one column): short-circuited half-wave rod parallel to E (i.e. perpendicular to propagation): I0 = E (lambda/pi)/|73.1 + j42.5| (effective length lambda/pi and Z_in, Balanis, Antenna Theory 4e, Sec. 4.6, Eq. 4-93a). This is the M&W "no attenuation" orientation, not the SLW one. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

E_rms = sqrt(Z0 S) (HYP map for SLW; identical for a classical plane wave). Scenarios: (a) and (b) are SLW flux at 2.45 GHz (HYP), and HYP flux only at 433.59 / 1296 MHz (the oven emits only 2.45 GHz). Hub drive: +2 dBm (TinySA + Zeenko, PHYSICS_LOCK.md:39; the HackRF power figure is still MISSING from the hub), at an assumed 1 m and 3 m.

Fluke verdict (FACT, Fluke specs)

  • Fluke 87V (Fluke 83V/87V detailed spec sheet): AC current 45 Hz to 2 kHz; 600.0 uA range, 0.1 uA resolution; burden 100 uV/uA (about 100 ohm in series); mA ranges 1.8 mV/mA; AC readings valid from 3 % of range (about 18 uA minimum on 600 uA); volts input 10 Mohm || < 100 pF; "in an RF field of 3 V/m total accuracy = specified accuracy" (EMC line).
  • Fluke 117 (fluke.com spec page, 114-117 datasheet): AC amps 45 Hz to 500 Hz, 6.000 A range, 1 mA resolution; burden 2 mV/A; volts AC > 5 Mohm < 100 pF.
  • 433.59 MHz is about 2e5 times above the 87V's top current frequency, and 2.45 GHz about 1e6 times. A DMM cannot read RF current at these frequencies regardless of amplitude (FACT from the spec). Any reading it shows is unspecified: rectification, pickup, or offset.
  • Wiring the meter across both ends closes a loop (rod + two leads + shunt). The structure is no longer a floating half-wave rod; it becomes a loop/folded antenna with different resonances and current distribution (FACT, classical).

  • Lead inductance: Rosa/Grover straight-wire formula L = (mu0 l/2pi)[ln(2l/r) - 3/4] (classical; Grover, Inductance Calculations), giving 1.51 uH for 1 m of 0.5 mm-radius wire: matches the ~1 uH/m rule of thumb.
  • Rod end impedance: classical half-wave end-feed values of about 2-5 kohm (AA5TB, "The End Fed Half Wave Antenna", practitioner source; depends on wire thickness). Treated here as a HYP range for our fat rods; no textbook value found for this geometry.
  • Reading: the Fluke's < 100 pF input is 0.65-3.7 ohm against a kilohm end impedance, i.e. effectively a short at the rod end. The 1 m leads are 1.5-8 wavelengths long, so the lumped picture fails (lumped elements need dimensions << lambda; Pozar, Microwave Engineering, Ch. 2). The leads become resonant antennas themselves. In current mode the 87V adds about 100 ohm (uA range) in series: larger than the rod's classical R_rad of 73 ohm (Balanis Eq. 4-93).

Least-perturbing contact options:

  • (i) High-Z active probe: the Tektronix TAP2500 (FACT, Tek datasheet: >= 2.5 GHz, <= 0.8 pF, 40 kohm, TekVPI; needs a TekVPI scope such as DPO4000/7000). 0.8 pF is still 81-459 ohm, about 0.02-0.2 of the end impedance: heavy loading and detuning. It also needs a ground reference (a second conductor) and a multi-GHz scope. Price: not verified.
  • (ii) Clamp-on RF current probe: the Tek CT6 does not fit (0.8 mm wire max, <= 2 GHz typical). Fischer F-65 (FACT, fischercc.com / Avalon listing): 10 kHz-1 GHz, 32 mm aperture (fits a 1/8 in rod), transfer impedance 1 ohm (1 MHz-1 GHz). 433.59 MHz only; no verified clamp probe covers 1296 or 2450 MHz with a 1/8 in window. Sensitivity at the TinySA floor: I_min ~ 0.56 uA (computed, ASSUMPTION floor). Caveat (HYP): a 71 mm OD ferrite/metal ring around the centre of a 346 mm resonant rod is itself a sizeable perturbation, even though Fischer describes "virtually no loading of the circuit".
  • (iii) Split rod + series sense resistor at the centre (e.g. 1 ohm << 73 ohm R_rad, classical), read by a zero-bias Schottky detector with resistive (high-R) leads or a fiber-coupled readout. Power in 1 ohm: -90 dBm at 1 uA, -50 dBm at 100 uA, -30 dBm at 1 mA (computed). A diode detector needs roughly -50 dBm or more (ASSUMPTION, typical class, not verified): it would see S1-capped mA currents, but not S2 nA. Splitting the rod makes it a dipole with a feed gap, exactly what M&W's passive rods are not (HL2019 Appendix D, p. 124, describes M&W's array as "half-wavelength, linear, electric-dipole antennas"; M&W treat them as continuous wires, p. 516).
  • (iv) RF thermocouple / thermal converter in series: needs a split rod too, and a heater drawing typically mA to tens of mA for a usable reading (ASSUMPTION, class, no product verified). It cannot see uA to nA.
  • Least perturbing: for real galvanic contact, (iii) with a small resistor and fiber readout; at 433.59 MHz the F-65 clamp (ii) perturbs less and needs no cut. Overall, the non-contact near-field probe (Sec. 5) is least perturbing of all.

3. Comparison with the bifilar coil into 50 ohm

  • The coil into the TinySA is a defined matched load: I = sqrt(P/50) (FACT identity). -40 dBm is 44.7 uA, -60 dBm 4.5 uA, -80 dBm 0.45 uA, -100 dBm 45 nA. Dan's actual coil readings (dBm) are not in the hub, so the coil current itself is unknown to us.
  • A floating rod has no load; its current is set by the absorption model. Under the SLW models (HYP) the rod current ranges from nA (S2) to mA (S1 capped) for the same flux, so it could be anywhere from well below to well above a typical coil reading. Without an SLW coupling formula for either the coil or the rod, the comparison is undefined. It becomes defined only if Dan supplies the coil's dBm and we pick a rod model.
  • CLASSICAL-TEM one line: at the FDA-limit door flux, a matched lambda/2 dipole delivers <= 98 mW (I ~ 37 mA into 73 ohm; A_e = 1.64 lambda^2/4pi, Balanis Sec. 2.15), while a shorted floating rod parallel to E carries I0 ~ 63 mA: the same ballpark classically. transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

4. Recommendation

Non-contact. A DMM is out on bandwidth alone (FACT). Contact methods all rebuild the object (loop, dipole-with-gap, or kilohm-end loading). Contact is worth doing only as a separate, labelled "modified-rod" experiment. The least-perturbing contact option is (iii) a split rod with a 1 ohm resistor and fiber/resistive-lead detector, or, at 433.59 MHz only, the F-65 clamp. Keep the unmodified rods for the M&W angle test.

5. Non-contact E5 / H10 near a rod (inside its own cage)

Cage rule: the cage strips TEM noise (FACT, classical). That SLW passes the wall is HYP (patent col. 2 ll. 65-67). No cable crosses a wall; battery TinySA inside. Per Hively, a TEM-type probe does not pick up the SLW itself ("TEM antennas detect only waves that produce a circulating current", HL2019 p. 116), so inside the cage the E5/H10 should see only the rod's own local field (HYP inference from a FACT quote). transverse-EM reasoning, NOT applicable under the SLW hypothesis

Classical near-field relations (CLASSICAL, quasi-static, valid for d << lambda/2pi):

  • H beside the centre: H = I0/(2 pi rho), with rho = a + gap (Ampere; Jackson 3e Sec. 5.3).
  • E off the end: end line-charge density |q| = I0/c for I0 cos(kz) (continuity). Field on axis at distance d from a semi-infinite uniform line charge: E = q/(4 pi eps0 d) = Z0 I0/(4 pi d) ~ 30 I0/d. This is an elementary Coulomb integral derived here, with an approximation (charge density taken as uniform near the end); not a formula quoted from a source.
  • Probe factors: Tekbox TBPS01 manual V2.2 (E5: dBm at 1 V/m; H10: dBm at 1 uT), digitized in the hub (physics-constants-fx1.json, FACT/DIGITIZED), log-f interpolated. E5: -58.4 / -49.0 / -44.7 dBm at 433.59 / 1296 / 2450 MHz; H10: -7.1 / -4.5 / -9.8 dBm. If Dan's E5/H10 are not Tekbox TBPS01: unknown, must be calibrated. The factors are from uniform-field calibration; near a rod the field varies across the probe (ASSUMPTION, several dB error). Peak vs rms is not stated by Tekbox (ASSUMPTION, +/-3 dB).
  • TinySA Ultra floor: -102 dBm at 30 kHz RBW (at 30 MHz, FACT spec). Scaled to 3 kHz RBW: -112 dBm (ASSUMPTION, thermal scaling). Sensitivity is about 10 dB lower above 2.5 GHz (FACT, tinysa.org Ultra-mode page), applied conservatively at 2.45 GHz: -102 dBm. FACT, same page: in ULTRA mode above ~750 MHz the LO leaks from the RF input "up to -10 dBm", which supports the two-TinySA coupling alternative (b) in the cage-coil section.

  • Verdict: in the cage, if S1 capped (or the classical reference) holds, the rod's local field is easily detectable (-50 to -20 dBm) in every leakage and hub-drive case. If S2 holds, it is not detectable except at the FDA-limit flux (-75 to -46 dBm). Near an open oven door (no cage) the E5 would read the oven's own field directly (137 V/m is about -2 dBm on an E5 at 2.45 GHz, computed), so the rod's contribution must be separated by the angle sweep, not by level.
  • Key discriminator (angle): SLW/M&W predicts maximum rod response at phi = 0/180 (rod along propagation) and cos^2 phi. The CLASSICAL-TEM rod response peaks when the rod is parallel to E (for horizontal polarization, phi = 90/270 relative to propagation). Both are 180-degree periodic but offset by 90 degrees, so the phase of the E5/H10-vs-phi curve tells which mechanism is at work (HYP on SLW, FACT classical).
