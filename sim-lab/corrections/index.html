Corrections log: charge-relaxation exponent (author-confirmed)

The transient in Hively & Loebl 2019 and in US 9,306,527 is printed with ε and σ swapped. The author confirmed the correct form on 2026-09-30. This page records the correction and where the hub uses it.

FACT, author-confirmed ← Simulation Lab catalogue

Author-confirmed, 2026-09-30. In a private email from Dr. Hively to Dan (2026-09-30), Dr. Lee Hively confirmed the form of the charge-relaxation transient:
"The form, E = E0 e^(-sigma t/epsilon), is the correct one."

What is corrected

  • TYPO as printed Hively & Loebl, Phys. Essays 32, 112 (2019), Eq. (A5), p. 122, prints E = E0 e^(−εt/σ).
  • TYPO as printed US 9,306,527 B1, col. 5, the exponent before EQN. 12, prints E = E0 exp(−εt/σ). In the patent the permittivity glyph is ∈.
  • FACT, author-confirmed Correct form: E = E0 exp(−σ t / ε), with relaxation time τ = ε / σ. ε and σ are swapped in the two printings above.
  • FACT, author-confirmed Hively & Land, IARD 2020, slide 60, prints E = Eo e^(−σt/ε). That slide is correct.

Units check

  • FACT σ is in S/m and ε in F/m, so ε/σ is in seconds and σt/ε is dimensionless. The swapped form εt/σ would have units of s² and cannot sit in an exponent.
  • FACT Arithmetic, τ = ε/σ: seawater with εr = 80 and σ = 4 S/m gives 80 × 8.854×10⁻¹² / 4 = 1.8×10⁻¹⁰ s. The hub's submarine survey gets 1.51×10⁻¹⁰ s with its seawater model at 60 Hz (σ = 4.29 S/m). Copper, ε0/σ with σ = 5.8×10⁷ S/m, gives 1.5×10⁻¹⁹ s, matching the ∼10⁻¹⁹ s the 2019 paper states beside Eq. (A5).
  • FACT This is a transient in time (charge relaxation). It is not a path loss over distance, and it does not by itself give an SLW attenuation constant. Under the SLW hypothesis the only loss term the hub uses is the Joule term σE∥² (or none, under the 2022 Erratum toggle).
