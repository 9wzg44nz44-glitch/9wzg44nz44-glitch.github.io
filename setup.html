How to set up the Hively replication on the bench

What you are building

Two mesh Faraday tents face each other on a wooden/plastic rail. The transmitter (TX) tent holds the stub + skirt and the signal generator. The receiver (RX) tent holds the folded bifilar pancake and the TinySA Ultra 406, which you read through the see-through mesh. No coaxial cable (coax) between tents if you can help it — a cable is a leak.

Transmitter (TX)

RG-405/U coaxial stub, one-tenth of a wavelength (λ/10) = 23.1 mm (one-quarter wavelength, λ/4 = 57.8 mm, as a transverse-electromagnetic (TEM) style control). Skirt 57.8 mm. Generator inside the TX tent. Licensed 23 cm; milliwatts.

Cage

RadioScreen Mesh (Less EMF #1124-0, nickel over copper on polyester, 130 threads per inch (TPI), 4 ft × 54 in). Two tents from one piece.

Receiver (RX)

One strand folded in half, outside diameter (OD) ≈ 35 mm, AWG 22 or 24. TinySA Ultra 406 in spectrum-analyzer (SA) / Ultra mode inside the RX tent.

The Cricket / Cricut sticky gummy board

A Cricut cutting mat (Cricket mat, gummy board) is the winding jig: each turn sticks, so 22/24 AWG magnet wire cannot spring off and knot. You are not cutting anything and you are not putting the coil in the machine.

Which mat. Green StandardGrip. Blue LightGrip if a new green mat wants to peel enamel. Skip purple StrongGrip.
  1. Green 12×12 mat on corrugated cardboard.
  2. If brand-new and grabby, press a cotton T-shirt once and peel — tacky, not a glue trap.
  3. Mask everything except a ~50 mm circle with parchment.
  4. Draw inside diameter (ID) 6 mm and outside diameter (OD) 35 mm (0.15 λ at 1296 MHz). Optional 58 mm (0.25 λ).
  5. Plastic push-pin or nylon screw through the center into the cardboard. Pull any steel pin before radio-frequency (RF) tests.
  6. One spool in a mug to the side so it can rotate. You only need one spool now.

Fold-in-half winding (no hub solder)

Cut one piece of magnet wire about 2.2 m (that is ~1.0 m per leg plus extra for leads). Fold it in half. The fold is the hub — it is the series-opposing connection. The two free ends are the radio-frequency terminals. You never scrape or solder the center.

  1. Measure 2.2 m. Mark the midpoint. Fold with a finger-radius, not a fingernail crease.
  2. Check the two free ends with a digital multimeter (DMM) before you wind (see next section). If it is already open, the fold broke the copper — cut a new piece.
  3. Tape the pair flat for 2 cm just outside the fold. No braid. A light twist (~1 / 3 cm) only if the pair splits; log it.
  4. Park the fold at the 6 mm hub on the gummy mat. Stick the first pair-turn. Wind outward, both legs at once, one layer, no daylight between turns. Count pair-turns.
  5. Stop at 35 ± 1 mm. Photo against a ruler. Leave ~80 mm free ends.
  6. Stop. Photograph the pancake still on the mat, ruler in frame. Then lift it off as its own procedure — lift off the sticky mat — so the fold does not spring open.

The fold method makes series-opposing only. For an aiding / transverse-electromagnetic-loop control, wind a second pancake as a single-filar of similar OD, or two wires paralleled. Label them.

Lift the fold-in-half pancake off the Cricut StandardGrip mat

Winding is done. The gummy board did its job: 22/24 AWG stayed planar and did not knot. Now you have to get the coil off the mat without spring-out, enamel cracks, or opening the hub fold. This is a handling step, not a new winding method.

Do not peel from a free end or from the fold. A yanked lead will telescope the spiral; a yanked fold can crack copper the digital multimeter already said was continuous.
  1. Leave the mat on the cardboard. Do not flex the mat first — bending the gummy board stores spring that dumps into the pancake.
  2. Slide a plastic card (old gift card, scraper, or Cricut spatula) under a mid-radius pair-turn, not under the hub and not under the outer free ends. Keep the card almost parallel to the mat.
  3. Work around the spiral in short arcs. The pair should release as a flat disk. If a turn jumps, park it with a fingertip and resume from the opposite side — do not chase it with tweezers.
  4. When the disk is free, lift it as a plate: two fingers under opposite outer turns, fold still a gentle U. Do not open the fold, do not flatten it into a crease, do not let the two legs scissor.
  5. Isopropyl alcohol (IPA) on a lint-free cloth: wipe adhesive residue off the enamel. Do not soak the fold. Do not scrape enamel on the spiral or the hub — scrape only the two free ends, and only after the pancake is on its fixture.
  6. Digital-multimeter continuity again, free-end to free-end, leads zeroed. Same pass band as the table below. Flex the fold gently; OL (open circuit) means rewind. Photograph the meter with the pancake in frame. That photo is the “survived lift-off” check.

If the mat still grabs, a drop of IPA at the card edge can release a turn. Wait for the alcohol to flash off before the next radio-frequency run so you are not measuring wet polyester adhesive.

Expected resistance and open-circuit check

Copper resistivity at 20 °C: AWG 22 ≈ 0.0530 Ω/m, AWG 24 ≈ 0.0842 Ω/m. Current walks the entire 2.2 m (out one leg, through the fold, back the other).

  1. Zero the digital multimeter by touching the probes together. Write that offset down (~0.1–0.3 Ω is common on cheap leads).
  2. Scrape 5 mm of enamel off each free end. Tin if you like.
  3. Measure free-end to free-end. Subtract the lead offset. Compare to the table.
  4. Flex the fold gently. The reading must not jump to OL (open circuit). If it does, the copper is cracked — rewind with a larger bend radius.
  5. Photograph the digital multimeter. That photo is the error check that the fold did not open-circuit the wire.

A digital multimeter cannot see a hairline enamel puncture mid-spiral. It can catch the failure that matters for fold-in-half: a fractured U-turn. Continuity after winding, again after mounting, and again after the first radio-frequency run.

TinySA Ultra 406 and impedance — skip the matching resistor on the detector

The TinySA Ultra+ ZS406 (4″ screen, SubMiniature version A (SMA) connector, 50 ohm) is a 2-in-1: spectrum analyzer and signal generator. The spectrum-analyzer input is specified 50 Ω when internal attenuation is ≥ 10 dB. Frequency: 100 kilohertz (kHz)–900 MHz normal, Ultra mode to ~5.4–6 GHz — so 1296 MHz receive is in Ultra mode, which is fine.

Generator caveat. Clean sine output is only specified to ~800–900 MHz. At 1296 MHz the Ultra 406 generator is in the square-wave / mixer region (harmonics and spurs). If it is the transmitter source, enable Ultra, add a 23 cm bandpass or low-pass after the SMA connector, log that it is not a clean sine, and stay licensed. A 23 cm transverter or brick is the cleaner transmitter; the TinySA is the better detector.

Do not put a 47–51 Ω resistor in series with the bifilar on the detector. The pancake is ~0.2 Ω of copper. A 51 Ω series resistor in front of a 50 Ω TinySA is a ~6 dB (factor-of-four in power) pad that throws away the already-tiny detected voltage. Hively’s “match the coil resistance to the source” is a transmit remark for a coil whose R was designed near 50 Ω. This 35 mm fold-in-half article is not that coil.

Keep the SMA jumper from pancake to TinySA under 10 cm. Ferrite on that jumper if you see cable-pickup that dummy-loading does not kill.

Mount the pancake on a semi-permanent non-metallic fixture

The coil does not live on the Cricut mat for radio-frequency work, and it should not sit loose in the mesh tent. Give it a rigid, non-metallic plate that keeps the pancake planar, carries a short SMA pigtail, and can be lifted in and out of the RadioScreen tent without crushing seams.

  1. Plate. Acrylic, HDPE, plywood, or a 3D-printed PLA/PETG disk/rectangle larger than 35 mm (something like 80–100 mm across). No copper-clad, no steel washers in the RF path. The pancake stays one layer, face-on, not warped into a dish.
  2. Hold-downs. Nylon screws, plastic clips, Kapton, or hot-glue dots at a few outer points. Do not cinch steel hardware through the hub. Do not use the fold as a mounting loop.
  3. SMA pigtail. Shortest practical jumper from the two scraped free ends to the TinySA Ultra 406 — under 10 cm. Strain-relieve the coax on the plate so a tug hits the fixture, not the enamel. No 51 Ω series resistor on this detector (see TinySA section).
  4. TinySA on the same sled (optional but tidy). Velcro or a plastic cradle so the 406 and the pancake move as one assembly. Screen still faces a mesh wall so you can read it from outside.
  5. Inside the tent. Slide the fixture in through the flap; do not stuff it through a seam. The mesh must close with a 50 mm overlap. The plate must not tent the cloth or pinch a corner. The fixture is removable: out for continuity checks, packing, and photos; in for sweeps.
  6. Fasteners in the cage. Nylon/plastic only on the sled. Clothes-pins, wood battens, or plastic clamps for the flap. No steel binder-skeleton sitting next to the pancake if you can help it — keep ferrous hardware off the RF path.

Continuity one more time after the SMA is on the plate. Log “pancake on fixture, pigtail < 10 cm, nylon hardware.” Photograph the assembly next to a ruler before the first sealed run.

Matching TX fixture (stub + skirt + generator)

Do the same idea on the transmitter side so the two tents are portable twins, not a permanent bench sculpture. The TX tent still holds the stub + skirt and the generator; they just ride a second non-metallic sled.

  1. Non-metallic plate or small wood block sized for the RG-405 stub, the 57.8 mm skirt, and the generator (TinySA in generator mode, brick, or transverter). Nylon/plastic fasteners only in the RF path.
  2. Stub tip remains the range origin: when you measure tent-to-tent distance, it is still stub tip to pancake face. Mark that axis on the rail.
  3. Short SMA / semi-rigid on the generator. If the Ultra 406 is the source: 23 cm filter, log square-wave / mixer-region output, optional 47 Ω only at the transmitter SMA. Licensed milliwatts; no magnetron.
  4. Slide the TX fixture into its RadioScreen tent the same way as the RX sled: flap open, fixture in, overlap closed, no crushed seams, no USB cable out on a sealed run.
  5. The TX fixture is removable for dummy-load swaps, photos of the 23.1 mm stub, and packing. Do not hard-tape the generator to the mesh.

RadioScreen mesh Faraday cages (Less EMF)

Product: RadioScreen Mesh, Less EMF (electromagnetic field products) part 1124-0. Nickel over copper on polyester (23.4% copper, 10.9% nickel, 65.7% polyester), 130 threads/inch, 0.09 mm, 54 in wide. Dan’s piece: 4 feet, $83.80. Lab-quoted attenuation: about 50–60 dB from 10 MHz–1 GHz, still >25 dB into the fifth-generation (5G) cellular bands. Surface resistivity < 0.1 Ω/sq. See-through, which is why the TinySA display can be read from outside.

Honest dynamic range. 60 dB is not Hively’s 25,000 dB copper-pipe claim. Mesh is a Faraday screen: plenty to knock transverse-electromagnetic leakage down for a first look, not a skin-depth fortress. Publish it as a mesh-cage test. A later soldered-copper can is a different (stronger) article. If a tone survives 60 dB of mesh and the dummy-load / transverse-electromagnetic-antenna controls, that is still worth ink — just do not call the mesh 2885 skin depths.

Best practices for this cloth

  1. Two tents from 4 ft × 54 in. Cut two ~2 ft lengths. Each wraps a cardboard or wood frame (~30×30×30 cm is enough for a TinySA + pancake or generator + stub). Overlap the closing flap by at least 50 mm.
  2. Seams, not the weave, are the leak. 130 threads-per-inch openings are ~0.2 mm (≪ λ/100 at 1296 MHz). The closing flap is a removable seal: conductive hook-and-loop, binder clips on a wood/plastic batten, or magnets on a non-metallic strip — 50 mm overlap, then clamp. Do not make the flap a permanent 3M 1181 / conductive-fabric-tape weld; you need to open this tent every run and pack it. Clothes-pins or wood battens still count. No steel wool “maybe.”
  3. You cannot easily solder this mesh (polyester core). Clamp, sew with conductive thread, or use removable conductive hook-and-loop. Grounding: Less EMF wants the mesh grounded for household use. For two experiment cages, bond each tent to itself; do not run a long ground wire between tents (that wire is a dipole). Optional single-point earth, logged.
  4. No cables out during a “sealed” run. A Universal Serial Bus Type-C (USB-C) cable to a laptop is an antenna. Run the TinySA on battery. No charging cable. If you must have a cable, it is a slotted / leaky config, not sealed.
  5. Instruments inside. Transmitter generator in the transmitter tent (contains its own transverse-electromagnetic leakage). TinySA in the receiver tent, screen facing a mesh wall so you can read it. The TinySA’s local oscillator also radiates — that is now inside the detector cage. Log “spectrum analyzer inside” as the default; one control run with the TinySA outside and a short feedthrough tells you whether the local oscillator (LO) is talking to the pancake.
  6. Looking through the mesh is allowed and is the point of RadioScreen. Don’t press your face into a gap. Don’t unzip to peek “just for a second” mid-sweep.
  7. Corners and floors. Mesh on the bench without a floor is an open box. Wrap five or six sides. A cardboard carton as the skeleton, mesh around it, copper-tape the joins.
  8. Roll, don’t crease leftover cloth (Less EMF: permanent creasing). Keep it dry; wipe, don’t launder.

Order of first measurements

  1. Stub 23.1 mm, skirt 57.8 mm, generator in the TX tent. TinySA in the RX tent, Ultra mode, atten ≥ 10 dB, pancake on the shortest SMA.
  2. License, identify, milliwatts (or TinySA generator with a 23 cm filter). Do not camp on 1296.100.
  3. Control matrix: open (flap up), slotted (flap half-lapped), sealed (50 mm overlap, clamped / hook-and-loop — not a permanent copper-tape weld), dummy_load (SMA dummy instead of stub), 90deg, tem_negative (small loop in place of the pancake), plus the single-filar control coil. comma-separated values (CSV) log on Lab.
Decision rule. A leftover tone that dies when you dummy-load the transmitter is leakage or pickup. A leftover tone that beats the TEM-loop and single-filar floors, with both flaps sealed and no USB cables, is the result you publish — whatever it is. Quote mesh attenuation in decibels, not copper skin depths.

Packing and shipping: Lady Lake, FL then Middletown, RI

The whole experiment should pack as one kit: both RadioScreen tents, TX fixture, RX/coil fixture, TinySA Ultra 406, generator, cables, and clips. First destination is bench time in Lady Lake, Florida; later the same bags go to a demo in Middletown, Rhode Island. Build so you can zip it, fly or drive it, and put the rail back together without rewinding the pancake.

  1. Mesh. Open the tents, pull the fixtures out, then roll the RadioScreen (Less EMF: do not crease — a fold is a permanent leak line). Slip each roll into a labeled bag or tube. Keep leftover cloth rolled with the tents.
  2. Pancake. Leave it on its rigid plate. Nest the plate in foam (closed-cell or tool-case pick-and-pluck) so nothing can spring the fold or mash the OD. Do not put the coil back on the Cricut mat for travel; the mat is a winding jig, not a shipping tray.
  3. TinySA Ultra 406 in its own case. Cap the SMA. No USB cables left attached — not to the TinySA, not dangling from a power bank, not “already plugged in for the next site.” Same rule for the generator. A small pick-and-pluck case (Pelican 1200 class; see shopping list) holds the RX plate in foam plus the flap clips if you want one outer shell.
  4. TX sled. Stub + skirt stay on their plate if the solder joints are happy; foam around the 23.1 mm stub so it cannot get bent. Dummy load rides in the same bag.
  5. Labeled bags. Suggested split: RX-coil+fixture, TX-stub+gen, mesh-tents, TinySA-406, cables+clips, DMM+IPA+card. Print a paper inventory in the outer case.
  6. Flap hardware in the clips bag: conductive hook-and-loop, binder clips, wood/plastic battens, magnets for removable seals. Clothes-pins. Measuring tape for tent-to-tent r. The Cricut StandardGrip mat only if you expect to rewind; otherwise leave it home.
Removable flap seals travel; a copper-tape weld does not. Do not pack by wrapping the tents in 3M 1181 or conductive fabric tape “so they stay shut.”

  • RX pancake still on rigid plate, in foam; DMM photo from last lift/mount in the log
  • TX fixture + stub/skirt + dummy load padded
  • TinySA Ultra 406 in case, SMA capped, battery charged, USB disconnected
  • Generator / filter / attenuators bagged and labeled
