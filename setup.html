Setup · 1.3 GHz (1296 MHz) · not 8 GHz · not 1.3 Hz

How to set up the Hively replication on the bench

A walk-through for Dan’s first article: wind a series-opposing bifilar pancake on a Cricut-style sticky “gummy” mat so 22 or 24 AWG magnet wire stays put, then stack the RG-405 stub, skirt balun, copper cage, and licensed 23 cm source. Numbers match the lab protocol and coil calculator.

This is 1.3 GHz (23 cm amateur band, build on 1296.000 MHz). 1.3 Hz would be audio / near-DC and is a different experiment. Licensed amateur radio only. No magnetron.

What you are building

Two copper Faraday cans face each other on a wooden/plastic rail. Inside each can: an RG-405/U feed, a λ/4 skirt balun, and either a short exposed-center stub (TX) or the bifilar pancake (RX). The only intended hole is the coax outer soldered 360° into an end cap.

TX

RG-405 stub λ/10 = 23.1 mm first article (λ/4 = 57.8 mm as TEM-ish control). Skirt balun 57.8 mm back along the cable. Licensed 23 cm source, milliwatts to start.

Cage

Copper pipe + end caps, seams soldered. Wall ≥ 1 mm is already hundreds of skin depths at 1.3 GHz. Outer of RG-405 soldered 360° to the cap.

RX

Bifilar pancake, OD ≈ 35 mm (0.15 λ), AWG 22 or 24, series-opposing. Face-on, on axis. Aiding twin is the coil control.

The Cricket / Cricut sticky gummy board

A Cricut cutting mat (often nicknamed a Cricket mat or gummy board) is a plastic sheet with a tacky adhesive. For a pancake coil it is a winding jig: each turn sticks where you put it, so 22/24 AWG cannot spring off and knot itself. You are not cutting anything. You are not putting the coil in the Cricut machine.

Which mat. Green StandardGrip is the one to use. Blue LightGrip if a new green mat is so sticky it wants to peel enamel. Skip purple StrongGrip — that adhesive is for chipboard and leather, and it will fight you when you lift the finished pancake.

Prepare the jig (5 minutes)

  1. Lay a 12×12 (or 12×24) green StandardGrip mat face-up on a piece of corrugated cardboard. The cardboard is the backup so a center pin has something to bite.
  2. If the mat is brand-new and grabby, press a clean cotton T-shirt onto it once and peel. Crafters do this to knock a new mat down from “glue trap” to “tacky.” You want tacky.
  3. Cover everything except a ~50 mm circle in the middle with baking parchment or the mat’s own clear film, taped at the edges. Magnet wire that wanders onto unused adhesive is how tangles start.
  4. Draw two circles on the exposed gummy with a compass and a fine marker: ID 6 mm and OD 35 mm (0.15 λ at 1296 MHz). That is the first-article size from the lab protocol. A 58 mm (0.25 λ) circle is the optional larger coil.
  5. Push a plastic push-pin, wooden skewer, or nylon 4-40 through the exact center, into the cardboard. This is the hub. Do not use a steel nail as a permanent former — fine for marking, pull it before RF tests.
  6. Put spool A on the left in a mug or on a saucer so it can rotate. Spool B on the right. Opposite sides means the two wires meet at the hub without crossing behind your hands.

22 vs 24 AWG

Both work. 24 AWG is what the protocol starts with (pair pitch ~1.15 mm, ~12.5 pair-turns to 35 mm). 22 AWG is stiffer, less tangle-prone, slightly fewer turns. If the 24 wants to bird-nest even on the gummy mat, switch to 22 for article 1 and log it.

Buy two equal lengths, about 1.0 m each for the 35 mm coil (extra for leads). Two enamel colours (red + natural) if you can find them. Same colour: Sharpie-stripe wire A every 5 cm.

Winding the pancake

  1. Pair, don’t twist (first article). Lay A and B side-by-side so enamel touches. Tape them as a flat pair for the first 2 cm. A gentle twist (~1 turn per 3 cm) is allowed if the pair keeps splitting; photograph it and write it in the log. Do not braid.
  2. Inner leads. Leave ~80 mm of pair as the hub leads. Poke them down through the center hole (or tape them to the back of the cardboard). These two inner ends will later be soldered to each other for series-opposing.
  3. Stick the first pair-turn onto the gummy at the 6 mm ID, both wires in one layer, flat. Press lightly with a fingernail — you want them parked, not buried in adhesive.
  4. Wind outward as a flat spiral, both wires at once, like laying a two-lane road. Each new pair-turn should kiss the previous one with no overlap and no daylight you can see. The gummy holds the last turn while you bring in the next. Count pair-turns out loud (1, 2, 3…).
  5. Stop at the 35 mm mark. Caliper the OD. Photograph against a ruler. Leave ~80 mm of pair as outer leads.
  6. Lift. Slide a thin plastic card (old gift card) under the pancake, then peel the mat away. Do not yank. If a turn lifts, press it back on a scrap of parchment and tape the pancake to a PTFE / acrylic disc of the same OD.
  7. Residue. If the enamel looks dull with adhesive, wipe with isopropyl on a cloth. No acetone (some polyurethane enamels hate it). No sandpaper on the spiral — only scrape enamel at the four lead ends.

Wind a second coil identical except series-aiding (see next section). Label it AIDING CONTROL. Same mat, same AWG, same OD. That coil is how you prove the first one is actually opposing.

Cage, stub, and first measurements

After the pancake exists and passes QC, the rest of the first afternoon is plumbing and milliwatts. Full numbers live on Lab; this is the order of operations so the coil does not sit in a drawer.

  1. Strip RG-405: exposed center 23.1 mm (λ/10). Skirt tube 57.8 mm, soldered to the outer at the stub end, open at the back. Use the 1.3 GHz preset on Tools and the skirt calculator.
  2. Drill the copper cap snug on RG-405 outer (~2.2 mm). Stub + skirt inside the can. Solder the outer 360° on the outside of the cap. DMM: center-to-cap open, outer-to-cap 0 Ω.
  3. Mount the pancake on axis, plane perpendicular to the pipe axis, face toward the other can. Photograph.
  4. Licensed 1296 MHz source → bandpass → step attenuator (start 40–70 dB in) → cage. Identify. Do not camp on 1296.100.
  5. Run the control matrix on Lab before you get excited: open, slotted, sealed, dummy_load, 90deg, tem_negative, plus the aiding coil. Log CSV.
Decision rule. A leftover tone through a leak-hunted sealed can that dies when you dummy-load the feedthrough is leakage. A leftover tone that beats the TEM-antenna and aiding-coil floors, and survives cable reseating, is the result you publish — whatever it is.

Printable checklist

  • Green StandardGrip mat on cardboard, parchment mask, 6 mm / 35 mm circles, center pin
  • Two 1.0 m lengths, AWG 22 or 24, A marked
  • Spools left and right, pair side-by-side, inner leads through the hub
  • Flat spiral, one layer, stop at 35 ± 1 mm, photo with ruler
  • Peel with a plastic card; isopropyl if gummy residue
  • Inner-A soldered to inner-B; outer ends are terminals; leads < 15 mm
  • DMM + L vs aiding twin; label OPPOSING and AIDING
  • Stub 23.1 mm, skirt 57.8 mm, 360° cap fillet
  • License, ident, milliwatts, control matrix, CSV export
