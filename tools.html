Bifilar pancake, RG-405 stub, and sleeve balun

Merged from the Hively-style coil calculator and the RG-405 sleeve-balun page. Experiment A build is a close-fitting sleeve, not US 9,306,527 Fig. 2A skirt. All linear dimensions track 1/f. Default is the hub lab frequency, not 2.45 or 8 GHz.

Design aid only. A sleeve (bazooka) choke is ordinary RF practice; calling the stub an “SLW launcher” is the disputed part. Do not build the patent skirt. Verify with a VNA (S11, outer-conductor current) before any on-air use.

2. Transmitter: RG-405/U exposed center conductor

RG-405/U (0.086″ semi-rigid) fixed data:

  • Center conductor: 0.020 in (0.51 mm) silver-plated copper-clad steel
  • Dielectric (PTFE) OD ≈ 0.066 in (1.68 mm)
  • Outer conductor OD ≈ 0.0865 in (2.20 mm)

Hively-style notes often use λ/10–λ/4. Spherical wavefront originates near the tip. Short stub, not a magnetron probe.

3. Sleeve (bazooka choke), not a patent skirt

Conductive sleeve over the outer conductor, shorted at the antenna end, open at the far end. At electrical λ/4 the open end is a high common-mode impedance.

At 1.3 GHz the sleeve is ~6× longer than at 8 GHz. Length must track 1/f.

Balun construction (from the original page)

  1. Install SMA (or Type N) on the source end of RG-405/U in the ordinary way. No special balun connection at the connector.
  2. Strip outer copper and PTFE for the calculated stub length. Leave a clean, straight center conductor. Deburr.
  3. Cut thin-wall copper/brass tubing (or foil tube) slightly longer than the physical sleeve. ID must clear 0.086″ with an air gap or thin dielectric.
  4. Slide the sleeve to the dielectric cut-back plane. Solder the near end all around to the RG-405 outer. Leave the far end open — no solder bridges.
  5. Optional: thin PTFE or tape between coax and sleeve to lower VF (shorter physical length).
  6. Confirm choking: S11 and a field probe on the outer conductor at the design frequency.

5. Winding — fold first, fold is the center

  1. Cut one long strand of 22 or 24 AWG, about 2.2 m (not two separate wires).
  2. Fold it in half with a gentle U, not a crease. That fold is the center/hub (series-opposing). No hub solder. You never scrape the midpoint.
  3. Park the fold at the 6 mm hub on a Cricut / Cricket StandardGrip sticky mat.
  4. Wind both legs outward together as a pair to OD 35 mm. Same geometry as setup.html#fold.
  5. The two free ends are the RF terminals (TinySA center and ground). Do not cut the fold open.
  6. When a longitudinal E drives charge, current goes out one free end and returns on the other, so neighboring turns have opposite current and magnetic fields cancel. Net inductance ≈ 0.
Do not cut two wires and solder a hub. Do not parallel the two legs. Keep interconnects short. Bench protocol lives on setup.html#fold.
