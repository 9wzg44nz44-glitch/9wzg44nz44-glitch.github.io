Round 1: three sphere experiments and a microwave oven

Each experiment uses a transmitting sphere, a receiving sphere and a polarising-rod attenuator between them, with brass and W1 tool-steel rods cut to λ/2 for each band. The oven replaces the transmitting sphere at 2.45 GHz. We are testing hypotheses, not reporting detections.

Tests of hypotheses, not results. Mainstream electrodynamics treats free-space radio as transverse (TEM). A longitudinal or scalar component is a hypothesis we are testing, and nothing on these pages claims that scalar or longitudinal waves have been detected. Values marked proposal are our suggestions and are not measured or sourced. Draft v0.1, 4 Oct 2026, written by Grok Bot for Dan Britton.

What every bench shares

  1. Baselines first: VNA S11 and S21 with the rods out, then the rods in, using the VNA how-to; spectrum-analyzer noise floor with the transmitter off.
  2. Rod scan: turn the rod array through φ and fit T = exp(-κ cos²φ) (Monstein and Wesley form), for brass and then W1.
  3. Range: tape-measure r, then compare with the range finder estimates (a tape is the truth, the estimates are hypotheses).
  4. Joule loss: compare κ between materials and bands, and read the limits on the Joule-loss page.
  5. Fixture position (proposal): repeat the rod scan with the fixture near the transmitter, midway and near the receiver, logging TX S11 as a loading check, and compare κ by station. Monstein and Wesley do not say where their array sat. See the Joule-loss page.
  6. Log everything: grounding state of the cage, instrument settings and raw files in every row.
