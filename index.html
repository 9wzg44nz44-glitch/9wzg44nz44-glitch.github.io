Experiment A — 1296 MHz Faraday-cage · ¾″ sphere

First-article antenna is a K-Mac KB-1820 0.750″ (¾″) 3003 aluminum hollow sphere with an Amphenol 901-9875 SMA (SubMiniature version A) bulkhead — coax into the ball, outer conductor as shield — same feed philosophy as Experiment B, but at 1296.000 MHz / 23 cm (λ = 231.3 mm). Two RadioScreen mesh tents keep the Faraday-cage context. Electrically separate from Experiment B: do not mix the 433 MHz ball feed into this coax. Cricut sticky-mat + bifilar pancake + sleeve-stub are archived at setup.html#archive-bifilar — historical / optional control only, not the primary first article.

Sphere first; Cricket/bifilar/sleeve archived. KB-1820 ships with no hole — machine shop drills a clearance hole (and may conditionally tap a thick boss) for the SMA bulkhead. Preferred path is clearance + locknut/washer, not tapping the thin stamped wall. Copy-ready shop notes: setup.html#sphere-a · #sma-shop-a. Archived Cricut sticky-mat + bifilar + sleeve: setup.html#archive-bifilar (patent sleeve/skirt education kept inside that block).

Experiment B — 433.59 MHz balls + polarizer rods

Monstein and Wesley 2002 geometry: aluminium ball antennas, brass (then optional hysteresis) λ/2 rods as a polarizer. No balun of any type — not a sleeve, not a skirt, not a bazooka. Coax into the ball, outer conductor as shield (MW Fig. 1 / Amphenol 901-9875). Do not put Experiment A sleeve-build steps on this bench.

No balun. The 433.59 MHz sphere feed is MW “centrally fed, outer as shield.” Experiment A’s λ/4 sleeve lives on RG-405 at 1296 MHz, not on this ball. Keep the RF feeds electrically separate even if both antennas later ride in one Pelican as cargo.

What this bench is

TX ball → optional 3×3 rod cube at angle φ → RX ball. Polarizer test for the direction of E, not a Faraday-cage skin-depth test and not a bifilar pancake. 70 cm Technician-legal with a license. Educational; longitudinal E is not settled physics.

Experiment C — Faraday-caged SLW/SW → atomic 1S–2S optical readout

v1 draft (9 Sep 2026). Faraday-caged SLW/SW launcher, hydrogen or clock-atom 1S–2S readout, dual solar-blind PMT bandpass at 243 nm and 121.6 nm, TEM-null monitors, quench-control discriminator. Electrically and conceptually separate from A and B: the PMT is on the atomic optical arm, not the RF launcher.

8 GHz ≠ 10.2 eV. RF SLW is a modulator / gate in v1, not a direct 1S–2S photon. Frequency plan and pass/fail live on the deep page.

Claim under test

A Faraday-caged SLW/SW source transfers population on 1S–2S (or modulates that interval) with E⊥ and B below a pre-registered TEM budget at the atom.

Experiment F — Pure Scalar Wave + hydrogen forbidden transition

Experiment G — Undersea SLW link via hydrogen 2S tickled relaxation

Desk study (gate G0). A 1057.8 MHz transmitter in seawater would tickle trapped hydrogen 2S atoms, inside their own Faraday cage inside a steel hull, into giving off Lyman-α photons. The SLW is assumed (HYP) to pass the cage wall. Key answer (default, with Hively's Joule-loss term): a ~1 GHz SLW falls by a factor e every 1.45 cm of seawater, so the range is centimetres (5.72 cm at 1 W with today's lab 2S receiver; TEM-equal, the classical comparison, gives 4.59 cm). As in Experiment C's field dictionary, SLW has no eddy/skin loss but does have Joule loss σE∥², and SW has neither. His 2022 Erratum claims no resistive loss; the page keeps that only as a labelled toggle (metres at 1 W) and flags the conflict. Only a pool test with TEM controls (gate G2) can decide.

What it is

2S chain (243 nm pump, 2E1 background, Lamb-shift tickle, Lyman-α counting), seawater model (Meissner & Wentz 2004), steel hull and cage, link budget, detector comparison. Gates: G0 desk → G1 TEM bench → G2 pool mockup → G3 comms.

Shared field kit — one TX Pelican, one RX case

Planned end-state for taking both experiments into the field. Not a claim the cube has been built, ballasted, or dunked. Plastic Pelican = hydrostatic shell; RadioScreen mesh = Faraday. Sequential is the default. Full write-up with SKUs and anchors: kit.html.

In one sentence

One cubical watertight TX case (1296 ¾″ sphere sled and 433 TX sphere in separate foam wells, whole inner pack in RadioScreen). Second case for RX, usually lid open, mesh tent over the instruments so two TinySA screens stay readable. Not submerged. Pelican 1200 remains the small coil/TinySA travel case on setup.html#shop — it is not this hull.
