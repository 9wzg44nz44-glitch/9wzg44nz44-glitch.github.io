Lab · 1.3 GHz · not 8 GHz

Faraday-cage replication of Hively US 9,306,527 at 1.3 GHz

Different experiment (not this cage): Monstein–Wesley 2002 brass-rod polarizer — monstein.html.

Scale the IARD 2020 copper-enclosure idea down in frequency to the 23 cm amateur band, where a licensed operator can use a legal source. The hypothesis under test is whether a bifilar (or plasma) detector outside a copper box still sees a coherent tone when TEM leakage is beaten down by many skin depths. A null is a successful experiment.

HAM legal box — 23 cm (1240–1300 MHz). Transmitting in this band requires a valid amateur radio license and compliance with Part 97 (US) or your administration’s rules: identification, band edges, power, spurious emissions, and coordination. 1296 MHz is the weak-signal calling frequency — do not park a continuous unattended carrier there. Prefer a quiet segment, identify, and listen first. This lab uses a licensed 23 cm source into RG-405 — no magnetron, no microwave oven, no 8 GHz TWT as the primary source.

Classical TEM power attenuation through a wall is on the order of e−2t/δ (plus aperture leakage). If a detector still hears the tone through a sealed, many-δ box and the TEM-antenna negative control is quiet, you have an anomaly worth publishing. If it does not, that is also worth publishing.

Transmitter

Hively-style (primary for this lab)

RG-405 exposed center-conductor stub + sleeve (bazooka) balun, driven by a licensed 23 cm source. Goal of the sleeve: choke common-mode TEM on the outer shield (λ/4 shorted line → high-Z) so residual radiation is dominated by the stub current — without a large radiating skirt. US 9,306,527 Fig. 2A is the historical annular skirt; this lab builds a close-fitting sleeve. Whether that current is “irrotational SLW” is the hypothesis, not the construction step.

N–Z-style (optional companion)

Zimmerman QEX 2011: 7-inch stovepipe TM01, λcut ≈ 23.23 cm, 69 cm guide + 69 cm longitudinal probe, 5.8 cm choke collar, Type N + 3 dB pad, U-tube fluorescent detector. Same frequency family, different launch. Do not mix data sets without labeling Tx type.

Publishing checklist

  • License class, call sign, frequency, and that 1296 calling was not occupied by an unidentified carrier.
  • Photographs of stub length, sleeve length, coil polarity connection, box/mesh seams, pancake on its fixture, and DMM continuity after lift-off.
  • VNA S11 of stub+sleeve; outer-conductor current with sleeve on vs off.
  • Complete control matrix, not a highlight reel. Source-off and dummy-load rows.
  • TEM-antenna negative control at the same r and RBW.
  • Distance sweep (even 0.3–3 m) plotted; state whether it looks like 1/r or a leak resonance.
  • Box wall thickness in mm and in skin depths at the operating frequency (use the table).
  • Raw CSV plus instrument screenshots with timestamps.
  • A one-sentence claim that matches the data: “sealed − dummy ≥ X dB” or “no excess above dummy.”
  • Cite US 9,306,527, IARD 2020, and (if used) Zimmerman QEX 2011. State that SLW is not settled physics.

Safety

RF: 23 cm can cook eyes and fingers at surprisingly modest power in the near field. Know your ERP, use attenuators, keep people out of the stub’s near zone, and never look into an open waveguide. Soldering: flux fumes, lead (if any), hot copper boxes. Do not cook a sealed cage: a driven antenna inside a closed conducting box dissipates power in the walls and the feed. Watch temperature; do not apply oven-like power; provide a load path (dummy-load control exists for a reason). No magnetron. No modified microwave oven in this protocol. GFCI on mains instruments. Discharge bias supplies on plasma tubes before handling.
