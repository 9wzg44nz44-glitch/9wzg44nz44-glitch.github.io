Round 1 · 1.3 GHz · sphere transmitter, sphere receiver, polarising-rod attenuator

Experiment A · 1.3 GHz

The bench is two 3/4 in aluminium spheres (one transmitting, one receiving) on a non-metallic rail, with a 3 x 3 array of half-wave rods between them that can be turned through an angle φ. Round 1 adds the rod attenuator to Experiment A (older hub text says rods are Experiment B only; Dan's 4 Oct 2026 plan supersedes that), VNA S21 baselines, an automatic range estimate, and a Joule-loss analysis of the rod data.

Tests of hypotheses, not results. Mainstream electrodynamics treats free-space radio as transverse (TEM). A longitudinal or scalar component is a hypothesis we are testing, and nothing on these pages claims that scalar or longitudinal waves have been detected. Values marked proposal are our suggestions and are not measured or sourced. Draft v0.1, 4 Oct 2026, written by Grok Bot for Dan Britton.

2. Run order

  1. Inspect the rods (length, diameter, magnet and mass test) on the To-Do page. Do not start until the bags are labelled brass or W1.
  2. Calibrate the VNA in slot 1 (1100 to 1500 MHz); full SOLT with the TX and RX cables, then check that a THRU reads 0 dB (vna-howto.html).
  3. Noise floor: terminate both ports and record S21 with the settings you will use. Anything within 10 dB of that floor is not usable.
  4. S11 of each sphere at the SMA reference plane. Record the lowest dip and the value at 1296.000 MHz. Do not tune to chase a number; log it and compare after any change.
  5. S21 baseline with no rods at a tape-measured reference range r0 (sphere centre to sphere centre). The hub's Experiment A guidance is to keep the receiver at 1 to 2 λ or more (about 0.25 to 0.5 m) on a non-metallic rail with a laser or scale and ±1 mm fine adjust (setup.html#ranging-a). Candidate ranges: 2λ = 0.46 m, 3λ = 0.69 m, 4λ = 0.93 m, 6λ = 1.39 m, 8λ = 1.85 m (proposal). Save frequency, dB and phase as CSV.
  6. Plates only (acrylic plates and bases, no rods): repeat the S21 baseline. This isolates the plate.
  7. Range check: paste the S21 sweep into the range finder and compare its estimate with the tape.
  8. Brass rods, angle scan: rotate the H array through φ = 0 to 180° in 10° steps (then once over 0 to 360° as an artefact check), logging S21 magnitude at the carrier each time. The V array stays fixed. Run this first with the fixture MIDWAY; the three-station version is in section 5.
  9. Settle after each step: after every 10° turn, hands off and wait about 3 s (or until the live trace is flat) before reading, so the rods stop ringing. Log it as settle_s. Estimates and reasons: rod-jig page, section 7.
  10. Fit the scan to T = T0 exp(-κ cos²(φ - φ0)) on the Joule-loss page. Keep κ, φ0, T0 and the residual.
  11. W1 rods: same plates, same positions, same range, same VNA settings. Fit again and compare κ with the brass value.
  12. Independent cross-check with a TinySA Ultra as receiver (peak hold) for one brass and one W1 scan.
  13. Dipole Detector DD-1296 in place of the receiving sphere: rotate it through 360°, fit the figure-eight on the range-finder page.
  14. Repeat on a second day. Save every raw file; edit nothing.

Ranges are centre to centre. Keep cables taped down after calibration; a moved cable is a phase error. Never exceed +10 dBm or 5 V DC on a VNA port.

5. Three-position fixture scan (proposal)

Added 4 Oct 2026. Analysis calculators: Joule-loss page, section 8. Checklist: To-Do group.

hypothesis Dan's early-attenuation idea. A rotating rod fixture placed close to the transmitter should give a more measurable change in the detected amplitude than one placed midway or near the receiver, because paths that would otherwise reflect off objects between TX and RX are attenuated before they can reflect. To keep the fixture out of the source's reactive near field, the closest point of approach of any rod during the rotation must stay at least λ/(2π) from the TX sphere centre: 110.0 mm at 433.59 MHz, 36.8 mm at 1296 MHz, 19.5 mm at 2450 MHz. This is a hypothesis test and a proposal. Nothing here is a measured result.

Where does the fixture go? The sources do not say. Monstein and Wesley (2002) report testing the wave by "intervening a cubic array of 9 half-wavelength wires" (abstract) and call it a polarizer-analyzer. They give no distance from either ball. Butterworth et al. (2013), describing that apparatus, write "Between Tx and Rx are positioned a pair of rotatable polarizer-analyzer arrays", which is where the word "between" comes from. Neither source fixes a position along the line, so this test treats the position as a variable to measure.

Pivot means the turntable axis. The V array (rods vertical, fixed) travels with the rotating array as one fixture; give it the same clearance and log the H to V spacing. If your turntable axis is not through the array centre, recompute with calculator G.

Protocol

  1. Fix the geometry. Choose r once (4λ above, or your own) and keep heights, cables, TX power, VNA settings and cage state fixed. Only the fixture moves, along the non-metallic rail.
  2. Mark the three pivot positions and hand-rotate the fixture through 360° at each near station with a tape: no rod point may come closer than 36.8 mm to the nearer sphere centre.
  3. Reference runs at every station: rods out, plates only (no rods), and an ambient baseline with the TX off.
  4. Angle scan at every station: φ = 0 to 180° in 10° steps, then once over 0 to 360°. At each angle log the detected amplitude, the TX S11 at the carrier (VNA port 1 at the SMA reference plane, to catch near-field loading or detuning), the optional RX S11 (the port 2 reflection on a two-port VNA) and the ambient baseline.
  5. Order: NEAR TX, MIDWAY, NEAR RX, then the reverse, so slow drift cancels.
  6. Repeat with W1 rods in the same holes and stations, then on a second day (and, if time allows, at r + λ/4 to move the standing-wave phase).
  7. Fit each station with calculator F and compare κ across stations and between brass and W1.

Log columns: date_time, pass (A = 0 to 180, B = 0 to 360), station (NEAR_TX, MIDWAY, NEAR_RX), material (brass, W1, none), band_MHz, r_m, pivot_mm (TX sphere centre to turntable axis), closest_approach_mm, angle_deg, rx_level_dB, tx_s11_dB, rx_s11_dB, ambient_dB, cage_state, settle_s, notes.
