Round 1 · 433.59 MHz · sphere transmitter, sphere receiver, polarising-rod attenuator

Experiment B · 433.59 MHz

The Monstein and Wesley (2002) replication: two aluminium balls, one transmitting and one receiving, with a 3 x 3 array of half-wave brass rods turned through φ between them. Their published curve is T = exp(-κ cos²φ) with a fitted κ = 4.4 for brass. No balun of any kind on this bench. Round 1 repeats it with brass and then W1 tool steel rods, adds VNA S21 baselines, an automatic range estimate and a Joule-loss analysis.

Tests of hypotheses, not results. Mainstream electrodynamics treats free-space radio as transverse (TEM). A longitudinal or scalar component is a hypothesis we are testing, and nothing on these pages claims that scalar or longitudinal waves have been detected. Values marked proposal are our suggestions and are not measured or sourced. Draft v0.1, 4 Oct 2026, written by Grok Bot for Dan Britton.

2. Run order

  1. Inspect the rods (length, diameter, magnet and mass test) on the To-Do page. Do not start until the bags are labelled brass or W1.
  2. Calibrate the VNA in slot 0 (380 to 490 MHz); full SOLT with the TX and RX cables, then check that a THRU reads 0 dB (vna-howto.html).
  3. Noise floor: terminate both ports and record S21 with the settings you will use. Anything within 10 dB of that floor is not usable.
  4. S11 of each sphere at the SMA reference plane. Record the lowest dip and the value at 433.59 MHz. Do not tune to chase a number; log it and compare after any change.
  5. S21 baseline with no rods at a tape-measured reference range r0 (sphere centre to sphere centre). The hub's Experiment B guidance is a baseline of 2 to 3 λ or more (about 1.4 to 2.1 m and up), with the rod cube at least λ/(2π) = 110 mm from either ball (monstein.html#ranging-b). Candidate ranges: 2λ = 1.38 m, 3λ = 2.07 m, 4λ = 2.77 m, 6λ = 4.15 m, 8λ = 5.53 m (proposal). Save frequency, dB and phase as CSV.
  6. Plates only (acrylic plates and bases, no rods): repeat the S21 baseline. This isolates the plate.
  7. Range check: paste the S21 sweep into the range finder and compare its estimate with the tape.
  8. Brass rods, angle scan: rotate the H array through φ = 0 to 180° in 10° steps (then once over 0 to 360° as an artefact check), logging S21 magnitude at the carrier each time. The V array stays fixed.
  9. Fit the scan to T = T0 exp(-κ cos²(φ - φ0)) on the Joule-loss page. Keep κ, φ0, T0 and the residual.
  10. W1 rods: same plates, same positions, same range, same VNA settings. Fit again and compare κ with the brass value.
  11. Independent cross-check with a TinySA Ultra as receiver (peak hold) for one brass and one W1 scan.
  12. Dipole Detector DD-433 in place of the receiving sphere: rotate it through 360°, fit the figure-eight on the range-finder page.
  13. Repeat on a second day. Save every raw file; edit nothing.

Ranges are centre to centre. Keep cables taped down after calibration; a moved cable is a phase error. Never exceed +10 dBm or 5 V DC on a VNA port.

3. Controls

ControlWhy
TX off (VNA port 1 terminated or TinySA generator off)Anything left is pickup, drift or the receiver itself.
50 Ω load in place of the TX sphereA signal that survives a dummy load is not coming from the sphere.
RX terminated with 50 ΩReceiver and cable leakage floor.
Rods removed, plates in; then plates removedSeparates the rods from their holder.
TX sphere, with its SMA bulkhead and feed, rotated 90° about the TX to RX axisA transverse (TEM) signal from a sphere and feed has a polarisation set by the feed axis, so T(φ) should move with it. A hypothetical longitudinal component would not care. This is a proposal for a discriminating test.
TX and RX swappedChecks reciprocity and symmetry of the fixture.
Three ranges, not oneDistance dependence separates near-field terms from radiating terms.
Cage open and closed (if a cage is used)Record grounded or floating in every log row. No cable through a cage wall.

4. What results would and would not mean

ObservationOrdinary readingWould support the hypothesis only if
The receiver sphere sees the transmitterExpected. Two small antennas couple at short range through ordinary near and far fields.Not evidence by itself. Detection is the instrument check, not the result.
T(φ) follows exp(-κ cos²φ)A wire array attenuates the field component along its wires. Transverse waves do this too (a wire-grid polariser).The curve does not move when the TX sphere and feed are rotated 90°, and κ behaves as the Joule-loss page predicts when brass is swapped for W1.
W1 and brass give the same κLoss is dominated by scattering from good conductors, independent of conductivity.Disfavours κ proportional to σ (literal Joule). Nothing more.
W1 κ is about σW1/σbrass times the brass valuePossible with real absorption in the rods.Repeats on another day, in another place, at a second frequency.
Figure-eight peaks end-on from a known sourceInside about λ/2π (and still somewhat beyond) an ordinary source has a radial E component.It persists at several λ and falls off as 1/r, not as the near-field terms.
