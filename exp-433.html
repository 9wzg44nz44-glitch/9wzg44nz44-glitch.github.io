Round 1 · 433.59 MHz · sphere transmitter, sphere receiver, polarising-rod attenuator

Experiment B · 433.59 MHz

The Monstein and Wesley (2002) replication: two aluminium balls, one transmitting and one receiving, with a 3 x 3 array of half-wave brass rods turned through φ between them. Their published curve is T = exp(-κ cos²φ) with a fitted κ = 4.4 for brass. No balun of any kind on this bench. Round 1 repeats it with brass and then W1 tool steel (annealed) rods, each turned through a full 360°, first on the open bench with no Faraday cage and then with the big spheres inside a closed Faraday cage. It also adds VNA S21 baselines, an automatic range estimate and a Joule-loss analysis.

Tests of hypotheses, not results. Mainstream electrodynamics treats free-space radio as transverse (TEM). A longitudinal or scalar component is a hypothesis we are testing, and nothing on these pages claims that scalar or longitudinal waves have been detected. Values marked proposal are our suggestions and are not measured or sourced. Draft v0.1, 4 Oct 2026, written by Grok Bot for Dan Britton.

2. Run order

Two runs, from Dan's 5 Oct 2026 email to Steve ("Big Sphere Experiment - no Faraday Cage Required to replicate Monstein & Wesley 2002").
  • Scan change (Dr. Hively's requested change, per Dan's 5 Oct 2026 email to Steve). Swap the brass rods for W1 tool steel (annealed) and turn the lazy Susan a full 360°. Both rod sets get the same full scan: φ = 0 to 360° in 10° steps (37 readings; the 360° reading is the return-to-start check). Monstein and Wesley's Fig. 3 covers φ (the angle between the wire direction and the propagation direction) from -90° to +90°, a 180° span, with the fit T = exp(-4.4 cos²φ). Our Exp B extends this to the full 0 to 360°, per Dr. Hively's request.
  • Run 1, primary: open bench, no Faraday cage. Per Dan's email, this data collection does not need the cage.
  • Run 2, planned: the same scans with the big spheres inside a closed Faraday cage. Its purpose is to answer the objection "you're not using a theoretical sphere, you drilled a hole in it, thus TEM waves are possible." proposal Cage layout still to be agreed: each sphere, with its feed and SMA bulkhead, in its own closed enclosure (the Experiment A layout) addresses the objection directly. A single cage around both spheres would still allow an ordinary TEM path between them inside the cage.
  • Sphere option (optional). If the drilled-hole approach needs modification, the machine shop can machine a sphere with the flange built in as one part, as Monstein and Wesley did (Fig. 1). Decision item on the To-Do page.
  1. Inspect the rods (length, diameter, magnet and mass test) on the To-Do page. Do not start until the bags are labelled brass or W1.
  2. Calibrate the VNA in slot 0 (380 to 490 MHz); full SOLT with the TX and RX cables, then check that a THRU reads 0 dB (vna-howto.html).
  3. Noise floor: terminate both ports and record S21 with the settings you will use. Anything within 10 dB of that floor is not usable.
  4. S11 of each sphere at the SMA reference plane. Record the lowest dip and the value at 433.59 MHz. Do not tune to chase a number; log it and compare after any change.
  5. S21 baseline with no rods at a tape-measured reference range r0 (sphere centre to sphere centre). The hub's Experiment B guidance is a baseline of 2 to 3 λ or more (about 1.4 to 2.1 m and up), with the rod cube at least λ/(2π) = 110 mm from either ball (monstein.html#ranging-b). Candidate ranges: 2λ = 1.38 m, 3λ = 2.07 m, 4λ = 2.77 m, 6λ = 4.15 m, 8λ = 5.53 m (proposal). Save frequency, dB and phase as CSV.
  6. Plates only (acrylic plates and bases, no rods): repeat the S21 baseline. This isolates the plate.
  7. Range check: paste the S21 sweep into the range finder and compare its estimate with the tape.
  8. Run 1 (open, no cage), brass rods, full angle scan: rotate the H array through φ = 0 to 360° in 10° steps (37 readings; the 360° reading is the return-to-start check), logging S21 magnitude at the carrier each time. The 0° and 360° readings should agree; if they do not, the fixture moved or the bench drifted. The V array stays fixed. Run this first with the fixture MIDWAY; the three-station version is in section 5.
  9. Fit the scan to T = T0 exp(-κ cos²(φ - φ0)) on the Joule-loss page. Keep κ, φ0, T0 and the residual.
  10. Run 1 (open), W1 tool steel (annealed) rods: the same full 0 to 360° scan with the same plates, positions, range and VNA settings (Dr. Hively's requested change, per Dan's 5 Oct 2026 email to Steve). Fit again and compare κ with the brass value.
  11. Independent cross-check with a TinySA Ultra as receiver (peak hold) for one brass and one W1 scan.
  12. Dipole Detector DD-433 in place of the receiving sphere: rotate it through 360°, fit the figure-eight on the range-finder page.
  13. Repeat on a second day. Save every raw file; edit nothing.
  14. Run 2 (planned), closed Faraday cage: repeat the brass and W1 full 360° scans with the big spheres inside a closed Faraday cage, same range and settings, to answer the drilled-hole TEM objection. Record the cage layout, closed state and grounding in every row.

Ranges are centre to centre. Keep cables taped down after calibration; a moved cable is a phase error. Never exceed +10 dBm or 5 V DC on a VNA port.

5. Three-position fixture scan (proposal)

Added 4 Oct 2026. Analysis calculators: Joule-loss page, section 8. Checklist: To-Do group.

hypothesis Dan's early-attenuation idea. A rotating rod fixture placed close to the transmitter should give a more measurable change in the detected amplitude than one placed midway or near the receiver, because paths that would otherwise reflect off objects between TX and RX are attenuated before they can reflect. To keep the fixture out of the source's reactive near field, the closest point of approach of any rod during the rotation must stay at least λ/(2π) from the TX sphere centre: 110.0 mm at 433.59 MHz, 36.8 mm at 1296 MHz, 19.5 mm at 2450 MHz. This is a hypothesis test and a proposal. Nothing here is a measured result.

Where does the fixture go? The sources do not say. Monstein and Wesley (2002) report testing the wave by "intervening a cubic array of 9 half-wavelength wires" (abstract) and call it a polarizer-analyzer. They give no distance from either ball. Butterworth et al. (2013), describing that apparatus, write "Between Tx and Rx are positioned a pair of rotatable polarizer-analyzer arrays", which is where the word "between" comes from. Neither source fixes a position along the line, so this test treats the position as a variable to measure.

Pivot means the turntable axis. The V array (rods vertical, fixed) travels with the rotating array as one fixture; give it the same clearance and log the H to V spacing. If your turntable axis is not through the array centre, recompute with calculator G.

Protocol

  1. Fix the geometry. Choose r once (4λ above, or your own) and keep heights, cables, TX power, VNA settings and cage state fixed. Only the fixture moves, along the non-metallic rail.
  2. Mark the three pivot positions and hand-rotate the fixture through 360° at each near station with a tape: no rod point may come closer than 110.0 mm to the nearer sphere centre.
  3. Reference runs at every station: rods out, plates only (no rods), and an ambient baseline with the TX off.
  4. Angle scan at every station: φ = 0 to 360° in 10° steps (37 readings; the 360° reading is the return-to-start check), on the open bench (Run 1) and, when it is set up, in the closed cage (Run 2). At each angle log the detected amplitude, the TX S11 at the carrier (VNA port 1 at the SMA reference plane, to catch near-field loading or detuning), the optional RX S11 (the port 2 reflection on a two-port VNA) and the ambient baseline.
  5. Order: NEAR TX, MIDWAY, NEAR RX, then the reverse, so slow drift cancels.
  6. Repeat with W1 tool steel (annealed) rods in the same holes and stations, with the same full scan, then on a second day (and, if time allows, at r + λ/4 to move the standing-wave phase).
  7. Fit each station with calculator F and compare κ across stations and between brass and W1.

Log columns: date_time, pass (B = full 0 to 360, the Experiment B scan; A = 0 to 180, used on the 1.3 and 2.45 GHz benches), station (NEAR_TX, MIDWAY, NEAR_RX), material (brass, W1, none), band_MHz, r_m, pivot_mm (TX sphere centre to turntable axis), closest_approach_mm, angle_deg, rx_level_dB, tx_s11_dB, rx_s11_dB, ambient_dB, cage_state (open, or closed with layout), notes.
