Seawater, steel hull and the detector's cage

Seawater permittivity (FACT, Meissner & Wentz 2004)

FACT We use the double-Debye seawater model of Meissner & Wentz, IEEE TGRS 42, 1836 (2004). It uses their Eq. 6 with Eqs. 7–8 (pure water, Table III), Eq. 17 (salinity, Table VI) and the Stogryn conductivity regression, Eqs. 11–16. Valid range: −2 to 29 °C for seawater, salinity 0–40, up to at least 90 GHz. The coefficients were typed from the printed tables (REMSS PDF) and checked against the authors' own Fortran (meissner_wentz_dielectric.f90, RSS GitHub). Note that the Fortran has a 2012 update to two coefficients, which we do not use.

ε(T,S,ν) = (ε_S − ε_1)/(1 + iν/ν_1) + (ε_1 − ε_∞)/(1 + iν/ν_2) + ε_∞ − i σ/(2π ε_0 ν) (MW2004 Eq. 6, ν in Hz here) TEM (FACT, plane wave in a lossy medium): k = (ω/c)√(ε' − jε″), α_TEM = −Im k, η = η_0/√(ε' − jε″)

At 1057.8 MHz, 15 °C, S = 35: ε′ = {{ep}}, ε″ = {{epp}}, σ = {{sig}}, loss tangent {{tand}}, wavelength in water {{lamw}}, wave impedance |η| = {{eta}}. The brief's round numbers (σ ≈ 4 S/m, εr ≈ 70, skin depth about 1 cm) agree.

A prior claim to keep in view: Tomilin, Lukin & Gulkov (2021)

FACT (what the paper reports) Tomilin, Lukin & Gulkov, Tech. Phys. Lett. 47, 577 (2021), in the hub library, report a voice-tone link over 470 m of seawater (σ = 4.77 S/m, 8 °C) at 27.4 MHz with 1.17 W, using insulated spheres driven by Tesla flat coils. They say the transmitter and receiver sat in sealed boxes with batteries and hung on nylon ropes. HYP The authors hypothesise that longitudinal waves carried it. They report no Faraday-cage or TEM control of the kind in our gate G2. ASSUMPTION (ours, S = 35 because the paper gives only σ) Our seawater model gives a TEM 1/e length of {{tom_dT}} at 27.4 MHz, so 470 m is about {{tom_dB}} of TEM loss. The Joule-literal SLW reading gives {{tom_dJ}}, which also rules the claim out. Only the Erratum reading (no loss at all) is consistent with it. This is one unreplicated field report, and G2's controls are designed to test exactly this kind of claim.

Steel hull (Thull)

FACT Ordinary radio does not pass through an intact metal wall. FX-1 estimated that a 1 mm stainless wall already absorbs more than 400 dB at 433 MHz (skin effect; Ott, EMC Engineering, 2009, ch. 6), and a centimetre-thick steel hull absorbs far more. What leaks in comes through seams, hatches and glands. HYP Hively reports the SLW passing thousands of skin depths of copper. Whether it passes a steel hull is untested, so Thull (field fraction) is swept from 10−12 to 1. The front page uses Thull = 1, the most favourable case for the link. ASSUMPTION For TEM we give the hull no credit (SEhull,TEM = 0 dB), because the hull is not the cage, and we include the textbook water-to-air field transmission factor |τ| = |2η0/(η0+ηw)| = {{tau_wa}} (FACT, normal incidence).

The detector's own cage (SEcage, Tcage) and the FX-1 openEMS seam results

FACT (for the FX-1 geometry) The FX-1 openEMS runs (433.59 MHz, 28 cm metal sphere; fx1-snr.html, fx1-openems-reference.json) show how much ordinary field leaks through seams. We turn them into a seam-leak shielding proxy: the field 5 cm outside ({{fx1_ext}} at 0.4 V drive) divided by the field at the centre inside. The centre value is the largest of the four probe directions in the full run output (snapshot in expt-g/sim/fx1_seam_inputs.json). The 2S chain: pump, wait, tickle, count

1 · Pump: two 243 nm photons (FACT)

FACT 1S → 2S is driven by two photons at {{lam243}}. A 972 nm laser is doubled twice (972 → 486 → 243 nm). Parthey et al., PRL 107, 203001 (2011) obtained “13 mW of the required uv light near 243 nm” this way. The 1S–2S energy is {{E1S2S}} (from f1S–2S, Parthey 2011), so each 243 nm photon carries {{E243}}.

Correction (brief 1a): the concept note's “two 972 nm photons” is wrong. A {{lam972}} photon carries {{E972}}, so two carry only {{E2x972}}, far short of the {{E1S2S}} needed. 972 nm is the laser's fundamental. It must be frequency-quadrupled to 243 nm, and then two 243 nm photons are absorbed.

FACT Lab values for N2S: Landhuis et al. (arXiv:physics/0210019) made “at least 5 × 107 magnetically trapped 2S atoms” below 100 µK, with a detection efficiency of “approximately 2×10−6”. Parthey 2011 used a 5.8 K beam with an atom flux of 1017/s. The number of 2S atoms in view is not quoted there, so it is unknown for this page.

2 · Wait: the 2E1 background (FACT)

FACT A lone 2S atom decays by two-photon (2E1) emission at 8.2283 s−1 (Klarsfeld 1969), so it lives {{tau2S}}. The photon spectrum is broad (Spitzer & Greenstein 1951). We use the Nussbaumer & Schmutz (1984) fit A(y) = C[w(1 − (4w)γ) + α wβ(4w)γ], w = y(1 − y), y = ν/νLyα, with C = 202.0 s−1, α = 0.88, β = 1.53, γ = 0.8. Our integral gives ½∫A dy = {{NSint}} s−1 (published: 8.2249). FACT The narrowest catalogue Lyman-α filter we found is the Teledyne Acton FN122-XN: 122 ± 2.5 nm, 10 nm FWHM maximum, T ≥ 15 % (Pelham 122-NBX is about 10 nm with T ≥ 5 %; 20 nm types are FN122-N and 122-NB). ASSUMPTION The passband shape is not published. We bracket it with a Lorentzian (wide wings, used for the lab case) and a Gaussian (used for the best case), centred at 121.6 nm. We set a blocking floor of 10−4 between 115 and 200 nm (Pelham quotes “visible rejection ~10−4”; the VUV blocking itself is not verified) and 10−4/8500 above 200 nm (Hamamatsu R13194: CsI sensitivity at 121.6 nm is 8500× that at 300 nm). Result, in 2E1 photons that reach the detector per 2S decay (relative to the filter's transmission at Lyman-α): {{F2_10L}} (10 nm Lorentzian) or {{F2_10G}} (10 nm Gaussian), that is {{R2E1pa_10L}} or {{R2E1pa_10G}} photons per atom per second. A 20 nm filter passes {{F2_20L}} / {{F2_20G}}. A hypothetical 1 nm filter would pass {{F2_1G}}. These match our separate desk comparison (snr-2s-vs-sphere, 25 Sep 2026), which used the same shape and floor.

3 · Tickle: the Lamb-shift resonance (FACT)

FACT Lamb & Retherford 1950 (Phys. Rev. 79, 549), Eqs. 25–27. We write them in SI with a peak field E0: rate = γV²/ħ² / [(ω−ω0)² + γ²/4], with V = √3·e·a0·E0/2 (|⟨e·r⟩|² = 3a0², Eq. 27). We also include the counter-rotating term and the 2S→2P3/2 path (ASSUMPTION: same γ). On resonance, 1 µV/m gives {{rate1u}} s−1 per atom, about one tickle per atom per 1,000 years, as the brief says. The line is broad: full width {{fwhm}} (γ/2π, γ = A(2P1/2) = 6.2648×108 s−1, NIST). Check: a 10 V/cm static field (Eq. 42) gives a 2S lifetime of {{tau10}}. Landhuis' formula “γs = 2800E²” gives {{tauLand}}.

3b · Hyperfine structure: 1057.8 MHz is a centroid (FACT)

FACT (ours, from the 2S hyperfine interval 177.557 MHz, Bullis et al., PRL 130, 203001 (2023), and the 2P1/2 interval 59.22 MHz, Lundeen, Jessop & Pipkin, PRL 34, 377 (1975)) {{L}} is the fine-structure centroid. The real 2S1/2→2P1/2 lines are at {{hfc01}} (F=0→F′=1; published 909.894(20) MHz, PRA 98, 012509 (2018)), {{hfc11}} (F=1→F′=1, weight 2/3) and {{hfc10}} (F=1→F′=0, weight 1/3). FACT Landhuis's trapped atoms are 2S F = 1. For them, a drive at {{L}} gives {{hfF1}}× the centroid rate ({{hfF1dB}}). Retuning the drive to {{hfc11}} recovers {{hfF1_1087}}×. ADS-B at 1090 MHz sits only {{hf_ads_df}} from the F=1→F′=1 line, so F=1 atoms feel it at {{lzF1_ads}}× the centroid peak, against {{lz_ads}} if hyperfine is ignored. Every rate on this page uses the F = 1 atoms unless stated. The front page keeps the brief's 1057.8 MHz drive.

Control (d), honestly: for F = 1 atoms, detuning by +300 MHz cuts the rate to {{lzF1_p300}} of the centroid peak, and −300 MHz to {{lzF1_m300}} (centroid-only formula: {{lz_p300}}, {{lzdB_p300}}). At ±500 MHz the rate is {{lzF1_p500}} / {{lzF1_m500}}. Relative to the 1057.8 MHz drive that is only {{dF1_p300}} / {{dF1_m300}} at ±300 MHz and {{dF1_p500}} / {{dF1_m500}} at ±500 MHz (16–20 dB if hyperfine is ignored), because the line is 100 MHz wide. It proves the atoms are responding. It does not tell SLW from TEM, because leaked TEM follows the same curve. That is control (a)'s job.

3h · Would an SLW tickle at all? (HYP)

HYP The brief assumes that a longitudinal SLW E-field couples to ⟨2S|z|2P⟩ like any E-field. That is untested. η, the coupling relative to an ordinary field of the same size, is swept from 10−12 to 1 in All controls. Hively's papers contain no atomic-transition analysis. Only the patent mentions 1S→2S (Expt D literature check).

7 · Magnetic-tuning option, with its caveats

FACT (our diagonalisation, hyperfine ignored) In a magnetic field of {{Bc}} the 2S1/2(mJ = −½) “β” level crosses the 2P1/2 “e” level (Lamb & Retherford 1950 §16 found 540 G using their older ~1000 Mc/s shift; our code reproduces that). The splitting changes by {{slope}}. Near the crossing a low-frequency or even static field ⊥ B quenches 2S: at the crossing, 1 µV/m gives {{Gmag1u}} s−1. Caveats: (1) Broadband: at the crossing, anything from DC up to tens of MHz quenches, so ambient HF/VHF noise and ripple count as signal. (2) Motional field v×B: a moving atom sees E = vB. At 573.5 G this is {{Emot_100uK}} for 100 µK trapped H ({{Gmot_100uK}}), {{Emot_5K8}} for a 5.8 K beam ({{Gmot_5K8}}), and {{Emot_300K}} at 300 K ({{Gmot_300K}}). Beams are quenched before they arrive. (3) The field must be held to a fraction of a gauss. Recommendation: use the direct 1057.8 MHz drive at B ≈ 0. There, stray fields of 1 mG–1 G shift the line by at most about 1.4 MHz (μB/h ≈ 1.4 MHz/G), which is small against 100 MHz, and motional quench is negligible. ASSUMPTION The magnetic-option numbers ignore hyperfine structure.

4 · Count: Lyman-α detection, and every background (FACT / ours)

FACT Each tickled atom emits one Lyman-α photon within {{tau2P}}. Counts = N2S × εdet × rate. The backgrounds per 2S atom, largest first for the lab case:

  • Stray-field Stark quench (FACT: Landhuis saw 2S lifetimes ≤1 ms in a non-metal cell, and up to 100 ms with copper-film electrodes and the best field compensation). Ours: 1/0.1 s − 8.23 s−1 = ≤{{Gstray}} s−1, an upper bound that also includes any other one-body loss. These are real Lyman-α photons, so no filter can remove them. This term dominates the lab case. The best case assumes a residual 10 mV/m (ASSUMPTION).
  • 2E1 continuum through the filter: {{R2E1pa_10L}} (Lorentzian) or {{R2E1pa_10G}} (Gaussian) s−1, above.
  • Room-temperature blackbody inside the cage (ours, Planck spectrum × Lamb matrix elements; Gallagher & Cooke, PRL 42, 835 (1979)): {{bbr290}} s−1 at 290 K, 99 % of it through the 9.9 GHz 2S→2P3/2 path. It is {{bbr4}} s−1 inside a 4 K shield.
  • Radio leaking through the cage (ADS-B, GPS, DME, Wi-Fi) and the transmitter's own ordinary-radio leak: see Link budget.
  • Detector dark counts: see Receiver chain.

ASSUMPTION (as in the desk comparison) A pump holds N2S steady against the no-signal loss rate. When the tickle rate approaches that loss rate (about 10 s−1 in the lab case) the cloud is depleted and the signal saturates. This is modelled. Landhuis made single-shot clouds, so a steady N2S of 5×107 is itself optimistic. FACT One channel electron multiplier counts at most about 15 million counts per second (Sjuts EDR CEM), and the page flags any case above that.
