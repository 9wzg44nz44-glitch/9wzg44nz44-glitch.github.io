Atoms against an ordinary antenna. Our desk comparison (snr-2s-vs-sphere, 25 Sep 2026, not yet on the hub) finds that for SNR = 1 in 1 s the atoms need 55–122 dB more field than the ¾″ sphere antenna with a ZX60-P33ULN+ LNA at 1058 MHz. It is 122 dB for lab-demonstrated atoms and 55 dB for an ideal bound in a 4 K shield. The gap widens by 5 dB for every factor of 10 in counting time. So the atoms are not a sensitive field detector. Their only value here is as a test of whether an SLW couples to an atomic transition at all (HYP).

Honest G1 summary. The hard parts are the 2S source and vacuum, not the RF. The brief's own chain (243 nm from a quadrupled 972 nm laser) is a frequency-metrology-lab system. No price for one was found, and none may be quoted without contacting vendors. The Lamb–Retherford electron-bombardment beam avoids the laser and is the historical way this exact 1058 MHz quench was first seen (1947). Either way, G1 is a university-lab-scale project. Its cost is unknown, and the only cited line item on this page is the Lyman-α filter.

Gates G0–G3 (in order; do not skip)

  1. G0 · desk simulation (now). Full chain model, link budget, detector comparison, on GitHub plus the Mathematica twin. A paper result only. Status: this page (expt-g-v0.1). The Python ↔ JS compare passes. The WL twin is written by hand but not run, so its compare is owed.
  2. G1 · TEM baseline on the bench. Reproduce standard RF quenching of 2S at 1058 MHz with an ordinary antenna. This proves the receiver works before any SLW claim. It needs hydrogen, the UV laser (or the L&R electron-bombardment alternative), vacuum and Lyman-α hardware. Cost and difficulty: Receiver chain. It should also reproduce the Lorentzian ({{fwhm}} wide) and the ±300 MHz contrast ({{dF1_p300}} / {{dF1_m300}} for F = 1 atoms), and survey 1058 MHz ± 100 MHz for DME, ADS-B and Wi-Fi products, using an LNA that covers 1058 MHz (ZX60-P33ULN+, not the Kuhne 1296 MHz LNA).
  3. G2 · POOL MOCKUP TEST (required before any comms claim). The detector goes inside its own cage, inside a steel hull mockup, in water of known salinity. The transmitter sits outside, across a measured seawater path. Required controls:
    1. An ordinary TEM transmitter with the same power and frequency must give no tickle signal through the cage and hull. (Expected: {{ctlA_Edet}} at the atoms for 1 W over 5 cm with an 80 dB cage.)
    2. Cage open versus closed.
    3. Transmitter on versus off, and modulation on versus off.
    4. Detuned drive (for example ±300 MHz) must lose the signal on the Lyman-α Lorentzian (expected contrast for F = 1 atoms {{dF1_p300}} / {{dF1_m300}}).
    5. Salinity and range must be varied. Under the Joule-literal reading the signal falls about {{aJdB}} with range in seawater and changes with salinity like TEM. Under the Erratum reading it follows 1/r only.
    Only a signal that survives all of these may be called SLW (HYP) evidence.
  4. G3 · comms demo. Proceed only if G2 is positive. Measure bit error rate against range.
