The field the receiver needs

ASSUMPTION (ours) The transmitter is keyed on/off, half the time each, and counts are Poisson. Then SNR = Rs√(T/2)/√(Rs + 2Rb), where Rs is the signal count rate, Rb the background count rate and T the counting time. We need SNR = 5 in T = 1 s. Longer counting helps only slowly: once background dominates, the needed field scales as T−1/4, so 1 hour lowers it by a factor {{Tint_scale}}.

What the brief's “1 µV/m” really costs. At 1 µV/m each atom is tickled at {{rate1u}} s−1 (×{{hfF1}} for F = 1 atoms). The same atom also emits {{gb_lab}} s−1 of background photons in the lab case. To see 1 µV/m at SNR 5 in 1 s therefore needs N2S ≈ {{N1u_lab}} with the lab detector ({{N1u_ratio_lab}} times Landhuis's cloud) or {{N1u_opt}} with the best one. Neither is possible. The practical sensitivity is the “needed field” column above: hundreds of mV/m today and a few mV/m at best.

Backgrounds through the cage (brief §3)

FACT Field levels come from the free-space far-field formula, with isotropic antennas assumed (ASSUMPTION). ADS-B: Mode S transponders emit 18.5–27 dBW peak (ICAO Annex 10 Vol IV); we use 500 W at 10 km. GPS L2C: −160 dBW minimum into a 3 dBi antenna (IS-GPS-200). DME: aircraft interrogate on 1025–1150 MHz on a 1 MHz grid (NRAO EDIR 313; FAA), so one DME channel sits at 1058 MHz, on our resonance; we use 300 W at 10 km. Wi-Fi: 100 mW EIRP at 3 m (ASSUMPTION). “Equivalent” means the field at 1057.8 MHz that would give the same tickle rate for F = 1 atoms (hyperfine lines and the Lorentzian included; ADS-B is {{hf_ads_df}} from the F=1→F′=1 line). There is no duty-cycle credit here; ADS-B and DME are pulsed, and the live model uses 10−3 (ASSUMPTION).

ADS-B leakage result: {{E_ads10}} outside ({{E_ads1}} at 1 km) becomes {{eq_ads_80}} equivalent behind an 80 dB cage. That is far below both receivers' needed field, so about {{SEneed_ads}} of cage is enough. Our desk comparison reached the same conclusion with a different ADS-B level (a JFK-average of 0.45 mV/m RMS; 1090ES Class A1 transmitters emit at least 125 W, FAA AC 20-165B). Submerged, the sea itself removes ADS-B (TEM at {{aTdB}}). Out of the water, at the G1 bench or the G2 pool, the cage must do the work. ASSUMPTION Wi-Fi harmonics and intermodulation products that land near 1058 MHz cannot be predicted here. G1 must include a spectrum survey at 1058 MHz ± 100 MHz inside and outside the cage.

The transmitter's own TEM leak (control a): 1 W through 5 cm of seawater gives {{ctlA_Eout}} at the hull (FACT), {{ctlA_Edet}} at the atoms behind an 80 dB cage ({{ctlA_Edet40}} at 40 dB), and a count rate of about {{ctlA_R}} with the lab receiver. Ordinary radio cannot fake the signal through a sound cage at these ranges. A leaky cage (see the FX-1 seam table) plus a short water path could, which is why control (a) and (b) are required.
