Monstein & Wesley 2002: brass-rod polarizer for a claimed longitudinal E

C. Monstein and J.P. Wesley reported a 433.59 MHz ball-antenna experiment they titled “Observation of scalar longitudinal electrodynamic waves.” This page is a study aid: what they built, the formulas in the paper, how later authors read it, and how it does and does not connect to Hively Faraday-cage SLW work, Nikolova–Zimmerman A(r), Meyl, and Puthoff polarizable-vacuum notes. Mainstream CED treats free-space radio as TEM. Longitudinal / “scalar” channels remain disputed.

Authors’ claim, not a verdict. We do not host the IOP/EPLA PDF. Cite doi:10.1209/epl/i2002-00136-9. Author-hosted PDF (filename typo “electroydnamic”): jamespaulwesley.org copy. What they built

  • Frequency. 433.59 MHz. λ = c/f ≈ 0.692 m. λ/2 = 34.6 cm (the rod length they quote).
  • Source / detector. Identical aluminium ball antennas, 6 cm diameter, coax fed to the inside of the sphere (outer conductor as shield). Heights: source 4.0 m above ground, receiver 4.4 m.
  • Polarizer-analyzer. Cubic array of nine brass λ/2 rods on a rotatable mount (hand or turntable). Absorb when rods are parallel to the claimed propagation direction; transmit when perpendicular. Two crossed arrays were proposed as a TEM filter.
  • Range. Signal vs distance, including two interference minima they attribute to an Earth image source. They argue only a wave (not a near-zone quasi-static field) can show that plus ~1/r².

Redrawn in words: TX ball → (optional rod cube at angle φ) → RX ball, both balls well above a ground plane. That is a polarizer test for the direction of E, not a Faraday-cage skin-depth test and not a bifilar pancake.

Formulas as written in the 2002 letter

Gaussian units in the original. They start from a retarded scalar potential (their eq. 1; typesetting of the d’Alembertian is as printed):

∇²Φ − (1/c²) ∂²Φ/∂t² = −4πρ                          (1)
ρ = q δ(r − r′) sin(ωt)                                 (3)
Φ = q sin(kr − ωt) / r                                  (4)
E = ∇Φ = [k q cos(kr − ωt)/r − q sin(kr − ωt)/r²] ê_r   (5)
⟨S⟩_t = (k ω q / (2 r²)) ê_r                            (6)

Ohmic loss in a rod of conductivity σ and cross-section A, only the component E₀ cos φ along the wire (φ = angle between rods and the TX–RX line):

δP = −σ A E₀² cos²(φ) δL                                (7)
T = exp[ −κ cos²(φ) ]                                   (9)
κ_fit = 4.4   (their fig. 3 best fit; κ ∝ L)

Ground-image sketch: observed field as superposition of amplitude A from the source at height y and B from an image at y′ (they place the reflecting surface λ/2 = 34.6 cm below the actual earth). Energy ~1/r² with interference minima. We do not reproduce their fitted A, B tables; read eqs. (12)–(15) in the letter.

CED reading: a spherically fed ball is not a proof that ∇·A = 0 forbids TEM. Feed cables, the ground, and the rod array all break spherical symmetry. Bray & Britton (EPL 2004) and Rębilas (EPL 2008) press that point. This hub lists MW’s equations so you can see what they claimed, not so you treat them as Maxwell.

Rotation curve (the U)

If T = exp[−κ cos² φ] and you rotate the cube through 180° or 360°, transmission is high near φ = 90° (rods across the path) and low near φ = 0°/180° (rods along the path). That is a U (or two dips per full turn). MW’s κ = 4.4 is an ohmic-brass fit, not a magnetic-hysteresis fit.

Blue: MW eq. (9) with κ = 4.4. Orange dashed: cartoon of a deeper U if a high-hysteresis rod replaced brass (not measured here — see Hively note below).

Later public literature (short)

  • Bray J.R. & Britton M.C., EPL 66, 153 (2004), doi:10.1209/epl/i2003-10144-9 — comment on the theory.
  • Monstein & Wesley reply, EPL 66, 155 (2004), doi:10.1209/epl/i2003-10145-8.
  • Rębilas K., EPL 83, 60007 (2008), doi:10.1209/0295-5075/83/60007 — argues the detected E along the line of sight is still classical TEM launched by earth currents from the ball feed, not a free-space Φ-wave.
  • Butterworth et al., J. Scientific Exploration 27(1) (2013), reconstruction — duplicated hardware; pattern interesting; they reject MW’s Maxwell reading and also found Rębilas’ ground-plasma model a poor match. They leave the mechanism unidentified.

Hively’s scientific distinction (2026, personal communication)

Paraphrase only. Not a quotation. Not an endorsement that the 2002 result is settled.

  1. Monstein–Wesley showed an electric field along the direction of propagation using brass rods as a polarizer. That is a statement about E∥k, not about a dynamical scalar C.
  2. They did not detect Hively’s scalar field (the EED C = ∇·A + c⁻² ∂Φ/∂t that appears with the SLW in US 9,306,527).
  3. Suggested follow-on (unpublished method; attribute: L.M. Hively, 2026, personal communication). Keep the same geometry and protocol, but replace brass with high-magnetic-hysteresis rods. Rotate the dipole array through 360° and look for a U-shaped attenuation vs angle. If that curve appears, he offered to help shape a publishable paper. This hub does not claim that experiment has been done.
  4. 2014–15 Hively hardware is gone. Replication notes from that era: sleeve balun, not skirt; all TX electronics in one Faraday enclosure and all RX in a second; time-stamp TX power droop, RX (Signal Hound class), and positioner together. Scatter on 1/r² was blamed on cabling outside the Faraday enclosures. Scale the λ/4 balun and center conductor to 1.296 GHz or 2.45 GHz.
  5. Google Scholar: author LM Hively plus keyword electrodynamics. The old title “Implications of a New Electrodynamic Theory” is obsolete; use the later EED / US 9,306,527 / Physics Essays / IARD chain on this hub.
No private email is reproduced here. Hively is not a GitHub collaborator on this repo. This is an educational map, not a coauthored paper.

Will a Meyl ball-antenna kit do Hively’s protocol?

No as a kit. Partial only if you scavenge the two spheres and rebuild everything else at UHF. Do not treat Meyl potential-vortex / over-unity language as Hively’s C field.

What meyl.eu actually sells (Demo / Experimental kits): Tesla pancake coils with two spherical electrodes on plastic columns, a low-impedance waveform generator, cables, LEDs, optional frequency counter. Demo generator about 4–8 MHz; Experimental historically ~0.2–14 MHz analog or DDS to ~8–10 MHz after 2014. Coil types quoted ~3.5 / 7 / 14 MHz. Typical bench distance in the manual is tens of centimetres with a ground strap between pancake outer turns. That is HF Tesla-coil-plus-ball, not a 433 MHz ball feed and not a 23 cm lab.

Buy the kit if you want to play Meyl’s HF pancake demonstrations. Do not buy it as a substitute for nine λ/2 rods, a rotator, two Faraday boxes, or a licensed 1296 / 2.45 GHz source. Two metal spheres are cheap without the suitcase. λ/2 at 1296 MHz is 11.6 cm; at 2.45 GHz 6.1 cm — scale the rods, not a 7 MHz pancake.

If someone replicates under Part 97

  • Scale λ/4 sleeve (not skirt) and center conductor to 1296.000 MHz (23 cm, licensed) or a legal 2.45 GHz allocation. Identify; milliwatts; do not camp on a calling frequency.
  • Two Faraday enclosures: TX stack in one, RX (Signal Hound class or TinySA Ultra 406) in the other. No cables out on a “sealed” run. Time-stamp TX power, RX, and rotator angle on one clock.
  • Brass-rod baseline: nine λ/2 rods, 360° rotation, log T(φ). Optional second article: high-hysteresis rods, same geometry, look for a U. That second article is Hively’s unpublished suggestion, not a MW figure.
  • See setup / lab for RadioScreen tents and packing. Different antenna family than the pancake unless you mix them on purpose and say so.
