Experiment G — Undersea SLW link via hydrogen 2S tickled relaxation

Dan controls both ends of an undersea link. A transmitter sends a radio-frequency field at about 1057.8 MHz through seawater, a steel hull and a metal box. Inside the box, hydrogen atoms parked in a long-lived state (called 2S) are nudged by that field into a short-lived state (2P). Each nudged atom then gives off one ultraviolet photon (Lyman-α, 121.567 nm), which a detector counts. Switching the transmitter on and off switches the photon count up and down, and that carries the data. The transmitted wave is assumed to be a scalar-longitudinal wave (SLW), the hypothetical wave in Hively's extended electrodynamics. That is a HYPothesis, not established physics.

The key question, answered first

Using Hively's own Joule-loss term, how far does a ~1 GHz SLW get through seawater?

Short answer: centimetres, if his Joule term is a real loss. The SLW field then falls by a factor e every 1.45 cm of seawater at 1057.8 MHz (599 dB/m), about as fast as ordinary radio (1.10 cm). His own 2022 Erratum says the term cancels and seawater takes nothing. In that case the range is set only by the receiver.

It depends on one thing Hively's papers do not settle numerically: whether his Joule term actually removes energy from the SLW. Two readings of the same term are possible. We show both, plus the TEM-equal case the brief asks for.

  1. Reading 1: Hively & Loebl's own 2022 Erratum (HYP). The Joule term is J·E in their energy balance, Eq. (44) of Hively & Loebl 2019, with Ohm's law J = σE. The Erratum (Phys. Essays 35, 320, 2022) substitutes and cancels it:
    “Physically, Eq. (A6) means that the SLW is not subject to resistive loss in conductive media. The same no-loss result occurs from the energy balance equation, Eq. (44) … Consequently, the SLW is unconstrained by the skin effect and power loss in resistive media.”
    So seawater absorbs nothing (αSLW = 0). Range is then limited only by spreading and by the receiver. With today's best lab 2S receiver, 1 W reaches 2.96 m and 1 kW reaches 93.5 m. With the best receiver physically possible from cited parts, 1 W reaches 435 m.
  2. Reading 2: the same J·E term taken literally as a loss (our reading, ASSUMPTION). Charge the Joule heat σE² against the SLW power flow CE/μ from Eq. (44). Use Hively's own SLW impedance, Eq. (37), and his definition σ = ε0ε″ω. At 1057.8 MHz, 15 °C and salinity 35 the field then falls by a factor e every 1.45 cm (599 dB/m). That is centimetres, close to an ordinary radio wave (TEM), which falls by e every 1.10 cm (788 dB/m). Under this reading 1 W reaches only 5.72 cm, and even 1 kW reaches only 9.93 cm. The best possible receiver stretches that to 16.4 cm.
  3. TEM-equal case. If the SLW loses energy like TEM, 1 W reaches 4.59 cm and 1 kW reaches 7.81 cm.

The unknown parameter is αSLW, the SLW field attenuation in seawater in nepers per metre. It is not an extra constant in Hively's formulas. It encodes which reading is right. It gets a slider below, with presets for 0 (Erratum), the Joule-literal value and the TEM value. Only the G2 pool mockup can decide it. If Reading 2 holds, the undersea link is a few centimetres long whatever receiver is used.

Distances here are for a 1 s count with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 5, a lossless hull and cage (Thull = Tcage = 1), and SLW coupling η = 1. Distances under about one wavelength in water (2.99 cm) are near-field, where our far-field formula is only indicative. Details: Hively term · link budget.

How the receiver is shielded (FACT for ordinary radio, HYP for SLW). The detector sits in its own Faraday cage, a closed metal box that strips out ordinary radio (TEM) waves. The steel hull is not that cage, because crew Wi-Fi and other radios live inside the hull. The SLW is assumed (HYP) to pass through the cage wall. No cable crosses the cage wall: a battery sits inside, and data leave by optical fibre. So any count change that follows the transmitter must come through the wall, and control (a) below tests whether ordinary radio can explain it.

Words and symbols used on this page (plain English)
SLW, scalar-longitudinal wave
A wave predicted by Hively's “extended electrodynamics” (EED). It has an electric field pointing along its direction of travel and no magnetic field. It has never been independently confirmed. HYP
TEM, ordinary radio
The normal radio wave of textbook physics (Maxwell). A closed metal box blocks it, and seawater absorbs it within centimetres at 1 GHz. FACT
2S and 2P
Two excited states of hydrogen. 2S lives 0.1215 s on its own, so it is “metastable”. 2P lives only 1.60 ns before emitting a Lyman-α photon.
Lamb shift, L
The tiny energy gap between 2S and 2P. As a radio frequency it is 1057.8 MHz (NIST ASD). A field at this frequency “tickles” 2S into 2P.
Lyman-α
The ultraviolet photon (121.567 nm) emitted when 2P falls to the ground state 1S.
2E1 background
A lone 2S atom eventually decays by giving off two photons that share 10.2 eV. Their energies are spread over a broad range, and a few land inside the Lyman-α filter.
Stray-field quench
Small leftover electric fields in the apparatus also knock 2S atoms down, giving real Lyman-α photons that look exactly like signal. With today's best field control this is the largest background.
E (V/m)
Electric field strength in volts per metre. We quote the peak value. The rms value is 0.707 × peak.
α (Np/m, dB/m)
Attenuation: how fast a field dies away in water. The field falls by a factor e (2.718) every 1/α metres. 1 Np/m = 8.686 dB/m.
N2S
Number of hydrogen atoms held in 2S at the receiver.
εdet
Overall detection efficiency: the fraction of emitted Lyman-α photons that are actually counted.
SEcage (dB)
Shielding effectiveness of the detector's cage against ordinary radio. 80 dB means the field inside is 10,000 times weaker than outside.
Thull, Tcage
The fraction of SLW field that gets through the hull and through the cage. Both are unknown (HYP) and are swept from 10−12 to 1 and 10−6 to 1.
η
How strongly an SLW field tickles 2S compared with an ordinary field of the same size. η = 1 means “the same”. Unknown (HYP), swept from 10−12 to 1.
SNR
Signal-to-noise ratio: how far the transmitter-on count rises above the random scatter of the background counts.

Hively's Joule-loss term, quoted, and what it gives in seawater

HYP Hively & Loebl, “Classical and extended electrodynamics”, Phys. Essays 32, 112 (2019). PDF on this hub. The revised energy balance, Eq. (44), contains the Joule (Ohmic) term J·E:

∂/∂t ( B²/2μ + C²/2μ + εE² ) + ∇·( E×B/μ + CE/μ ) + J·E = ρC/(εμ) (Hively & Loebl 2019, Eq. 44, as printed) “Equation (44) has new energy density terms: scalar field energy (C²/2μ), SLW energy (CE/μ), and a power source (ρC/εμ).”

In their Appendix A they use Ohm's law, J = σE, “for linearly conductive media”. After Eq. (36) they write: “The resultant electric field is EL = JL/σ … Thus, no eddy currents occur, so the SLW is unimpeded by the skin effect.” They relate σ to the loss part of the permittivity: “σ = εoε″ω”. Their SLW impedance is Eq. (37):

Z = |E_r| / |C/μ_oμ'| = √(μ_o/ε_o) · √(μ'/ε') · [1 − 1/(jkr)] / [1 − j tan(δ_ε)], tan δ_ε = ε″/ε' (Eq. 37)

HYP Erratum, Phys. Essays 35, 320 (2022) (doi:10.4006/0836-1398-35.3.320). It corrects Appendix A and concludes: “Physically, Eq. (A6) means that the SLW is not subject to resistive loss in conductive media. The same no-loss result occurs from the energy balance equation, Eq. (44), by substituting for (ρ/ε) from Eq. (22) and by substituting for JL from Eq. (21) with B = 0; then cancelling terms. Consequently, the SLW is unconstrained by the skin effect and power loss in resistive media.” So Hively's own term, as Hively evaluates it, gives αSLW = 0. The 2019 Appendix A reached the same no-loss conclusion by a different route (Eq. A7). Hively's patent (US 9,306,527) lists “E, C, and J exchange energy in conductive media” (Table 1, EQNS 11–17).

ASSUMPTION (ours) Joule-literal reading. Suppose the Joule heat J·E = σE² is not cancelled but drains the SLW power flow S = |CE/μ| = E²/|Z| (CE/μ sits inside the divergence in Eq. 44, so we treat it as the SLW power flow), using Eq. (37) to relate C to E. Then dS/dr = −σE² = −σ|Z| S, so the field attenuation is:

α_J = σ_H |Z| / 2, σ_H = ε_0 ε″ ω, |Z| = η_0 / (√ε' · |1 − j tan δ|) (far field, kr ≫ 1, μ' = 1)

This is our algebra built from Hively's Eqs. (37) and (44). It is not an equation printed by Hively, and the Erratum says the terms cancel instead. At 1057.8 MHz, 15 °C, salinity 35 (Meissner–Wentz): ε′ = 73.0, ε″ = 77.5, σH = 4.56 S/m, |Z| = 30.2 Ω, so αJ = 69.0 Np/m = 599 dB/m: 1/e length 1.45 cm. For comparison, ordinary radio (TEM) has αTEM = 90.7 Np/m = 788 dB/m: 1/e length 1.10 cm. Over 10 cm, that is 60 dB of SLW loss against 79 dB for TEM.

Seawater, steel hull and the detector's cage

Seawater permittivity (FACT, Meissner & Wentz 2004)

FACT We use the double-Debye seawater model of Meissner & Wentz, IEEE TGRS 42, 1836 (2004). It uses their Eq. 6 with Eqs. 7–8 (pure water, Table III), Eq. 17 (salinity, Table VI) and the Stogryn conductivity regression, Eqs. 11–16. Valid range: −2 to 29 °C for seawater, salinity 0–40, up to at least 90 GHz. The coefficients were typed from the printed tables (REMSS PDF) and checked against the authors' own Fortran (meissner_wentz_dielectric.f90, RSS GitHub). Note that the Fortran has a 2012 update to two coefficients, which we do not use.

ε(T,S,ν) = (ε_S − ε_1)/(1 + iν/ν_1) + (ε_1 − ε_∞)/(1 + iν/ν_2) + ε_∞ − i σ/(2π ε_0 ν) (MW2004 Eq. 6, ν in Hz here) TEM (FACT, plane wave in a lossy medium): k = (ω/c)√(ε' − jε″), α_TEM = −Im k, η = η_0/√(ε' − jε″)

At 1057.8 MHz, 15 °C, S = 35: ε′ = 73.0, ε″ = 77.5, σ = 4.29 S/m, loss tangent 1.06, wavelength in water 2.99 cm, wave impedance |η| = 36.5 Ω. The brief's round numbers (σ ≈ 4 S/m, εr ≈ 70, skin depth about 1 cm) agree.

A prior claim to keep in view: Tomilin, Lukin & Gulkov (2021)

FACT (what the paper reports) Tomilin, Lukin & Gulkov, Tech. Phys. Lett. 47, 577 (2021), in the hub library, report a voice-tone link over 470 m of seawater (σ = 4.77 S/m, 8 °C) at 27.4 MHz with 1.17 W, using insulated spheres driven by Tesla flat coils. They say the transmitter and receiver sat in sealed boxes with batteries and hung on nylon ropes. HYP The authors hypothesise that longitudinal waves carried it. They report no Faraday-cage or TEM control of the kind in our gate G2. ASSUMPTION (ours, S = 35 because the paper gives only σ) Our seawater model gives a TEM 1/e length of 5.1 cm at 27.4 MHz, so 470 m is about 79,523 dB of TEM loss. The Joule-literal SLW reading gives 40 cm, which also rules the claim out. Only the Erratum reading (no loss at all) is consistent with it. This is one unreplicated field report, and G2's controls are designed to test exactly this kind of claim.

Steel hull (Thull)

FACT Ordinary radio does not pass through an intact metal wall. FX-1 estimated that a 1 mm stainless wall already absorbs more than 400 dB at 433 MHz (skin effect; Ott, EMC Engineering, 2009, ch. 6), and a centimetre-thick steel hull absorbs far more. What leaks in comes through seams, hatches and glands. HYP Hively reports the SLW passing thousands of skin depths of copper. Whether it passes a steel hull is untested, so Thull (field fraction) is swept from 10−12 to 1. The front page uses Thull = 1, the most favourable case for the link. ASSUMPTION For TEM we give the hull no credit (SEhull,TEM = 0 dB), because the hull is not the cage, and we include the textbook water-to-air field transmission factor |τ| = |2η0/(η0+ηw)| = 1.84 (FACT, normal incidence).

The detector's own cage (SEcage, Tcage) and the FX-1 openEMS seam results

The 2S chain: pump, wait, tickle, count

1 · Pump: two 243 nm photons (FACT)

FACT 1S → 2S is driven by two photons at 243.13 nm. A 972 nm laser is doubled twice (972 → 486 → 243 nm). Parthey et al., PRL 107, 203001 (2011) obtained “13 mW of the required uv light near 243 nm” this way. The 1S–2S energy is 10.199 eV (from f1S–2S, Parthey 2011), so each 243 nm photon carries 5.099 eV.

Correction (brief 1a): the concept note's “two 972 nm photons” is wrong. A 972.5 nm photon carries 1.275 eV, so two carry only 2.55 eV, far short of the 10.199 eV needed. 972 nm is the laser's fundamental. It must be frequency-quadrupled to 243 nm, and then two 243 nm photons are absorbed.

FACT Lab values for N2S: Landhuis et al. (arXiv:physics/0210019) made “at least 5 × 107 magnetically trapped 2S atoms” below 100 µK, with a detection efficiency of “approximately 2×10−6”. Parthey 2011 used a 5.8 K beam with an atom flux of 1017/s. The number of 2S atoms in view is not quoted there, so it is unknown for this page.

2 · Wait: the 2E1 background (FACT)

FACT A lone 2S atom decays by two-photon (2E1) emission at 8.2283 s−1 (Klarsfeld 1969), so it lives 0.1215 s. The photon spectrum is broad (Spitzer & Greenstein 1951). We use the Nussbaumer & Schmutz (1984) fit A(y) = C[w(1 − (4w)γ) + α wβ(4w)γ], w = y(1 − y), y = ν/νLyα, with C = 202.0 s−1, α = 0.88, β = 1.53, γ = 0.8. Our integral gives ½∫A dy = 8.226 s−1 (published: 8.2249). FACT The narrowest catalogue Lyman-α filter we found is the Teledyne Acton FN122-XN: 122 ± 2.5 nm, 10 nm FWHM maximum, T ≥ 15 % (Pelham 122-NBX is about 10 nm with T ≥ 5 %; 20 nm types are FN122-N and 122-NB). ASSUMPTION The passband shape is not published. We bracket it with a Lorentzian (wide wings, used for the lab case) and a Gaussian (used for the best case), centred at 121.6 nm. We set a blocking floor of 10−4 between 115 and 200 nm (Pelham quotes “visible rejection ~10−4”; the VUV blocking itself is not verified) and 10−4/8500 above 200 nm (Hamamatsu R13194: CsI sensitivity at 121.6 nm is 8500× that at 300 nm). Result, in 2E1 photons that reach the detector per 2S decay (relative to the filter's transmission at Lyman-α): 0.044 (10 nm Lorentzian) or 0.021 (10 nm Gaussian), that is 0.366 or 0.173 photons per atom per second. A 20 nm filter passes 0.110 / 0.064. A hypothetical 1 nm filter would pass 3.9×10⁻⁴. These match our separate desk comparison (snr-2s-vs-sphere, 25 Sep 2026), which used the same shape and floor.

3 · Tickle: the Lamb-shift resonance (FACT)

FACT Lamb & Retherford 1950 (Phys. Rev. 79, 549), Eqs. 25–27. We write them in SI with a peak field E0: rate = γV²/ħ² / [(ω−ω0)² + γ²/4], with V = √3·e·a0·E0/2 (|⟨e·r⟩|² = 3a0², Eq. 27). We also include the counter-rotating term and the 2S→2P3/2 path (ASSUMPTION: same γ). On resonance, 1 µV/m gives 3.1×10⁻¹¹ s−1 per atom, about one tickle per atom per 1,000 years, as the brief says. The line is broad: full width 99.7 MHz (γ/2π, γ = A(2P1/2) = 6.2648×108 s−1, NIST). Check: a 10 V/cm static field (Eq. 42) gives a 2S lifetime of 3.56 µs. Landhuis' formula “γs = 2800E²” gives 3.57 µs.

3b · Hyperfine structure: 1057.8 MHz is a centroid (FACT)

FACT (ours, from the 2S hyperfine interval 177.557 MHz, Bullis et al., PRL 130, 203001 (2023), and the 2P1/2 interval 59.22 MHz, Lundeen, Jessop & Pipkin, PRL 34, 377 (1975)) 1057.8 MHz is the fine-structure centroid. The real 2S1/2→2P1/2 lines are at 909.87 MHz (F=0→F′=1; published 909.894(20) MHz, PRA 98, 012509 (2018)), 1087.43 MHz (F=1→F′=1, weight 2/3) and 1146.65 MHz (F=1→F′=0, weight 1/3). FACT Landhuis's trapped atoms are 2S F = 1. For them, a drive at 1057.8 MHz gives 0.573× the centroid rate (-2.4 dB). Retuning the drive to 1087.43 MHz recovers 0.805×. ADS-B at 1090 MHz sits only 2.6 MHz from the F=1→F′=1 line, so F=1 atoms feel it at 0.81× the centroid peak, against 0.706 if hyperfine is ignored. Every rate on this page uses the F = 1 atoms unless stated. The front page keeps the brief's 1057.8 MHz drive.

Control (d), honestly: for F = 1 atoms, detuning by +300 MHz cuts the rate to 0.0400 of the centroid peak, and −300 MHz to 0.0211 (centroid-only formula: 0.0274, -15.6 dB). At ±500 MHz the rate is 0.0127 / 0.0093. Relative to the 1057.8 MHz drive that is only -11.6 dB / -14.3 dB at ±300 MHz and -16.5 dB / -17.9 dB at ±500 MHz (16–20 dB if hyperfine is ignored), because the line is 100 MHz wide. It proves the atoms are responding. It does not tell SLW from TEM, because leaked TEM follows the same curve. That is control (a)'s job.

3h · Would an SLW tickle at all? (HYP)

HYP The brief assumes that a longitudinal SLW E-field couples to ⟨2S|z|2P⟩ like any E-field. That is untested. η, the coupling relative to an ordinary field of the same size, is swept from 10−12 to 1 in All controls. Hively's papers contain no atomic-transition analysis. Only the patent mentions 1S→2S (Expt D literature check).

7 · Magnetic-tuning option, with its caveats

FACT (our diagonalisation, hyperfine ignored) In a magnetic field of 573.5 G the 2S1/2(mJ = −½) “β” level crosses the 2P1/2 “e” level (Lamb & Retherford 1950 §16 found 540 G using their older ~1000 Mc/s shift; our code reproduces that). The splitting changes by 1.82 MHz/G. Near the crossing a low-frequency or even static field ⊥ B quenches 2S: at the crossing, 1 µV/m gives 6.2×10⁻¹¹ s−1. Caveats: (1) Broadband: at the crossing, anything from DC up to tens of MHz quenches, so ambient HF/VHF noise and ripple count as signal. (2) Motional field v×B: a moving atom sees E = vB. At 573.5 G this is 52.1 mV/m for 100 µK trapped H (1.7×10⁻¹ s⁻¹), 12.5 V/m for a 5.8 K beam (9.7×10³ s⁻¹), and 90.2 V/m at 300 K (5.0×10⁵ s⁻¹). Beams are quenched before they arrive. (3) The field must be held to a fraction of a gauss. Recommendation: use the direct 1057.8 MHz drive at B ≈ 0. There, stray fields of 1 mG–1 G shift the line by at most about 1.4 MHz (μB/h ≈ 1.4 MHz/G), which is small against 100 MHz, and motional quench is negligible. ASSUMPTION The magnetic-option numbers ignore hyperfine structure.

4 · Count: Lyman-α detection, and every background (FACT / ours)

FACT Each tickled atom emits one Lyman-α photon within 1.60 ns. Counts = N2S × εdet × rate. The backgrounds per 2S atom, largest first for the lab case:

  • Stray-field Stark quench (FACT: Landhuis saw 2S lifetimes ≤1 ms in a non-metal cell, and up to 100 ms with copper-film electrodes and the best field compensation). Ours: 1/0.1 s − 8.23 s−1 = ≤1.77 s−1, an upper bound that also includes any other one-body loss. These are real Lyman-α photons, so no filter can remove them. This term dominates the lab case. The best case assumes a residual 10 mV/m (ASSUMPTION).
  • 2E1 continuum through the filter: 0.366 (Lorentzian) or 0.173 (Gaussian) s−1, above.
  • Room-temperature blackbody inside the cage (ours, Planck spectrum × Lamb matrix elements; Gallagher & Cooke, PRL 42, 835 (1979)): 8.1×10⁻⁴ s−1 at 290 K, 99 % of it through the 9.9 GHz 2S→2P3/2 path. It is 1.0×10⁻⁵ s−1 inside a 4 K shield.
  • Radio leaking through the cage (ADS-B, GPS, DME, Wi-Fi) and the transmitter's own ordinary-radio leak: see Link budget.
  • Detector dark counts: see Receiver chain.

ASSUMPTION (as in the desk comparison) A pump holds N2S steady against the no-signal loss rate. When the tickle rate approaches that loss rate (about 10 s−1 in the lab case) the cloud is depleted and the signal saturates. This is modelled. Landhuis made single-shot clouds, so a steady N2S of 5×107 is itself optimistic. FACT One channel electron multiplier counts at most about 15 million counts per second (Sjuts EDR CEM), and the page flags any case above that.

Link budget: field needed, power needed, atoms needed, backgrounds

The field the receiver needs

ASSUMPTION (ours) The transmitter is keyed on/off, half the time each, and counts are Poisson. Then SNR = Rs√(T/2)/√(Rs + 2Rb), where Rs is the signal count rate, Rb the background count rate and T the counting time. We need SNR = 5 in T = 1 s. Longer counting helps only slowly: once background dominates, the needed field scales as T−1/4, so 1 hour lowers it by a factor 0.13.

The practical sensitivity is the “needed field” column above: hundreds of mV/m today and a few mV/m at best.

Atoms against an ordinary antenna. Our desk comparison (snr-2s-vs-sphere, 25 Sep 2026, not yet on the hub) finds that for SNR = 1 in 1 s the atoms need 55–122 dB more field than the ¾″ sphere antenna with a ZX60-P33ULN+ LNA at 1058 MHz. It is 122 dB for lab-demonstrated atoms and 55 dB for an ideal bound in a 4 K shield. The gap widens by 5 dB for every factor of 10 in counting time. So the atoms are not a sensitive field detector. Their only value here is as a test of whether an SLW couples to an atomic transition at all (HYP).

Honest G1 summary. The hard parts are the 2S source and vacuum, not the RF. The brief's own chain (243 nm from a quadrupled 972 nm laser) is a frequency-metrology-lab system. No price for one was found, and none may be quoted without contacting vendors. The Lamb–Retherford electron-bombardment beam avoids the laser and is the historical way this exact 1058 MHz quench was first seen (1947). Either way, G1 is a university-lab-scale project. Its cost is unknown, and the only cited line item on this page is the Lyman-α filter.

  2. G1 · TEM baseline on the bench. Reproduce standard RF quenching of 2S at 1058 MHz with an ordinary antenna. This proves the receiver works before any SLW claim. It needs hydrogen, the UV laser (or the L&R electron-bombardment alternative), vacuum and Lyman-α hardware. Cost and difficulty: Receiver chain. It should also reproduce the Lorentzian (99.7 MHz wide) and the ±300 MHz contrast (-11.6 dB / -14.3 dB for F = 1 atoms), and survey 1058 MHz ± 100 MHz for DME, ADS-B and Wi-Fi products, using an LNA that covers 1058 MHz (ZX60-P33ULN+, not the Kuhne 1296 MHz LNA).
  3. G2 · POOL MOCKUP TEST (required before any comms claim). The detector goes inside its own cage, inside a steel hull mockup, in water of known salinity. The transmitter sits outside, across a measured seawater path. Required controls:
    1. An ordinary TEM transmitter with the same power and frequency must give no tickle signal through the cage and hull. (Expected: 99 µV/m at the atoms for 1 W over 5 cm with an 80 dB cage.)
    2. Cage open versus closed.
    3. Transmitter on versus off, and modulation on versus off.
    4. Detuned drive (for example ±300 MHz) must lose the signal on the Lyman-α Lorentzian (expected contrast for F = 1 atoms -11.6 dB / -14.3 dB).
    5. Salinity and range must be varied. Under the Joule-literal reading the signal falls about 599 dB/m with range in seawater and changes with salinity like TEM. Under the Erratum reading it follows 1/r only.
    Only a signal that survives all of these may be called SLW (HYP) evidence.
