Scalar electric Aharonov–Bohm inside a Faraday sphere

Chiao, Hart, Scheibner, Sharping, Inan, Singleton & Tobar (2023), Phys. Rev. A 107, 042209, plus the 2025 Comment and Reply. Summary, Dan’s reading notes with an evaluation, and an EED (Hively–Loebl) interpretation. Labels: FACT = what the papers say; HYP = our interpretation or derivation, not published and not settled physics.

Educational. The EED section is our own derivation from the Hively–Loebl equations, not a statement by Dr. Hively, Chiao et al., or Gao. Sign conventions still need checking against Hively & Loebl 2019.

Download PDF (arXiv v2) arXiv:2212.03437 PRA 107, 042209 Gao Comment, PRA 111, 066203 Gao preprint Chiao et al. Reply, PRA 111, 066204

Summary of the paper (FACT)

  • Setup. A closed conducting (Faraday) shell has an oscillating voltage V(t) = V0 cos Ωt applied to its surface. The drive is a voltage on the shell itself, not an incident electromagnetic wave. Inside, the electric field is zero and the potential is spatially uniform, apart from very small fields that any time variation produces.
  • Claim. A quantum system inside picks up a time-dependent Aharonov–Bohm phase φ(t) = α sin Ωt with α = eV0/(ħΩ). Expanding with Bessel functions (Jacobi–Anger) splits each level Ei into sidebands Ei ± nħΩ weighted by Jn(α). For large α the strongest sidebands sit near Ei ± eV0.
  • Relation to known physics. The math is the same as the AC Stark / Autler–Townes effect without the polarizability term, because E = 0. The authors argue the sidebands survive a gauge change because the wavefunction phase changes along with the potential.
  • Proposed test. Rubidium vapor inside the cage with Ω = 10⁸ s⁻¹ (about 15.9 MHz) and V0 = 0.5 mV. That gives α ≈ 7.6×10³ and sidebands about eV0/h ≈ 121 GHz away, easily resolved against the Rb D1 line. The readout is electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) in a Λ scheme using 5S1/2, 5P1/2, and the ground state’s upper sideband. Charged quantum dots are offered as an alternative probe.
  • The S→S point. The sideband is an image of the ground state, so it has S character. The electric-dipole selection rule forbids a direct S→S transition. The paper uses that rule to predict a transparency hole from destructive two-path interference in energy space, which it calls the energy-space analog of AB interference in coordinate space.

The 2025 published debate (FACT)

  • Gao, Comment, PRA 111, 066203 (13 Jun 2025). Inside the cage the uniform potential can be removed with λ(t) = (V0/Ω) sin Ωt, which leaves V′ = 0, A′ = 0 and H′ = H0. The gauge-invariant energy −(∂tS + eV) stays Ei, so transition energies do not move and the sidebands are artifacts of the gauge. Gao contrasts this with the magnetic AB ring, where the phase ∮A·dl is topological and cannot be removed. Gao also suggests a “true gauge” picture in which something physical does change inside the cage but cannot be measured.
  • Chiao et al., Reply, PRA 111, 066204 (same issue). The authors say Gao’s gauging procedure is inconsistent and would give wrong answers if applied the same way to the magnetic AB effect. They reaffirm that the energy shifts are physical.
  • Status. As of this page, no experiment has been reported that settles it.

Dan’s reading notes, with evaluation

Dan · forbidden S-to-S

“This paper speaks to the forbidden S to S transitions.”

Correct, with one refinement. The paper invokes the S→S dipole selection rule. It does not claim the transition becomes allowed. The rule forbids jumping from the ground state to its own S-type sideband, and that is what shapes the predicted EIT transparency hole. The forbidden transition is part of the readout mechanism, not the effect being discovered.

Dan · Faraday sphere with sinusoidal modulation

“A time-varying scalar potential … inside a Faraday spherical cage which itself is subject to a sinusoidal exterior electromagnetic modulation, leaving only the scalar field modulation inside.”

Essentially right; one correction. The modulation is a voltage driven onto the shell, not an exterior electromagnetic wave. That matters: a wave would be screened, while a shell voltage moves the potential inside by the same amount everywhere. The spelling is Aharonov–Bohm.

Our added critique (HYP, not in Gao). The paper’s Hamiltonian H0 + eV(t) is written for one charge. For a neutral rubidium atom, the electron and the nucleus-plus-core sit in the same uniform potential with opposite charges, so their coupling terms cancel. Any shift that did exist would also be common to all levels of the same system, so optical transition frequencies would not change. A convincing test probably needs a net-charged probe (quantum dot, trapped ion) or a charge path referenced to something outside the cage.

Dan · longitudinal E inside the cage

“The paper is silent on a longitudinal electric field also possibly being present inside the Faraday cage, as I think Dr. Hively declares physically possible.”

Right: the paper is silent, because it assumes standard electrodynamics. For a perfectly symmetric shell, standard electrodynamics gives exactly zero E inside at any frequency, since a spherically symmetric charge oscillation produces no interior or radiated field. Under Hively’s extended electrodynamics the answer changes (next section).

EED reading of the same cage (HYP)

Using the SI equations from our Hively–Loebl ledger:

E = −∇Φ − ∂t A        B = ∇×A        C = ∇·A + (1/c²) ∂t Φ
∇·E + ∂t C = ρ/ε0     ∇×B − (1/c²) ∂t E − ∇C = μ0 J
Modes: TEM (C = 0) · SLW (B = 0, E ∥ k, C ≠ 0) · SW (E = B = 0, C ≠ 0)
  1. C is not zero inside. With the paper’s interior potentials (A = 0, Φ = V0 cos Ωt), C = (1/c²) ∂tΦ = −(V0 Ω/c²) sin Ωt, uniform in space and oscillating in time.
  2. Gao’s gauge change is not allowed in EED. EED only permits gauge functions with □λ = 0, because other choices change the physical field C. Gao’s λ = (V0/Ω) sin Ωt has □λ = −(V0 Ω/c²) sin Ωt ≠ 0. In EED terms Chiao et al. are right that something physical changes inside. Note that the same objection applies to the gauge argument inside Chiao et al.’s own paper.
  3. E cannot be zero. With no charge inside, the EED Gauss law demands ∇·E = −∂tC ≠ 0, which is equivalent to □Φ = 0. Solving for a sphere of radius R with Φ(R) = V0 cos Ωt and k = Ω/c:
    Φ(r) = V0 cos Ωt · R sin(kr) / (r sin kR)
    E_r(r) = V0 cos Ωt · R [sin(kr) − kr cos(kr)] / (r² sin kR) ≈ (r/3)(Ω²/c²) V0 cos Ωt for kR ≪ 1.
    The field is radial (longitudinal), zero at the center, and has B = 0. That is an SLW, not a pure SW.
  4. A pure SW cannot oscillate in a charge-free, field-free region. If E = B = 0 and ρ = J = 0, the two equations above force ∂tC = 0 and ∇C = 0. Under these equations, a time-varying C inside the cage always brings a longitudinal E with it.
  5. Resonance. The solution blows up at kR = π (shell diameter = one wavelength). That would be a spherically symmetric interior resonance that standard electrodynamics does not have.

Role in pure scalar-wave detection

Concept (FACT). The paper is a proposal for sensing a time-varying scalar potential in a region where E and B are zero. That is the regime scalar-wave detection is aimed at, and it comes from mainstream physicists (Chiao, Tobar) in Phys. Rev. A.

Three competing predictions (HYP framing). Gao and standard textbook gauge reasoning predict no effect inside. Chiao et al. predict energy sidebands. EED predicts a small radial longitudinal E growing toward the wall, and a scalar resonance at kR = π. A driven Faraday sphere with a probe inside could in principle tell these apart.

Candidate future experiment (not a lab protocol yet). Drive an Exp A or Exp B aluminum sphere’s potential directly from the TinySA, place a small radial E-probe inside off center, and read it out without a conducting path to the outside reference (battery-powered recorder or fiber, in the spirit of Tomilin’s sealed box). Controls: probe rotated tangential, probe at the center (EED predicts zero there), and frequency swept toward kR = π. The main false-positive risks are feed-point asymmetry, holes or seams in the shell, and cable pickup, all of which also give small interior fields in standard electrodynamics.
